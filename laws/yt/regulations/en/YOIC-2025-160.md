*Made under the Residential Tenancies Act*

## PART 1

## GENERAL

### Excluded living accommodations

1 The Act does not apply

(a) to any group home, out-of-home care, within

the meaning of the Child and Family Services

Act , or similar living accommodation that the

Government of Canada, the Government of

Yukon, a municipality or a First Nation directly

or indirectly provides for persons with special

needs;

(b) to living accommodation provided on a

temporary basis to a person who is in immediate

need of emergency shelter; and

(c) except to the extent necessary to give effect to

section 13, to any occupancy in a hotel, motel,

lodge, inn or tourist home until the occupancy

has lasted for more than six months.

### Prescribed housing agency

2 The following are prescribed as housing agencies for the

purposes of the Act:

(a) Kwanlin Dün First Nation;

(b) Grey Mountain Housing Society.

### Administrative fees

3 (1) The application fee under subsection 95(1) of the Act

for dispute resolution is $50.

(2) The application fee under subsection 114(3) of the

Act for a review of a decision or order of the director is

$75.

(3) The application fee under subsection 142(4) of the

Act for a review in respect of an administrative penalty

is $75.

## PART 2

## TENANCY AGREEMENTS

### Minimum health, safety and housing standards

4 The minimum health, safety and housing standards set

out in the Schedule apply to all rental units.

### Standard terms

5 (1) The standard terms of a tenancy agreement are

terms consistent with the Act and this Regulation in

relation to the following:

(a) condition inspections;

(b) a landlord’s right to enter a rental unit;

(c) fees that a landlord may or may not impose on

a tenant;

(d) plumbing and water supply;

(e) locks;

(f) repairs;

(g) rent increases, including the timing and the

maximum amount of each increase;

(h) assignment;

(i) subletting;

(j) ending the tenancy.

(2) In addition to the terms referred to in

subsection (1), every tenancy agreement must include a

clear description of any areas of the residential property,

other than the rental unit, that the tenancy agreement

gives the tenant access to.

### Rules respecting fees

6 (1) A landlord may only impose the following fees on a

tenant:

(a) a fee to reimburse the landlord’s direct cost to

provide an additional or replacement lock, key

or other means of access if

(i) due to the tenant’s actions, the

replacement is necessary to restore the

same number of working locks, keys or

other means of access that the landlord

provided to the tenant at the beginning of

the tenancy, or

(ii) it is provided at the tenant ’s request;

(b) a fee to reimburse the landlord in the event that

a cheque of the tenant is returned because of

insufficient funds, which may include

(i) the amount, if any, that the bank or other

financial institution charges the landlord, and

(ii) an additional amount, not exceeding $25, for

the landlord’s administrative costs.

(2) A landlord may only impose a fee under

subsection (1) if the fee is specified in the tenancy

agreement.

### Exception

7 Despite section 6, if a tenant requests the provision of a

service or the use of a facility that the landlord would

otherwise not be required to provide under a tenancy

agreement, a landlord may include terms respecting

that service or facility in the tenancy agreement,

including terms specifying the fee to be paid by the

tenant and the obligations of the landlord in respect of

the service or facility.

### Interest rate on deposits

8 (1) In this section

“ prime rate ”, at any time, means the prime rate

reported by the Bank of Canada

(a) if the time is in the six-month period that begins

on January 1 in a year, that was in effect on

January 1 of that year, and

(b) if the time is in the six-month period that begins

on July 1 in a year, that was in effect on July 1

of that year. « taux de base »

(2) The rate of interest payable on a deposit is the

greater of

(a) the prime rate minus 2%; and

(b) 0%.

### Statements to end tenancy early

9 (1) The following persons are authorized to make a

statement under section 59 of the Act in respect of a

notice under section 57 of the Act:

(a) a medical practitioner, within the meaning of the

Medical Profession Act ;

(b) a nurse practitioner, within the meaning of the

Registered Nurses Profession Act ;

(c) a registered nurse, within the meaning of the

Registered Nurses Profession Act ;

(d) a person responsible for the operation of, or

admissions to, a long-term care facility;

(e) an employee of a First Nation governing body or

Indigenous organization, who provides support

services.

(2) The following persons are authorized to make a

statement under section 59 of the Act in respect of a

notice under section 58 of the Act:

(a) a lawyer who is a member in good standing of

the Law Society of Yukon;

(b) a medical practitioner, within the meaning of the

Medical Profession Act ;

(c) a nurse practitioner, within the meaning of the

Registered Nurses Profession Act ;

(d) a registered nurse, within the meaning of the

Registered Nurses Profession Act ;

(e) a registered psychiatric nurse, within the

meaning of the Registered Psychiatric Nurses

Regulation ;

(f) a psychologist who is a registrant within the

meaning of the Psychologists Regulation ;

(g) an employee of the Government of Yukon or the

Government of Canada, who provides victim

services;

(h) an employee of a First Nation governing body or

Indigenous organization, who provides support

services;

(i) an employee of a government, who provides

counselling or social work services;

(j) an employee of an organization that receives

funding from the Government of Yukon or the

Government of Canada, who provides support

services;

(k) an employee of a school or university, who

provides counselling services;

(l) a member of the Royal Canadian Mounted Police.

(3) For the purposes of making a statement under

section 59 of the Act, a letter of admission from a long-

term care facility, including one sent by email,

constitutes a statement by a person referred to in

paragraph (1)(d).

(4) A person who carries out an assessment for the

purposes of making a statement under section 59 of the

Act must comply with the standards and generally

accepted practices of the person’s profession.

(5) A person who makes a statement under section 59

of the Act must maintain the confidentiality of the

statement, the statement’s contents, the identity of the

tenant and the tenant’s dependant and the

circumstances of the tenant’s dependant , as applicable.

(6) A person who makes a statement under section 59

of the Act must retain any notes or other records relating

to their assessment of the tenant and the tenant ’ s

circumstances and, if applicable, the tenant’s dependant

and their circumstances for a period of at least

12 months.

## PART 3

## MAXIMUM RENT INCREASES

### Maximum annual rent increase

10 (1) In this section

“ Consumer Price Index ” means the all-items Consumer

Price Index for Whitehorse, as published by the Yukon

Bureau of Statistics; « indice des prix à la

consommation »

“ rent adjustment year ” means the period that begins on

May 15 of one year and ends on May 14 of the next year.

« année de révision des loyers »

(2) During any rent adjustment year, a landlord must

not impose a rent increase that is greater than the

average percentage change in the Consumer Price Index

for the two calendar years immediately preceding the

rent adjustment year.

(3) If, before or after this section comes into force, a

landlord has given notice under subsection 40(2) or

90(2) of the Act of a rent increase in an amount greater

than the maximum annual rent increase allowed in

subsection (2), that notice is to be read as a notice

imposing a rent increase of the maximum increase

allowed in subsection (2).

(4) This section does not apply to rent in respect of a

rental unit operated by a housing agency if the rent of

that unit is related to the tenant’s income or the income

of the tenant’s household.

### Exception

11 (1) In this section

“ below market rate unit ” means a rental unit that is

occupied by a tenant under a tenancy agreement and for

which the rent payable is lower than the rent that is

being charged for similar rental units in the same

market; « unité au-dessous du taux du marché »

“ extraordinary expenses ” means unusual or

unexpected expenses incurred by a landlord with respect

to any of the following:

(a) major renovations to a residential property or a

rental unit that are required to meet minimum

health, safety and housing standards;

(b) renovations to a residential property or a rental

unit that improve the security or the energy

efficiency of the residential property or the

rental unit;

(c) renovations to a residential property or a rental

unit that reduce the vulnerability of the

residential property or the rental unit to wildfire.

« dépenses extraordinaires »

(2) Despite section 10, if a landlord incurs extraordinary

expenses in respect of a residential property or a rental

unit or purchases a residential property containing a

below market rate unit, the landlord may, within

12 months after incurring the extraordinary expenses or

purchasing the residential property, apply in writing to

the director for an order authorizing a rent increase

above the maximum annual rent increase allowed under

that section.

(3) An application under subsection (2) must

(a) be in the approved form;

(b) contain documentation respecting the

extraordinary expenses incurred by the landlord,

including receipts, or evidence respecting the

rent that is being charged for similar rental units

in the same market, as the case may be; and

(c) be accompanied by the fee set out in

subsection (4) or (5), as applicable.

(4) The fee for an application is $100 plus an additional

$50 for each rental unit to which the application relates.

(5) Despite subsection (4), if an application relates to

more than 10 rental units, the fee is $600.

(6) The landlord must serve the application on each

affected tenant.

(7) An affected tenant has the right to make

representations to the director with respect to the

application at the time and in the manner specified by

the director.

(8) If the director is of the opinion that the landlord has

incurred extraordinary expenses or purchased a

residential property containing a below market rate unit

and that an exception to the maximum annual rent

increase allowed is appropriate, the director may make

an order authorizing a rent increase that is in excess of

the maximum annual rent increase allowed under

section 10.

(9) The director must not make an order under this

section authorizing a rent increase in excess of an

additional 3% above the maximum annual rent increase

allowed under section 10.

(10) An order made under this section may authorize

rent increases above the maximum annual rent increase

allowed under section 10 for up to three consecutive

years.

### Frequency of rent increases in fixed term tenancies

12 (1) In this section

“ successive tenancies ” means a series of two or more

fixed term tenancies, each of which

(a) is agreed between the same landlord and the

same tenant,

(b) deals with the same or a similar rental unit, and

(c) is preceded or followed, with no intervening

tenancy, by another tenancy in the series.

« locations consécutives »

(2) For the purposes of sections 39, 40, 90 and 91 of the

Act, successive tenancies are deemed to be a single

tenancy.

(3) If subsection (2) applies to a tenancy, and without

limiting the generality of that subsection, the landlord

must not impose a rent increase for at least 12 months

after

(a) if the tenant’s rent has not previously been

increased, the date on which the tenant’s rent

was first established under the tenancy

agreement for the first tenancy in the series of

successive tenancies; or

(b) if the tenant’s rent has previously been

increased, the effective date of the last rent

increase.

## PART 4

## DISPUTE RESOLUTION

### Interrupted tenancy

13 (1) In this section

“ specified period ”, in respect of a tenancy, means

(a) if the tenancy is on a weekly basis, or shorter,

seven days,

(b) if the tenancy is on a basis that is longer than

weekly but not longer than monthly, 14 days,

and

(c) if the tenancy is on a basis that is longer than

monthly, 30 days; « période déterminée »

“ tenancy ” means

(a) a periodic tenancy, or

(b) an occupancy in a tourist establishment;

« location »

“ tourist establishment ” means a hotel, motel, lodge, inn

or tourist home. « établissement touristique »

(2) In this section, a reference to “landlord”, “tenancy

agreement” and “tenant” include s, in respect of a

person’s occupancy in a tourist establishment, the

operator of the tourist establishment, the agreement

that governs the occupancy and the person, respectively.

(3) For the purposes of this section

(a) a landlord interrupts a tenancy if the landlord

terminates the tenancy and, within the specified

period after the tenancy is terminated, the

tenant and the landlord enter into a new tenancy

agreement for the same or a similar rental unit;

and

(b) a landlord is deemed to have terminated a

tenancy if the landlord induces the tenant to

terminate a tenancy by fraud or duress.

(4) If a landlord interrupts a tenancy, the tenant may,

within 14 days after the day on which the new tenancy

begins, apply to the director for a review under this

section.

(5) Part 5 of the Act applies, with any necessary

changes, to a review under this section as though the

application under subsection (4) were an application for

dispute resolution.

(6) If the director, after carrying out a review under this

section, determines that the purpose, or one of the

purposes, for which a landlord interrupted a tenancy

was to avoid the application of any provision of the Act

or the regulations, the director may

(a) order that, for the purposes of the Act,

(i) the interrupted tenancy is to be treated as

though it had not been interrupted but had

instead continued throughout the time

between the tenancies, and

(ii) the new tenancy is to be treated as a further

continuation of the terminated tenancy; or

(b) make, in respect of the interrupted tenancy or

the new tenancy, any decision or order that the

director may make under Part 5 of the Act.

### Rent paid in trust

14 If the director makes an order under subsection 106(1)

of the Act that requires a tenant to pay an amount of

rent to the director in trust

(a) the payment must be made in the manner, if

any, that the director specifies in the order; and

(b) despite any other law to the contrary, no interest

is payable at any time to the landlord or the

tenant in respect of the amount, and any money

earned from the investment of the amount while

it is in trust is the property of the Government

of Yukon.

## SCHEDULE

MINIMUM HEALTH, SAFETY AND HOUSING

STANDARDS

PART 1

GENERAL

1 Purpose

The purpose of this Schedule is to establish minimum

health, safety and housing standards that ensure rental

units and residential properties are safe, sanitary and fit

for human habitation.

2 Principles

(1) The minimum health, safety and housing standards

set out in this Schedule are to be interpreted having

regard to the age, character and location of the rental

unit and residential property, and the services or

facilities that are provided or agreed to be provided

under a tenancy agreement.

(2) A tenant or landlord must not interfere with the

responsibility of the other for complying with the Act or

this Regulation, or for providing a service or facility as

agreed in a tenancy agreement.

(3) The tenant and the landlord of a rental unit each has

a duty to inform the other if there are concerns or issues

regarding the rental unit or another part of the

residential property.

(4) A tenant must not do anything at or in respect of the

rental unit or any other part of the residential property

that would reasonably be expected to create a health,

fire or safety hazard.

3 Interpretation

(1) In this Schedule

“ drinking water ” means water that meets the health

parameters of the Guidelines for Canadian Drinking

Water Quality published by Health Canada, as amended

or replaced from time to time; « eau potable »

“ plumbing ” means all or any part of a drainage system,

a water system or a related venting system, including

pipes, tanks, water heaters, vents, drain fittings and

fixtures. « plomberie »

(2) A reference in this Schedule to a residential property

in respect of a rental unit means, unless the context

indicates otherwise, the rental unit and any other parts

of the residential property to which a tenant has access

under a tenancy agreement.

PART 2

LOT AND EXTERIOR

4 Surface drainage

The landlord must ensure that there is sufficient surface

water drainage and disposal on the residential property

to help prevent erosion, ponding and entry of water into

buildings and other structures located on the residential

property.

5 Accessory buildings and other structures

The landlord must ensure that the accessory buildings

and other structures on the residential property, other

than fences, are in good repair and free from conditions

that would reasonably be expected to create a health,

fire or safety hazard.

6 Walkways, etc.

(1) The landlord must ensure that the walkways, steps,

driveways and parking areas of the residential property

are in good condition and afford safe, unobstructed

passage and a safe surface.

(2) Unless otherwise agreed to by both the landlord and

the tenant and stated in the tenancy agreement, the

walkways, steps, driveways and parking areas of the

residential property must be maintained by

(a) the tenant, for any area of the residential

property that is for the tenant’s exclusive use;

and

(b) the landlord, for all other areas of the residential

property.

7 Yard

The landlord must ensure that the yard of the residential

property is clean and free from rubbish, debris, holes,

excavations and other objects and conditions that would

reasonably be expected to create a health, fire or safety

hazard.

8 Porches, etc.

The landlord must ensure that the porches, balconies,

landings and stairs on the residential property

(a) have handrails as required by the building code

within the meaning of the Building Standards

Act ; and

(b) are free from conditions that would reasonably

be expected to create a health, fire or safety

hazard.

9 Exterior walls

The landlord must ensure that the exterior walls of the

buildings on the residential property have a cladding or

covering that is reasonably free of holes, cracks and

excessively worn surfaces so as to prevent the entrance

of moisture, insects and rodents into the structure and

to provide reasonable durability.

10 Roofs

The landlord must ensure that

(a) each building on the residential property has a

weathertight roof, including eavestroughing and

water piping as appropriate; and

(b) water from the roof of each building on the

residential property is reasonably directed away

from the building.

11 Doors and windows

The landlord must ensure that the exterior doors,

windows and frames on the residential property operate

satisfactorily and are reasonably weathertight and must

repair or replace any damaged or missing parts,

including broken glass and defective hardware.

12 Entrance doors

The landlord must ensure that the entrance doors to the

rental unit are capable of being locked from both inside

and outside.

PART 3

SAFETY AND FIRE PROTECTION

13 Safety alarms and equipment

(1) The landlord must ensure that the residential

property conforms to all applicable requirements under

the National Fire Code of Canada 2020 published by the

National Research Council of Canada and the Fire

Prevention Act .

(2) The landlord and the tenant must comply with the

obligations imposed on them under the Fire Prevention

Act , including those that relate to smoke alarms and

carbon monoxide alarms (approved CO alarms) under

the Fire Safety Regulation, 2015 .

14 Fuel burning appliances

(1) The landlord must ensure that all fuel burning

appliances on the residential property are lawfully

installed, are maintained in good working condition and

are regularly serviced in accordance with the

manufacturer’s instructions.

(2) The landlord must ensure that chimneys, smoke

pipes, connections and their components on the

residential property are maintained in good working

condition, are free from obstructions and are cleaned

annually, or more frequently if necessary.

15 Safe passage out

(1) The landlord must ensure that there is a safe, direct

and unobstructed passage from the interior of the rental

unit to the exterior grade level of the building which does

not pass through a separate rental unit.

(2) The tenant must ensure that the passage from the

interior of the rental unit to the exterior grade level of

the building is reasonably free from obstructions.

(3) The landlord must ensure that every room in the

rental unit that is intended to be a bedroom has a

window that provides a safe passage out as required by

the building code within the meaning of the Building

Standards Act .

PART 4

INTERIOR

16 Basements, etc.

The landlord must ensure that every cellar, basement,

crawl space and foundation on the residential property

is in good repair such that they are reasonably

weathertight and rodent proof.

### Walls, etc.

17 The landlord must ensure that the walls, ceilings and

floors of the residential property are structurally sound

and reasonably free from major cracks, crevices, holes

and defects.

### Floors

18 The landlord must ensure that the floors in the

washrooms, shower rooms, toilet rooms, bathrooms and

laundry rooms of the residential property are reasonably

resistant to moisture.

### Heating

19 (1) The landlord must ensure that the heating system in

the building in which the rental unit is located is

maintained in good working condition.

(2) The tenant must not use, and the landlord must not

require the tenant to use, a cooking appliance or a

portable space heater as the primary source of heat for

all or any part of the rental unit.

(3) The person who controls the temperature of the

rental unit, whether it is the landlord or the tenant, must

not allow the rental unit to be so cold, or cause it to be

so hot, that it is reasonable to expect the temperature

(a) to be a health or safety hazard; or

(b) to cause damage to the rental unit.

(4) Subsection (3) does not require the landlord or the

tenant to do anything to reduce the temperature of the

rental unit other than to refrain from heating it.

### Plumbing and water supply

20 (1) In this section

“ large public drinking water system ” has the same

meaning as in the Drinking Water Regulation . « grand

réseau public d’alimentation en eau potable »

(2) If the rental unit includes a plumbed water supply

system and the water for the system is supplied directly

from a large public drinking water system

(a) the landlord must ensure that the system

provides an adequate supply of drinking water

to the rental unit;

(b) the landlord must ensure that all plumbing in the

rental unit is in good condition, reasonably free

from leaks and obstructions and protected from

freezing; and

(c) the tenant must ensure that all plumbing in the

rental unit is maintained in good working

condition and is reasonably free from

obstructions, and must immediately inform the

landlord if the tenant has reason to believe the

plumbing is not in good condition or is not

reasonably free from leaks and obstructions.

(3) If the rental unit includes a plumbed water supply

system and the water for the system is supplied

otherwise than directly from a large public drinking

water system

(a) the landlord must supply drinking water in the

amount, if any, specified in the tenancy

agreement;

(b) the landlord must ensure that all plumbing in the

rental unit is in good condition, reasonably free

from leaks and obstructions and protected from

freezing; and

(c) the tenant must ensure that all plumbing in the

rental unit is maintained in good working

condition and is reasonably free from

obstructions and, must immediately inform the

landlord if the tenant has reason to believe the

plumbing is not in good condition or is not

reasonably free from leaks and obstructions.

(4) If the rental unit does not include a plumbed water

supply system, the landlord must supply drinking water

in the amount, if any, specified in the tenancy

agreement.

(5) For the purposes of paragraph (2)(a), water that is

supplied to the rental unit directly from a large public

drinking water system is deemed to be drinking water

unless there are reasonable grounds to believe that it is

not.

### Toilets

21 (1) The landlord must ensure that the rental unit has a

toilet facility, whether indoor or outdoor, that meets

reasonable health and safety standards and has a

lockable door to provide privacy.

(2) If a shared toilet facility includes one or more toilet

stalls, the landlord must ensure that each toilet stall has

a lockable door that provides privacy.

### Bathrooms

22 (1) If the rental unit contains a bathroom with one or

more fixtures including sinks, showers, tubs and toilets,

the landlord must ensure that the fixtures are in good

working condition.

(2) The landlord must ensure that each indoor

bathroom has a lockable door that provides privacy.

### Sewage disposal

23 (1) The landlord must ensure that the rental unit

(a) is connected to a public sewage system or to a

maintained and functioning private sewage

disposal system if the rental unit includes a

plumbed water supply system; or

(b) has an outhouse if the rental unit does not

include a plumbed water supply system.

(2) The landlord must ensure that the sewage system

and all related components are maintained in good

working condition, free from leaks, defects and

obstructions, and protected from freezing.

### Ventilation

24 (1) The landlord must ensure that the rental unit has

sufficient ventilation so as to avoid the creation of

dampness, humidity or condensation in the rental unit

that would reasonably be expected to lead to rot, mildew

or other conditions that are a potential health hazard.

(2) The tenant must use the means provided by the

landlord to ensure sufficient ventilation as described in

subsection (1).

### Electrical services

25 If the rental unit is connected to an electrical power

system

(a) the landlord must provide all outlets, switches,

wiring and fixtures in safe working condition;

and

(b) the tenant must not change the system in such

a way as to create a safety or fire hazard or

overload it.

### Appliances

26 If the tenancy agreement requires the landlord to

provide appliances in the rental unit

(a) the landlord must provide properly installed and

vented appliances that meet applicable

consumer safety standards and are in good

working condition; and

(b) the tenant must ensure that the appliances are

maintained in good working condition and must

immediately inform the landlord if the tenant

has reason to believe an appliance is not in good

working condition.

### Pest prevention

27 (1) The landlord must ensure that the residential

property is free of rodents, other vermin and insect

infestations, and must take appropriate measures to

deal with the presence of rodents, vermin or

infestations.

(2) The tenant must ensure that the residential property

is free of attractants that would reasonably be expected

to entice rodents or other vermin to the property or lead

to insect infestations on it.

### Overcrowding

28 The landlord and the tenant must not allow more people

to reside in the rental unit than the lowest maximum

number permitted under the building code within the

meaning of the Building Standards Act , the National Fire

Code of Canada 2020 published by the National Research

Council of Canada and any other applicable health and

safety standard.
