## PART 1

## INTERPRETATION

### Definitions

1 In this Regulation,

“ analyst ” means an environmental protection analyst;

« analyste »

“ contaminated material ” means soil, sediment or water

that contains a concentration of any contaminant that

exceeds a standard set out in Schedule 2 to Schedule 7,

as applicable; « matière contaminée »

“ designated contaminated site ” means an area that an

analyst has designated as a contaminated site under

paragraph 114(1)(a) of the Act; « lieu contaminé

désigné »

“ external technical review ” means an analysis of

technical information conducted by an external

reviewer; « examen technique externe »

“ person ” includes a corporation, a partnership, a trust

and the heirs, executors, administrators or other legal

representatives of a person; « personne »

“ protocol ” means a protocol developed, established, or

made under subsection 59(2) of the Act; « protocole »

“ relocation permit ” means a permit referred to in

section 114.07 of the Act; « permis de déplacement »

“ remediation facility ” means a facility that is designed

and operated for the purpose of the remediation, by

bioremediation, thermal treatment or other means, of

contaminated material; « installation de remise en

état »

“ remediation facility permit ” means a permit referred

to in section 23; « permis d’installation de remise en

état »

“ remediation permit ” means a permit referred to in

section 114.06 of the Act; « permis de remise en état »

“ restricted activity permit ” means a permit referred to

in section 114.05 of the Act; « permis d’activités

restreintes »

“ risk assessment ” means the process of identifying

contaminants and evaluating the existing human health

risk levels, potential future human health risk levels,

existing ecological risk levels and potential future

ecological risk levels from exposure to the contaminants;

« évaluation des risques »

“ risk management ” means actions, including

monitoring, that are designed to prevent or mitigate

impacts on human health or the environment that may

be caused by contamination at a contaminated site;

« gestion du risque »

“ sediment ” means particulate material that lies below

surface water, on either a continuous or intermittent

basis; « sediments »

“ site-specific standard ”, in respect of a contaminant in

an area, means the concentration of the contaminant

calculated for the area, based on the area’s land uses,

water uses or sediment uses and factors unique to that

area; « norme propre au lieu »

“ soil ” includes unconsolidated mineral or organic

material, sand, gravel, rock, fill and sediment deposited

on land; « sol »

“ technical information ” includes data, plans,

assumptions, conclusions and other information

submitted in support of an application;

« renseignements techniques »

“ vapour ” means gaseous emissions from contaminants

in soil, sediment or water; « vapeur »

“ water ” includes surface water, groundwater, snow, ice

and water that results from snow or ice melt. « eau »

### Application of provisions to renewals

2 Sections 12, 16, 19 and 23 apply, with any necessary

modifications, to the renewal of a permit.

## PART 2

## RELEASE OF CONTAMINANTS

### Limits prescribed

3 (1) Subject to subsection (2), for the purposes of

paragraph 112(a) of the Act, the prescribed standard for

a contaminant is the quantity or concentration set out in

the column entitled “Prohibited Release Threshold” in

Schedule 1 for the applicable category of substance.

(2) Subsection (1) does not apply to a release

(a) that is authorized under the Act;

(b) of an ozone depleting substance or other

halocarbon listed in Schedule A to the Ozone

Depleting Substances and Other Halocarbons

Regulation ; or

(c) that is authorized under the Oil and Gas Act .

### Release reporting thresholds

4 For the purposes of subsection 113(1) of the Act, the

reporting and the notification of a release of a

contaminant into the natural environment are not

required if the quantity of the release is below the

quantity set out in the column entitled “Release

Reporting Threshold” in Schedule 1 for the applicable

category of substance.

### Naming of particular responsible parties

5 (1) In deciding which responsible party to name in an

order under section 115 or 115.04 or a direction under

section 113.03, the Minister or an environmental

protection officer may consider any information or

factors that the Minister or officer considers appropriate,

including the following:

(a) the condition of the area of the release or of the a)

contaminated site, as the case may be, when the

particular responsible party

(i) became, was or ceased to be an owner of or

operator at the area, or

(ii) acquired, had or ceased to have possession,

charge or control of a contaminant at the

area;

(b) the activities undertaken by the particular b)

responsible party on or in the land, water or

sediment;

(c) the measures, if any, that the particular c)

responsible party has taken to prevent or

remediate contamination at the area;

(d) in relation to contamination at the area or d)

released from it

(i) the nature and quantity that is attributable to

the particular responsible party, and

(ii) the nature and quantity, if any, that is

attributable to other persons, including other

responsible parties with respect to the area;

(e) any site investigation report, site assessment e)

report or other information that is available with

respect to the release or the area.

(2) If the Minister intends to issue an order under (2)

section 115 or 115.04 of the Act, the Minister must des

fois

(a) provide a copy of the information relevant to the a)

intended order to each person to whom the

Minister intends to issue an order;

(b) allow those persons 30 days to make b)

representations respecting the intended order;

and

(c) before issuing the order, consider the c)

representations, if any, that those persons

make.

## PART 3

## CONTAMINATED SITES

### Local background concentration

6 (1) A proposed local background concentration of a

contaminant in an area must be determined in

accordance with all applicable protocols.

(2) A proposed local background concentration of a

contaminant must be approved by an analyst in order

for it to be considered the local background

concentration of the contaminant for an area for the

purposes of this Part.

### Land, water and sediment use

7 (1) For the purposes of applying applicable standards

set out in Schedules 2 to 7, a proposed land use, water

use or sediment use of an area must be determined in

accordance with all applicable protocols and this

Regulation.

(2) The proposed land use, water use or sediment use

must be approved or determined by an analyst in order

to be considered a land use, water use or sediment use

for the purposes of this Part.

(3) In approving or determining an area’s land use,

water use or sediment use, an analyst may, to the extent

possible given the information available, consider any

information or factors that the analyst considers

appropriate, including the following:

(a) the area’s current and proposed zoning;

(b) a land use plan relevant to the area;

(c) any traditional use of the land or water by a

Yukon First Nation on whose traditional territory

the area is located;

(d) current and probable future activities in the

area;

(e) current and probable future land use, water use

and sediment use of the area;

(f) current and probable future water uses in

adjacent and nearby areas, as determined in

accordance with all applicable protocols;

(g) the potential for groundwater or surface water

from the area to have negative effects on

receptors elsewhere than in the area;

(h) the distance or estimated travel time from a

contaminant plume in groundwater to a

receptor, as determined in accordance with all

applicable protocols.

### Contaminated site standards

8 (1) Subject to subsections (2) and (3), for the purpose

of the definition “contaminated site” in section 1 of the

Act, the applicable standards are prescribed to be the

following:

(a) subject to paragraphs (b) and (c),

(i) in respect of a contaminant in soil, the

concentration of the contaminant

(A) set out in Schedule 2 for the applicable

approved or determined land use and the

applicable site-specific factors, or

(B) set out in Schedules 3 and 4 for the

applicable approved or determined land

use,

(ii) in respect of a contaminant in the surface

water or groundwater, the concentration of

the contaminant set out in Schedule 5 for the

applicable approved or determined water use

and the applicable site-specific factors,

(iii) in respect of a contaminant that is in vapour,

the concentration of the contaminant set out

in Schedule 6 for the applicable approved or

determined land use,

(iv) in respect of a contaminant in sediment, the

concentration of the contaminant set out in

Schedule 7 for the applicable approved or

determined sediment use;

(b) in respect of a contaminant in the soil, water,

vapour or sediment in an area in which the

approved local background concentration is

greater than the applicable concentration set out

in Schedule 2 to Schedule 7, the approved local

background concentration of the contaminant;

(c) in respect of an area for which a site-specific

standard is calculated for a contaminant in

accordance with all applicable protocols and is

approved by an analyst, the site-specific

standard for the contaminant.

(2) If an area has been determined or approved by an

analyst to have a land use referred to in Schedules 2, 3

and 4, other than industrial land use, the prescribed

standard for a contaminant, in respect of soil at a depth

of more than three metres below the surface of the area

is the concentration set out in the particular Schedule for

that contaminant in respect of

(a) the land use, if any, that an analyst specifies for

the purpose of this subsection; or

(b) commercial land use, if no land use is specified

under paragraph (a).

(3) If an analyst determines that an area has more than

one land use, water use or sediment use, the prescribed

standard for each contaminant in the area is the lowest

concentration among those set out in Schedules 2 to 7

for the applicable land uses, water uses or sediment uses

and the applicable site-specific factors.

### Designation or determination

9 (1) Before making a designation or determination in

respect of an area under subsection 114(1) of the Act, an

analyst

(a) must provide written notice of the analyst’s

intention to make a designation or

determination in respect of the area to the

following persons:

(i) in respect of a designation, each responsible

party that is intended to be subject to the

designation and all owners and occupiers of

the property within which the area that is

intended to be designated is located,

(ii) in respect of a determination, all owners of

the property within which the area that is

intended to be subject to the determination is

located;

(b) may provide written notice of the analyst’s

intention to make a designation or

determination in respect of the area to the

following:

(i) the Yukon First Nation on whose traditional

territory the site is located,

(ii) the municipality in which the site is located,

(iii) any local community organization or person

who, in the opinion of the analyst, may have

an interest in the site, if the site is not located

in a municipality,

(iv) any person with an interest in the site that is

registered with the land titles office at the

time the analyst searches the land titles

records, and

(v) any agency or government body that, in the

opinion of the analyst, has an interest in the

site;

(c) must inform each person described in

paragraph (a) and each person or entity

described in paragraph (b) who receives a

written notice that they may make

representations to the analyst in respect of the

intended designation or determination; and

(d) must allow the persons and entities described in

paragraph (c) a reasonable opportunity to make

representations.

(2) In making a designation or determination under

section 114(1) of the Act, an analyst

(a) must consider

(i) all representations made by persons and

entities described in paragraphs (1)(a) and

(b),

(ii) the information in any relevant site

investigation report, site assessment report,

remediation report or risk assessment report,

and

(iii) any other relevant information that relates to

the area; and

(b) may consider the information collected in

relation to a direction issued under

section 113.03 of the Act or an order issued

under section 115, 115.04, 159 or 160 of the Act.

(3) After making a decision under section 114 of the Act,

an analyst must, in a timely manner, provide a copy of

the designation or determination to each person

described in paragraph (1)(a) and each person or entity

described in paragraph (1)(b) who received a written

notice.

### Application for designation or determination

10 (1) An application under section 114.02 of the Act for a

designation or determination in respect of an area of a

property must contain a copy of each relevant site

investigation report, site assessment report,

remediation report and risk assessment report.

(2) The analyst who receives an application from an

owner of land

(a) may require the owner to provide any other

relevant information that the analyst considers

necessary in order to make a designation or

determination;

(b) must, subject to subsections (3) and (4), make a

designation or determination;

(c) must, if making a designation or determination,

do so in accordance with section 9 and with

Part 9 of the Act.

(3) An analyst may reject an owner’s application for a

designation or determination in respect of an area if the

owner fails to provide all of the information that the

analyst considers necessary in order to make the

designation or determination.

(4) An analyst must not make a designation or

determination in respect of an area until the applicant

has complied with subsection 24(2).

### Contaminated sites registry

11 (1) For the purposes of paragraph 114.04(2)(g) of the

Act, the following documents and instruments are

prescribed:

(a) a site investigation report;

(b) a site assessment report;

(c) a designation of an area as a contaminated site,

including the information used in making the

designation;

(d) a determination that an area is not a

contaminated site, including the information

used in making the determination;

(e) a remediation permit;

(f) a restricted activity permit;

(g) a relocation permit;

(h) a transfer of responsibility agreement;

(i) a remediation report;

(j) a risk assessment report;

(k) a remediation facility permit;

(l) any documents or reports submitted in relation

to a remediation permit, relocation permit,

restricted activity permit or remediation facility

permit;

(m) a record of the amendment or cancellation of a

certificate of compliance;

(n) a record of any information relating to the

presence of a contaminant in an area that an

analyst or environmental protection officer

determines should be filed in the contaminated

sites registry.

(2) Subject to any contrary provision of the Access to

Information and Protection of Privacy Act , the Minister

must provide a copy of a document or instrument that

is in the contaminated sites registry to any person who

requests it.

### Restricted activity permit

12 (1) An application for a restricted activity permit in

respect of one or more restricted activities within a

designated contaminated site must contain

(a) a description of each proposed restricted

activity; and

(b) any relevant site investigation report, site

assessment report or risk assessment report.

(2) The Minister must not issue a restricted activity

permit in respect of a designated contaminated site until

the applicant has complied with subsection 24(2).

(3) A restricted activity permit is not required in respect

of a restricted activity that is carried out in accordance

with a remediation permit or an order issued under

section 115.04 of the Act if the permit or order contains

terms and conditions respecting the restricted activity

and states that a separate restricted activity permit is

not required.

### Site investigation

13 (1) A site investigation of an area must include

(a) a review of all available records of current and

past activities, land uses, accidents, releases,

practices and management relating to actual or

potential contamination in the area;

(b) at least one visit to the area;

(c) a visual inspection of the land, surface water,

buildings and equipment in the area for the

presence or indicators of contamination;

(d) an interview with each individual who

(i) is a current or former

(A) owner or occupant of the area or an

adjacent area,

(B) manager or other employee of an owner or

occupier of the area or an adjacent area, or

(C) government employee or officeholder,

(ii) can reasonably be expected to have

information that relates to any contamination

in the area,

(iii) can reasonably be contacted, and

(iv) consents to be interviewed; and

(e) a detailed review of all available information as

to what contaminants may be in the area.

(2) If a person is required to submit a site investigation

report under the Act, this Regulation or a permit, the

report must contain all of the relevant information

acquired by the means described in paragraphs (1)(a) to

(d).

(3) An analyst may require the person who submits a

site investigation report in respect of an area to

(a) provide any additional information that the

analyst considers necessary; and

(b) make any revisions to the report that the analyst

considers necessary.

### Site assessment

14 (1) A site assessment must be conducted in accordance

with all applicable protocols.

(2) If a person is required to submit a site assessment

report under the Act, this Regulation or a permit, the

report must contain the following:

(a) a list of all contaminants that have been

identified as potentially being present in the area

on the basis of a site investigation or other

available information;

(b) information on the physical and chemical form

of each contaminant found in the area;

(c) maps, photographs, cross-sections or other

graphic representations that

(i) provide details of the area’s overall location,

and

(ii) delineate the horizontal and vertical locations

of contamination in the area;

(d) an analysis of soil type, stratigraphy and grain

size;

(e) information as to

(i) the specific areas, depths and degree of

contamination, including any migration to

nearby properties that may have occurred,

(ii) if groundwater is assessed, the groundwater

use, presence, quality, depth and direction of

flow, and

(iii) if surface water is present in or near the area,

the surface water use and proximity to the

area;

(f) a description of the relationship between the site

assessment and any relevant site investigation,

including in particular how the methods of

investigation and findings of the site

investigation were used to design and carry out

the site assessment;

(g) an interpretation and evaluation of the data in a

manner that clearly compares the concentration

of contaminants in soil, water, vapour or

sediment to the applicable concentrations set

out in Schedules 2 to 7;

(h) a compilation and presentation of all relevant

field observations, field measurements and data

and laboratory analytical data;

(i) the information required by all applicable

protocols;

(j) a description of all activities undertaken, and all

methods used, in the course of the site

assessment;

(k) the proposed local background concentration of

a contaminant, if applicable;

(l) the proposed site-specific standards, if

applicable.

(3) An analyst may require the person who submits a

site assessment report in respect of an area to

(a) provide any additional information that the

analyst considers necessary; and

(b) make any revisions to the report that the analyst

considers necessary.

### Risk assessment

15 (1) A risk assessment must be conducted in accordance

with all applicable protocols.

(2) If a person is required to submit a risk assessment

report under the Act, this Regulation or a permit, the

report must contain the following:

(a) identification of the human health risk levels

associated with all contaminants known to be

present in the assessed area;

(b) identification of the ecological risk levels

associated with all contaminants known to be

present in the assessed area;

(c) descriptions of all of the risk management

measures that are proposed to be implemented

in the area;

(d) descriptions of the testing and monitoring that

will be undertaken to evaluate the performance

of the risk management measures after any

proposed remediation strategy has been

implemented;

(e) the proposed local background concentration of

a contaminant, if applicable;

(f) the proposed risk-based standards, if applicable.

(3) A risk assessment report must be approved and

signed by an individual who

(a) works in the field of the risk assessment of

contaminated sites,

(b) holds a bachelor’s degree in science, engineering

or applied technology, and

(c) has at least eight years of experience in

conducting, supervising, or reviewing ecological

and human health risk assessments of

contaminated sites.

(4) An analyst may require a person who submits a risk

assessment report to do one or more of the following:

(a) provide proof acceptable to the analyst that the

individual signing the risk assessment report

meets the requirements in subsection (3);

(b) provide any additional information that the

Minister considers necessary;

(c) make any revisions to the report that the analyst

considers necessary.

### Remediation permit

16 (1) An application for a remediation permit in respect

of an area must contain the following:

(a) a plan for the remediation that includes

(i) a timetable with estimated dates for the

beginning and end of the remediation,

(ii) the details of the proposed sampling and

analyses to be undertaken during and after

the remediation, and

(iii) a description of the measures to be taken for

the prevention and mitigation of further

releases of contaminants during the

remediation;

(b) if a site investigation or site assessment has

been carried out in respect of the area, a copy

of the resulting report and all associated

documents, or, if a report or document has

already been submitted to the Minister or an

analyst, a reference to the report or document,

unless the Minister or an analyst requests

otherwise;

(c) a list of all other regulatory requirements,

instruments and processes, including permits,

authorizations, reviews or assessments, from all

levels of government, that it is reasonable to

expect will be required for the remediation or

may affect it;

(d) detailed information, in the form of maps, cross-

sections or other appropriate representations,

as to the area’s location and the horizontal and

vertical location of the contamination;

(e) any information that the applicant has about the

concentrations of contaminants in the area;

(f) the details of the remediation strategy that is

proposed;

(g) a summary of each other remediation strategy

that has been considered for the management of

the contamination and an explanation of why

that other remediation strategy is not proposed;

(h) if it is proposed that any contaminated material

be relocated as part of the remediation, an

estimate of its volume, the concentration of each

contaminant, if known, and information about

the receiving location;

(i) the details of any public engagement that has i)

occurred, or that is proposed to occur, before or

during the remediation;

(j) in respect of ongoing on-site remediation or risk j)

management measures, the details of any

proposed means of prohibiting public access.

(2) An application for a remediation permit that would (2)

authorize the application of risk-based standards must état

contain, in addition to the items listed in subsection (1), sur

the following: des

(a) a risk assessment report that identifies the a)

proposed risk-based standards, determined in

accordance with all applicable protocols, and

justifies the standards;

(b) a summary of the reasons why risk-based b)

standards are proposed to be applied instead of

the applicable concentrations set out in

Schedules 2 to 7;

(c) a risk assessment report that c)

(i) demonstrates that the levels of risk to human

health of all contaminants known to be

present in the area are not, or will not be,

after risk management measures are taken,

above the applicable acceptable human health

risk levels as determined in accordance with

all applicable protocols, and

(ii) demonstrates that the levels of risk to

ecological receptors of any contaminants

known to be present in the area are not, or

will not be, after risk management measures

are taken, above the applicable acceptable

ecological risk levels as determined in

accordance with all applicable protocols;

(d) a statement by the applicant, made in a form d)

and manner satisfactory to the Minister, in

which the applicant acknowledges and assumes

all of the risks and liabilities that may be

associated with applying risk-based standards

or implementing risk management measures.

(3) An application for a remediation permit that would

authorize the application of site-specific standards must

contain, in addition to the items listed in subsection (1),

the following:

(a) the proposed site-specific standards, as

calculated in accordance with all applicable

protocols;

(b) all site-specific information and calculations

used to calculate the proposed site-specific

standards.

(4) The proposed site-specific standards must be

approved by an analyst.

(5) An application for a remediation permit that would

authorize the application of a local background

concentration standard must contain, in addition to the

items listed in subsection (1), the following:

(a) the proposed local background concentration, as

developed in accordance with all applicable

protocols;

(b) all information and calculations used to develop

the proposed local background concentration.

(6) The proposed local background concentration

standard must be approved by an analyst.

(7) The Minister must not issue a remediation permit in

respect of a contaminated site until the applicant has

complied with subsection 24(2).

(8) The following terms and conditions must be included

in a remediation permit in respect of an area:

(a) a condition that, despite any expiry provision,

the remediation permit remains in effect until an

analyst is satisfied that the area has been

remediated to the applicable standards, unless

the permit is cancelled earlier;

(b) if the remediation permit authorizes the

application of risk-based standards, a condition

that the permit holder must not take or allow to

be taken any action that would invalidate a risk

assessment for the area or potentially increase

the human health risk level or ecological risk

level resulting from contamination in the area;

(c) a condition that the permit holder must notify

an analyst in writing, as soon as practicable, of

any significant change in the use or

management of the area, other than an action

described in paragraph (b), including any

change in the ownership of all or any part of it.

(9) A remediation permit is not required in respect of

(a) any reasonable measures that a person

undertakes, as soon as they become aware of a

release of a contaminant into the natural

environment, to contain or stop the release;

(b) measures that are carried out under

subsection 113.02(2) of the Act; or

(c) remediation that is carried out in accordance

with a remediation facility permit, if the

remediation facility permit contains terms and

conditions respecting the remediation and states

that a separate remediation permit is not

required.

### Remediation standards

17 (1) Subject to subsections (2) and (3), a contaminated

site is satisfactorily remediated if the following

conditions are met, as applicable:

(a) in respect of an area for which the

concentrations set out in Schedules 2 to 7 are

the prescribed contaminated site standards

under paragraph 8(1)(a)

(i) the soil in the area does not contain any

contaminant in a concentration greater than

the concentrations, for the applicable land use

and site-specific factors set out in

Schedules 2, 3 and 4,

(ii) the surface water or groundwater in the area

does not contain any contaminant in a

concentration greater than the concentration,

for the applicable water use and site-specific

factors set out in Schedule 5,

(iii) the vapour in the area does not contain any

contaminant in a concentration greater than

the concentration, for the applicable land use

set out in Schedule 6, and

(iv) the sediment in the area does not contain any

contaminant in a concentration greater than

the concentration, for the applicable sediment

use set out in Schedule 7;

(b) in respect of an area for which a risk-based

remediation standard is approved, the soil,

water, vapour and sediment in the area do not

contain a contaminant in a concentration

greater than the risk-based standard;

(c) in respect of an area for which the approved

local background concentration is the prescribed

contaminated site standard under

paragraph 8(1)(b) or is the remediation

standard authorized in a remediation permit,

the soil, water, vapour and sediment in the area

do not contain a contaminant in a concentration

greater than the approved local background

concentration of the contaminant;

(d) in respect of an area for which an approved site-

specific standard is the prescribed contaminated

site standard under paragraph 8(1)(c) or is the

remediation standard authorized in a

remediation permit, the soil, water, vapour and

sediment in the area do not contain a

contaminant in a concentration greater than the

approved site-specific standard.

(2) If contaminated site standards for an area are

prescribed under subsection 8(2), the area is

satisfactorily remediated if the concentration of each

contaminant in the area does not exceed the

concentration set out in the applicable Schedule for the

applicable land use under subsection 8(2).

(3) If contaminated site standards for an area are

prescribed under subsection 8(3), the area is

satisfactorily remediated if the concentration of each

contaminant in the area does not exceed the

concentration set out in the applicable Schedule for the

applicable land use, water use or sediment use under

subsection 8(3).

### Remediation reports

18 (1) An analyst may direct a person who undertakes

remediation in an area to submit a remediation report.

(2) A remediation report must contain any information

respecting any remedial or monitoring work conducted

in the area that the analyst considers necessary for

evaluating the remediation, including the following:

(a) information that demonstrates compliance with

all of the terms of each permit, order or direction

that has been issued for the remediation, and

with all applicable protocols;

(b) a summary of the remedial activities, including

any relocation of contaminated material from

the area;

(c) the results, location and depth of all

confirmatory sampling and analyses;

(d) the details of

(i) all post-remedial monitoring, including the

results of any analyses, and

(ii) all necessary risk management measures and

all relevant limitations or requirements

associated with risk management measures.

(3) If a permit, order or direction of an analyst under

subsection (1) requires a person to submit a remediation

report, the analyst may require the person to make any

revisions to the report that the analyst considers

necessary.

### Relocation permit

19 (1) An application for a relocation permit must contain

the following:

(a) written consent of the owner of the land from

which the contaminated material is to be

removed;

(b) for land to which contaminated material is to be

relocated other than the site of a remediation

facility, written consent of the owner of the land

to which the contaminated material is to be

relocated;

(c) for land to which contaminated material is to be

relocated that is the site of a remediation

facility, written consent of the operator of the

remediation facility.

(2) A relocation permit is not required in respect of

relocation that is carried out in accordance with a

remediation permit if the remediation permit contains

terms and conditions respecting the relocation and

states that a separate relocation permit is not required.

(3) The Minister must not issue a relocation permit until

the applicant has complied with subsection 24(2).

### Transfer of responsibility agreement

20 (1) A person must not enter into a transfer of

responsibility agreement in respect of an area unless

(a) an analyst has designated the area as a

contaminated site under paragraph 114(1)(a) of

the Act; and

(b) an analyst is satisfied that all contamination at

and originating from the contaminated site has

been identified and delineated in accordance

with all applicable protocols.

(2) Each responsible party who is an owner of the

contaminated site that is the subject of a transfer of

responsibility agreement must sign the declaration

referred to in subsection 116(2) of the Act in respect of

the contaminated site.

(3) A transfer of responsibility agreement in respect of

a contaminated site

(a) must be in writing and must be signed by each

owner of the contaminated site and by each new

responsible party;

(b) must clearly state that each new responsible

party assumes all responsibility for the

contamination identified in the transfer of

responsibility agreement and for any other

contamination that results from that

contamination; and

(c) must attach a copy of the most recent site

assessment report, and of any risk assessment

report in respect of the contaminated site and

documentation respecting the designation of the

site.

(4) A transfer of responsibility agreement has no effect

unless it is approved by an analyst or by an individual to

whom the Minister has assigned this function.

(5) An analyst or a person referred to in subsection (4)

may require an owner of a contaminated site or any

other party to a transfer of responsibility agreement in

respect of a contaminated site to provide any additional

information that the analyst or person considers

necessary for evaluating the transfer of responsibility

agreement.

### Certificate of compliance

21 (1) An application for a certificate of compliance in

respect of an area within a property must contain

(a) subject to subsection (2), the following:

(i) if remediation has not occurred, information

that supports a determination that an area

within the property is not a contaminated

site, including all sampling data, survey

information or maps and data on local

background soil, groundwater and sediment

concentrations, as applicable,

(ii) if remediation has been conducted to a

standard other than a risk-based remediation

standard, a remediation report, or

(iii) if remediation has been conducted to a risk-

based remediation standard, a risk

assessment report;

(b) subject to subsection (2), monitoring data or

other reporting as required in a permit, order or

direction in respect of the area within the

property; and

(c) a declaration, made in a manner satisfactory to

the Minister, that affirms

(i) that all necessary risk management measures

are in place and are adequate to ensure that

any concentrations of contaminants are at or

below the acceptable human health risk level

and the acceptable ecological risk level, each

as determined in accordance with all

applicable protocols, and

(ii) that all active remediation in the area is

complete.

(2) If a document or information described in

paragraph (1)(a) or (b) has already been submitted to

the Minister or an analyst, an application for a certificate

of compliance may refer to the document or information

instead of including it, unless the Minister or an analyst

requests otherwise.

(3) The Minister may require an applicant for a

certificate of compliance to provide any additional

information that the Minister considers necessary for the

consideration of the application.

(4) The Minister must not issue a certificate of

compliance until the applicant has complied with

subsection 24(2).

(5) A certificate of compliance

(a) must not have an expiry date; and

(b) must clearly state that it is valid only in respect

of

(i) the land uses, water uses or sediment uses

approved or determined by the analyst that

are applicable at the time of the application,

(ii) the site conditions that exist at the time of the

application, and

(iii) the relevant remediation standards that apply

at the time of the application.

### Amendment or cancellation of certificate

22 (1) In providing notice under subsection 117.02(2) or

section 117.04 of the Act of an intention to amend or

cancel a certificate of compliance in respect of a

property, the Minister must include in the notice

(a) the reasons for the intended amendment or

intended cancellation; and

(b) a statement that the owner of the property may

make written representations to the Minister

and may include as part of the representations

any supporting data or other information.

(2) The Minister may, in response to any

representations made respecting the intended

amendment or cancellation of a certificate of

compliance, modify or withdraw the intended

amendment or cancellation.

(3) The Minister need not provide notice of an intention

to modify or withdraw an intended amendment or

cancellation of a certificate of compliance.

### Remediation facility permit

23 (1) The construction, operation and decommissioning of

a remediation facility are designated as activities that

require a permit.

(2) An application for a remediation facility permit must

contain the following information in respect of the area

of the proposed facility:

(a) distance from the area to the nearest residential

property;

(b) distance from the area to the nearest surface

water body;

(c) distance from the area to the nearest existing or

potential drinking water source;

(d) depth to groundwater in the area.

(3) The Minister must not issue a remediation facility

permit until the applicant has complied with

subsection 24(2).

(4) A remediation facility permit must contain a

condition that the permit holder notify an analyst in

writing, as soon as practicable, of any significant change

in the use or management of the facility property,

including any change in the ownership of all or any part

of it.

(5) A remediation facility permit is not required in

respect of remediation that is carried out in accordance

with a remediation permit, if the remediation permit

contains terms and conditions respecting the

remediation facility and states that a separate

remediation facility permit is not required.

## PART 4

## ADMINISTRATION

### Statement of compliance

24 (1) This section applies in respect of

(a) any application for

(i) a designation or determination,

(ii) a restricted activity permit,

(iii) a relocation permit,

(iv) a remediation permit,

(v) a remediation facility permit, or

(vi) a certificate of compliance; and

(b) any submission, to the Minister or an analyst,

that is required under the Act in respect of

(i) a site investigation, or

(ii) a site assessment.

(2) If a person makes an application or submission

described in subsection (1), the person must include

with the application or submission a statement, signed

by the person or their agent, that affirms that the

documentation prepared in support of the application or

submission has been prepared in accordance with all of

the requirements of the Act, the regulations under the

Act and all applicable protocols.

### External technical reviews

25 (1) On receiving an application described in

paragraph 24(1)(a), the Minister or the analyst who

receives the application may decide that an external

technical review is required.

(2) If the Minister or analyst decides that an external

technical review is required, the Minister or analyst must

(a) as soon as practicable after making the decision,

notify the applicant in writing

(i) that an external technical review is required

to be carried out before the application is

further considered,

(ii) that the applicant is required to pay the

estimated cost of the external technical

review to the Minister,

(iii) of the estimated cost of the external technical

review, and

(iv) of the timeline for the completion of the

external technical review; and

(b) suspend the consideration of the application

until the applicant has paid to the Minister the

estimated cost of the external technical review.

(3) If the Minister or analyst notifies the applicant that

they must pay for an external technical review, and the

applicant does not pay the estimated cost of the external

technical review within 60 days after the notice is sent,

the applicant is deemed to have abandoned the

application, and the Minister or analyst must not

consider it further.

(4) If the applicant has paid an amount to the Minister

on account of the cost of an external technical review,

the Minister may refund the amount to the applicant only

if, before the Minister enters into a contract for services

with an external technical reviewer in respect of the

external technical review, the applicant notifies the

Minister in writing that they wish to withdraw their

application from consideration.

(5) If an external technical review is carried out in

respect of an application to the Minister or an analyst,

the Minister or analyst may rely on all or any part of the

information derived from the external technical review

for the purpose of

(a) making decisions, including whether or not to

(i) issue a permit or amend, suspend or cancel a

permit that had been issued,

(ii) make a designation or determination in

respect of an area, or

(iii) issue, amend or cancel a certificate of

compliance; and

(b) setting the terms and conditions, in the case of

a permit or certificate of compliance.

### Special waste permits

26 A relocation permit or remediation facility permit that

authorizes the relocation or remediation of

contaminants that are special wastes under the Special

Waste Regulations is considered to be a special waste

permit for the purposes of the Special Waste

Regulations .

### Review of standards

27 The Minister must, at five-year intervals or any other

interval the Minister considers advisable, review the

standards under this Regulation.

## PART 5

## TRANSITIONAL PROVISIONS AND

## CONSEQUENTIAL AMENDMENTS

### Definition

28 In this Part

“ former Regulation” means the Contaminated Sites

Regulation . « ancien règlement »

### Transition — registry of contaminated sites

29 Each document that was, immediately before the coming

into force of this Regulation, included in the public

registry of contaminated sites, is prescribed for the

purposes of paragraph 114.04(2)(g) of the Act.

### Transition — special waste permit

30 A permit under the former Regulation that authorizes

the relocation or remediation of contaminants that are

special wastes under the Special Waste Regulations and

that is in effect immediately before the coming into force

of this Regulation continues to be considered a special

waste permit for the purposes of the Special Waste

Regulations until it expires or is cancelled.

### Transition — authorization respecting activities under subsection 114(8) of the Act

31 An authorization, given under subsection 114(8) of the

Act as it read immediately before the coming into force

of section 17 of the Act to Amend the Environment Act ,

that is in force immediately before the coming into force

of this Regulation and that would continue in force

according to its terms after the coming into force of this

Regulation, is, on the coming into force of this

Regulation, continued as a restricted activity permit and

remains subject to the same terms and conditions.

### Transition — permit respecting risk-based restoration

32 A permit under the former Regulation that authorizes

restoration using risk-based restoration standards, that

is in force immediately before the coming into force of

this Regulation and that would continue in force

according to its terms after the coming into force of this

Regulation, is, on the coming into force of this

Regulation, continued as a remediation permit and

remains subject to the same terms and conditions.

### Transition — permit respecting relocation of contaminants

33 A permit under the former Regulation that authorizes

the relocation of contaminants from a contaminated site,

that is in force immediately before the coming into force

of this Regulation and that would continue in force

according to its terms after the coming into force of this

Regulation, is, on the coming into force of this

Regulation, continued as a relocation permit and

remains subject to the same terms and conditions.

### Transition — land treatment facility permit

34 A permit under the former Regulation that authorizes

the construction and operation of a land treatment

facility, that is in force immediately before the coming

into force of this Regulation and that would continue in

force according to its terms after the coming into force

of this Regulation is, on the coming into force of this

Regulation, continued as a remediation facility permit

and remains subject to the same terms and conditions.
