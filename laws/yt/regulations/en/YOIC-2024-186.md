## PART 1

## INTERPRETATION

### Definitions

1 In this Regulation

“ access authorization ” means an access authorization

issued under Part 4; « autorisation d’accès »

“ access permit ” means an access permit issued under

Part 4; « permis d’accès »

“ approved land use plan ” means a land use plan

approved by the Government of Yukon that is one of the

following:

(a) a land use plan developed under Chapter 11 of a

final agreement,

(b) a management plan developed under Chapter 10

of a final agreement for a special management

area,

(c) a plan for a habitat protection area designated

under the Wildlife Act ,

(d) a local area plan developed under the Area

Development Act ,

(e) an approved park management plan within the

meaning of the Parks and Land Certainty Act ,

(f) interim guidelines for the management of a park

established by the Commissioner in Executive

Council under subsection 20(2) of the Parks and

Land Certainty Act ,

(g) any other plan respecting land use

(i) developed under an Act, or

(ii) agreed to in writing by the parties who

developed and signed it to be an approved

land use plan for the purpose of this

Regulation; « plan d’aménagement

approuvé »

“ close ”, in relation to a resource road, means to prevent

or prohibit use or access to all or to part of the resource

road; « fermer »

“ decommissioning and reclamation plan ” means a

decommissioning and reclamation plan under

section 12; « plan de mise hors service et de remise en

état »

“ director ” means the director of land management

branch in the Department of Energy, Mines and

Resources of the Government of Yukon, or if there is no

such individual, the member of the public service who is

responsible for the administration and supervision of the

land management branch or other operational

equivalent of the branch; « directeur »

“ enforcement officer ” means a person, or class of

persons, appointed or designated by the Minister under

section 5.1 of the Act to enforce the provisions of this

Regulation; « responsable de l’application »

“ final agreement ” means an Agreement or a

Subsequent Agreement within the meaning of an Act

Approving Yukon Land Claim Final Agreements ;

« entente définitive »

“ hazardous substance ” has the same meaning as in the

Environment Act; « substance dangereuse »

“ heritage resource assessment ” means an assessment

of an area to determine the potential for the existence

of heritage resources in the area; « évaluation des

ressources patrimoniales »

“ heritage resources ” has the same meaning as in a final

agreement; « ressources patrimoniales »

“ in writing ” , in relation to a communication, includes a

communication sent by email; « par écrit »

“ letter of clearance ” means a letter of clearance issued

by the director under section 42; « lettre

d’acquittement »

“ materials ” means limestone, granite, slate, marble,

gypsum, marl, gravel, loam, sand, clay, volcanic ash or

stone found in a natural state on territorial lands or

Yukon lands; « matériaux »

“ operational plan ” means an operational plan under

section 9; « plan opérationnel »

“ pit ” means a site where materials, not including

consolidated rock, are being taken or have been taken

from territorial lands or Yukon lands; « arénière »

“ primary permit ” means a primary permit issued under

Part 3; « p ermis d’exploitation primaire »

“ quarry ” means a place where consolidated rock is

being taken or has been taken from territorial lands or

Yukon lands; « carrière »

“ release ” has the same meaning as in the Environment

Act ; « rejet »

“ renewable energy generation project ” means an

electricity generation project with a maximum rated

output of electricity for the project of 50 kW or more

generated exclusively from one or more of the following

sources:

(a) biomass,

(b) heat from the earth,

(c) moving water,

(d) sunlight,

(e) wind; « projet de production d’énergie

renouvelable »

“ resource use authorization ” means a resource use

authorization listed in subsection 7(2); « autorisation

d’utilisation des ressources »

“ road construction plan ” means a road construction

plan under section 10; « plan de construction routière »

“ site assessment ” means a site assessment under

section 11; « évaluation de site »

“ winter road ” means a resource road that

(a) is constructed primarily of ice and snow on

frozen ground,

(b) is reasonably only able to be used for travel by a

motor vehicle when the ground is frozen, and

(c) is intended to be used only when the ground is

frozen and the surface of the lands on which the

road is constructed is covered by ice and snow;

« chemin d’hiver »

“ Yukon lands ” has the same meaning as in the Lands

Act. « terres du Yukon »

### Prescribed resources

2 For the purpose of the definition “resource road” in

section 1 of the Act, the following are prescribed as a

resource:

(a) coal;

(b) a renewable energy generation project located

on territorial lands or Yukon lands.

### Application

3 This Regulation does not apply to a road constructed

under the authority of the Quartz Mining Act or the

Placer Mining Act.

## PART 2

## RESTRICTIONS ON CONSTRUCTION,

## MODIFICATION, USE, MAINTENANCE, CLOSURE,

## DECOMMISSIONING AND RECLAMATION

### Prohibition

4 A person must not enter on, construct, modify, use,

maintain, close or decommission a resource road or

reclaim lands affected by the construction, modification

or use of a resource road except in accordance with the

terms and conditions of a primary permit, an access

permit, an access authorization or this Regulation.

### Government and emergency use

5 (1) In this section

“ municipality ” has the same meaning as in the

Municipal Act. « municipalité »

(2) The following persons may use a resource road:

(a) an employee of the Government of Yukon, a First

Nation, a municipality, or the Government of

Canada, as is reasonably necessary in carrying

out their employment obligations;

(b) an enforcement officer, as is reasonably

necessary to enforce this Regulation;

(c) a member of the Royal Canadian Mounted Police,

as is reasonably necessary in carrying out their

employment obligations;

(d) a person who is appointed under section 59 of

the Workers’ Safety and Compensation Act , as is

reasonably necessary to administer and enforce

Part 3 of the Workers’ Safety and Compensation

Act ;

(e) a person, if they do so for the purpose of

assisting a person referred to in paragraph (a),

(b), (c) or (d) in carrying out their employment

obligations;

(f) a person, as is reasonably necessary in an

emergency that threatens life, property, or the

natural environment, to respond to the

emergency.

## PART 3

## PRIMARY PERMITS

### Primary permit

6 (1) A primary permit authorizes the holder to construct,

modify, and use a resource road, in accordance with the

terms and conditions of the permit.

(2) A primary permit requires the holder to

(a) maintain the resource road;

(b) take specified measures to prevent unauthorized

users from entering onto the resource road;

(c) close and decommission the resource road when

the resource road is no longer required; and

(d) reclaim the lands affected by the construction,

modification or use of the resource road.

(3) A resource road may be divided into parts, each with

its own primary permit holder.

### Eligibility for primary permit

7 (1) A person may apply to the director for a primary

permit if the person

(a) holds a resource use authorization listed in

subsection (2); or

(b) has applied for a resource use authorization

listed in subsection (2) in accordance with the

statutory requirements for that application.

(2) The resource use authorizations referred to in

paragraphs (1)(a) and (b) are:

(a) an approved operating plan within the meaning

of Part 2 of the Quartz Mining Act ;

(b) a licence within the meaning of Part 2 of the

Quartz Mining Act ;

(c) an approved operating plan within the meaning

of Part 2 of the Placer Mining Act ;

(d) an oil and gas disposition within the meaning of

the Oil and Gas Act ;

(e) a lease of land under the Quarry Regulations

providing for the exclusive use of the land for the

purpose of establishing a pit or quarry;

(f) a licence within the meaning of the Coal

Regulation ;

(g) a lease of a location for coal mining purposes

issued under the Coal Regulation ;

(h) a lease issued under the Dredging Regulation

granting the exclusive right to dredge for

minerals in the submerged bed of a river;

(i) a lease of Yukon lands under the Lands Act for

the purpose of generating electricity from a

renewable energy generation project;

(j) a lease of territorial lands under the Territorial

Lands (Yukon) Act for the purpose of generating

electricity from a renewable energy generation

project;

(k) a permit within the meaning of the Land Use

Regulatio n authorizing activities undertaken for

the purpose of generating electricity from a

renewable energy generation project.

(3) If the resource road is to be a road to a renewable

energy generation project intended to generate

electricity from moving water, in addition to holding or

having applied for a lease or a permit under the Land

Use Regulation , the applicant must

(a) hold a licence issued under the Waters Act for

the purpose of a power undertaking within the

meaning of the Waters Regulation ; or

(b) have applied under section 14 of the Waters Act

for a licence for the purpose of a power

undertaking within the meaning of the Waters

Regulation.

### Permit application process

8 (1) An applicant for a primary permit must submit their

application in the form approved by the director and

must include with the application the following:

(a) information to establish that the applicant is

eligible to apply for a primary permit under

section 7;

(b) information to establish that there is no

practicable alternative to constructing a road or

designating an existing road to be a resource

road in order to access the resource in respect

of which they hold or have applied for a resource

use authorization;

(c) a detailed map of the route the applicant is

proposing to use to access the resource that

(i) shows the location of each resource road,

highway or other road that the applicant

intends to use or to construct, and

(ii) identifies all roads and highways in the area

whether the applicant intends to use them or

not;

(d) if there is a highway or road in the area that the

applicant does not intend to use to access the

resource, an explanation of why the applicant

does not intend to use the highway or road;

(e) an operational plan that meets the requirements

of section 9;

(f) if the application includes a request to construct

a road or to modify an existing road, a road

construction plan that meets the requirements

of section 10;

(g) a site assessment of the proposed route of the

road that meets the requirements of section 11;

(h) a plan for the decommissioning of the resource

road and for the reclamation of the lands on

which the resource road is located that meets

the requirements of section 12;

(i) any other information requested by the director;

(j) the application fee determined by the director

and published on a Government of Yukon

website.

(2) The applicant must acknowledge in the application

that they understand that

(a) their use of the resource road will be shared with

other users that hold an access permit or access

authorization to use the resource road; and

(b) the multi-use of the resource road will be a term

of the permit.

(3) A person may apply for a permit respecting an

existing road that is not a resource road if they propose

in the application that the Minister designate the road to

be a resource road.

### Operational Plan

9 (1) An operational plan is a plan setting out the terms

on which the use of the resource road is to be managed.

(2) An operational plan must be in the form approved

by the director and must include the following:

(a) a plan to prevent unauthorized persons from

entering onto the resource road;

(b) a plan for using the resource road, including in

respect of road user safety considering multi-

users;

(c) a plan for maintaining the resource road,

including in respect of equipment, methods and

materials and any proposed pits or quarries;

(d) a plan for managing any heritage resources

found while undertaking activities under the

permit;

(e) a plan for controlling invasive species;

(f) a wildlife management plan that identifies

potential adverse effects to fish and wildlife and

sets out measures to eliminate, reduce or

control the effects;

(g) a plan for the handling of hazardous substances;

(h) a plan for the management of releases of fuels

and petroleum products;

(i) an emergency preparedness plan including a

plan for fires and a plan for medical

emergencies.

(3) The director may establish standard terms that

must be included in an operational plan.

### Road construction plan

10 (1) A road construction plan must include the following:

(a) a detailed map of the proposed route of the a)

resource road and the existing roads in the area;

(b) the nature of the resource road proposed to be b)

constructed, including the number of lanes and

whether it will be a seasonal road;

(c) if the applicant holds a resource use c)

authorization, information establishing that the

standards to which the resource road will be

constructed are consistent with the scope of the

authorization to construct a road or use a vehicle

that is set out in the resource use authorization;

(d) if the applicant has applied for a resource use d)

authorization, information establishing that the

standards to which the resource road will be

constructed are consistent with the scope of the

authorization to construct a road or use a vehicle

that the applicant expects to be set out in the

resource use authorization in respect of which

they have applied;

(e) the proposed construction techniques and the e)

equipment proposed to be used, including the

method proposed for the construction of ditches,

culverts, bridges, fords and other water course

crossings;

(f) the proposed erosion control methods; f)

(g) a plan for controlling invasive species; g)

(h) a wildlife management plan that identifies h)

potential adverse effects to fish and wildlife and

sets out measures to eliminate, reduce or

control the effects;

(i) each source of materials proposed to be used in i)

the construction, modification or maintenance of

the resource road and a development plan for

each pit or quarry to be developed for that

source;

(j) a plan for the handling of hazardous substances; j)

(k) a plan for the management of releases of fuels k)

and petroleum products;

(l) an emergency preparedness plan including a

plan for fires and a plan for medical

emergencies;

(m) the following information about any proposed

camps and staging and laydown areas:

(i) a map showing their location and size,

(ii) a camp management plan for each camp that

includes the following:

(A) a water management plan for all waste

produced at the camp including sewage

and grey water,

(B) the identification of potential adverse

effects to fish and wildlife and the

environment including potential effects on

water and a plan for how these effects will

be eliminated, reduced or controlled.

(2) A road construction plan must be prepared or

reviewed by a person who has the qualifications that the

director requires for that purpose.

### Site assessment

11 (1) A site assessment must include the following:

(a) a description of the existing biological and

physical characteristics of the general area and

of the lands proposed to be used for the resource

road and for the construction of the resource

road, including any area used for camps or

staging;

(b) the identification of areas of potential land

disturbances such as effects on permafrost,

erosion, slumping and other environmental

concerns that could be caused by the

construction or use of the resource road;

(c) the manner in which land disturbances would be

eliminated, reduced or controlled;

(d) any requirements for a site assessment

determined by the director and published on a

Government of Yukon website;

(e) any other information that the director

determines is necessary to be included in the

site assessment under the circumstances.

(2) A site assessment must be prepared or reviewed by

a person who has the qualifications that the director

requires for that purpose.

### Decommissioning and reclamation plan

12 A decommissioning and reclamation plan must include

the following:

(a) the method by which the applicant intends to

decommission the road;

(b) the method by which the applicant intends to

reclaim the lands.

### Heritage resource assessment

13 If it is likely that heritage resources are located in the

area to be used during the construction of a resource

road or during its use, the director must undertake or

request that the applicant undertake a heritage resource

assessment.

### Calculation and form of security

14 (1) After the director determines that an application is

complete

(a) the Minister must determine whether to require

security; and

(b) if security is required, the Minister must

(i) calculate, in accordance with subsection (2),

the amount of security that the applicant

must give under section 4.5 of the Act, and

(ii) inform the applicant of the amount.

(2) The calculation of the amount of security to be given

must be done in accordance with the method published

by the Minister on a Government of Yukon website.

(3) The Minister may require either

(a) that a payment in the total amount calculated

under subsection (2) be made before the

commencement of any activities authorized by

the permit; or

(b) that an initial payment in an amount determined

by the Minister be made before the

commencement of any activities authorized by

the permit and, in addition, subsequent annual

payments of security be made after the permit

is issued such that the total amount of security

is paid over a specified period of time.

(4) A security payment, whether a payment in the total

amount or an initial or subsequent payment, must be

made in cash or in another form determined by the

Minister to be sufficiently liquid, accessible and secure.

### Designation of resource road

15 (1) The Minister may designate all or part of an existing

road to be a resource road if an application has been

submitted under section 8 for a primary permit

respecting the road.

(2) The Minister must, prior to designating all or part of

the road as a resource road, ensure

(a) that there has been a period for public review

that provides the public with an opportunity for

input on the proposed designation, taking into

account any public engagement that was

undertaken during the development and

approval of an approved land use plan; and

(b) that there has been consultation, taking into

account consultation that was undertaken

during the development and approval of an

approved land use plan, on the proposed

designation with

(i) each First Nation whose existing or asserted

Aboriginal or treaty rights could potentially be

adversely impacted by the designation of the

road and its subsequent use as a resource

road, and

(ii) the Inuvialuit, if their treaty rights could

potentially be adversely impacted by the

designation of the road and its subsequent

use as a resource road.

(3) The Minister must, as part of the public review and

the consultation under subsection (2), provide the public

and each First Nation referred to in

subparagraph (2)(b)(i), and if applicable under

subparagraph (2)(b)(ii), the Inuvialuit, with a copy of the

application submitted under section 8.

### Request for information

16 (1) If, after reviewing the application, the director

determines that the application is not complete or that

they require additional information before issuing a

primary permit, the director must provide the applicant

with notice in writing requesting the information they

require.

(2) The applicant must, within 12 months after the

request is made

(a) provide the required information; or

(b) notify the director, in writing, of the applicant’s

refusal to provide the information.

(3) If the director determines that additional

information respecting the site is required, the director

may undertake an assessment that considers one or

more of the matters described in paragraphs 11(1)(a)

to (e).

### Withdrawal of application

17 An applicant is considered to have withdrawn the

application if subsection 16(2) is not complied with.

### Consultation on primary permit

18 (1) The director must, prior to making a decision under

section 20, ensure that there has been consultation with

First Nations, and if applicable the Inuvialuit, respecting

potential adverse impacts on existing or asserted

Aboriginal or treaty rights that might occur as a result

of the construction, modification, designation, use or

maintenance of the resource road or by the

decommissioning of the resource road and the

reclamation of the lands affected by the construction,

modification or use of the resource road.

(2) Consultation under this section must consider the

potential multi-use of the resource road and the

likelihood of applications for access permits and access

authorizations and the effects the multi-use may have

on the health and safety of persons, including road

users, and on the environment, and the potential

adverse impacts on existing and asserted Aboriginal and

treaty rights.

### Public engagement on primary permit

19 (1) The director must, prior to making a decision under

section 20, ensure that there has been public

engagement respecting the proposed resource road and

the plans referred to in paragraphs 8(1)(e), (f) and (h).

(2) Public engagement under this section must consider

the potential multi-use of the resource road and the

likelihood of applications for access permits and access

authorizations and the effects the multi-use may have

on the health and safety of persons, including road

users, and on the environment.

(3) The requirement for public engagement under this

section is fulfilled by the opportunity for the public to

provide information and views on the project to a

designated office under section 55 of the Yukon

Environmental and Socio-economic Assessment Act

(Canada) or to the executive committee under section 57

of the Yukon Environmental and Socio-economic

Assessment Act (Canada).

(4) Despite subsection (3), the director may arrange for

additional public engagement if the director believes

that it would assist in the decision under section 19 as

to whether to issue the primary permit.

### Decision regarding primary permit

20 (1) When the director determines that an application for

a primary permit is complete, and when any information

requested under section 16 has been provided and the

consultation under section 18 and public engagement

under section 19 have been carried out, the director

must make one of the following decisions:

(a) to issue the permit in accordance with this

section if satisfied that the requirements of the

Act and this Regulation, including the criteria set

out in subsection (3), have been met;

(b) to refuse to issue the permit.

(2) The director must consider the following

information in deciding whether to issue a primary

permit and in setting the terms and conditions of the

permit:

(a) information in the application;

(b) information in a heritage resource assessment if

one was completed;

(c) information in a site assessment;

(d) each decision document, if any, that has been

issued under the Yukon Environmental and

Socio-Economic Assessment Act (Canada) in

respect of the resource road or the project of

which the resource road is a component;

(e) an approved land use plan if there is one for any

area within which the resource road is proposed

to be constructed or used;

(f) the results of a consultation with First Nations,

and if applicable the Inuvialuit, undertaken

under section 18;

(g) comments from the public engagement

undertaken under section 19;

(h) the applicant’s past compliance with permits or

authorizations issued by the Government of

Yukon, including

(i) whether the applicant has previously been

non-compliant with the terms of a resource

use authorization or of a primary permit,

access permit or access authorization,

(ii) whether there is an outstanding issue

respecting remediation or reclamation

requirements on another project for which a

resource use authorization or a primary

permit has been issued to the applicant, or

(iii) whether the applicant has been found guilty

of an offence

(A) respecting an activity undertaken without

a resource use authorization when a

resource use authorization was required,

(B) respecting unauthorized use of a resource

road, or

(C) in contravention of a resource use

authorization, a primary permit, access

permit or access authorization.

(3) The director may, if the following criteria are met,

issue a primary permit:

(a) the director determines that the application is

complete;

(b) the applicant holds a resource use authorization

that provides that, within the geographical

boundaries set out in the resource use

authorization, a person may construct a road or

use a vehicle;

(c) the director determines that there is no

practicable alternative to constructing a road or

designating an existing road to be a resource

road in order for the applicant to access the

resource for which they hold a resource use

authorization;

(d) the director is satisfied that the resource road,

if constructed or designated, would be

consistent with any approved land use plan in

place for any area in which the resource road is,

or is proposed to be, located;

(e) the director is satisfied that the applicant has

incorporated the use of existing roads in their

proposed route to the resource to the extent that

the director determines is reasonable;

(f) the director determines that issuing a permit is

consistent with any decision document issued

under the Yukon Environmental and Socio-

Economic Assessment Act (Canada);

(g) the director is satisfied that any adverse impacts

on existing or asserted Aboriginal or treaty

rights can be reasonably accommodated;

(h) the director determines the operational plan,

road construction plan and decommissioning

and reclamation plan submitted by the applicant

are satisfactory;

(i) if the Minister determines that security is

required, the applicant has made the security

payment required under subsection 14(3);

(j) if the application is with respect to an existing

road, the Minister has designated the road as a

resource road;

(k) the applicant has paid the fee determined by the

director and published on a Government of

Yukon website.

(4) Despite paragraph (3)(j), if the existing road has not

been designated a resource road, the director may issue

the primary permit conditional upon the Minister

designating the road as a resource road.

(5) After making a decision with respect to the

application, the director must provide the applicant with

notice in writing of the decision and the reasons for the

decision.

### Terms and conditions of a primary permit

21 (1) Each primary permit must include a term setting out

a fixed number of years for which the permit is to be

valid with the term not exceeding the date on which the

resource use authorization referred to in subsection 7(2)

and held by the applicant expires.

(2) Each primary permit must include terms and

conditions requiring the holder of the permit to maintain

the resource road, to decommission the resource road

and to reclaim the lands affected by the construction,

modification or use of the resource road in the manner

that the director determines is appropriate in the

circumstances.

(3) Each primary permit must include as a term and

condition of the permit each standard term and

condition of a permit for a resource road as those

standard terms and conditions are determined by the

director and published on a Government of Yukon

website.

(4) The director may include in the primary permit any

other term or condition that they consider appropriate.

### Statutory terms of primary permit

22 It is a term of each primary permit that the holder of

the permit

(a) must not hinder the use of the resource road by

a person authorized by the director to use the

resource road if the use is in accordance with

the terms of an access permit or access

authorization issued by the director;

(b) must work in good faith with each holder of an

access permit or access authorization to ensure

road safety and to prevent environmental

damage;

(c) must act in conformance with the operational

plan, road construction plan and the

decommissioning and reclamation plan; and

(d) must give to the Minister the security as

required by the Minister under the Act and the

regulations and make any security payments

required under subsection 14(3).

## PART 4

## ACCESS AND USE

### Access permit

23 An access permit authorizes the holder to access and use

a resource road in accordance with the terms and

conditions of the permit and in conformance with the

operational plan for the resource road.

### Eligibility for access permit

24 A person may apply for a permit to access and use a

resource road, if the person

(a) holds a resource use authorization; or

(b) has applied for a resource use authorization in

accordance with the statutory requirements for

the application.

### Permit application process

25 (1) An applicant for an access permit must submit their

application for an access permit in the form approved by

the director and must include with the application the

following:

(a) information to establish that the applicant is

eligible to apply for an access permit under

section 24;

(b) information to establish why the applicant needs

to use the resource road to access the resource

in respect of which they hold or have applied for

a resource use authorization or to access the

resource road in respect of which they have

applied for or hold a primary permit;

(c) a detailed map of the route the applicant is

proposing to use to access the resource that

(i) shows the location of each resource road,

highway or other road that the applicant

intends to use to access the resource, and

(ii) identifies all resource roads, highways or

roads in the area whether the applicant

intends to use them or not;

(d) information on how the applicant intends to use

the resource road including information on the

equipment to be used and the time and

frequency of use;

(e) information to establish that the proposed use of

the resource road conforms to any existing or

proposed operational plan for the resource road;

(f) information on how the applicant will connect

the resource road for which they are seeking an

access permit

(i) to the resource in respect of which they hold

or have applied for a resource use

authorization, and

(ii) if applicable, to a resource road in respect of

which they have applied for or hold a primary

permit;

(g) if the applicant and the holder of the primary

permit have entered into a user agreement, the

user agreement;

(h) any other information requested by the director;

(i) the application fee as determined by the director

and published on a Government of Yukon

website.

(2) The applicant must acknowledge in their application

that they understand that

(a) their use of the resource road will be shared with

other users that the director permits or

authorizes to use the resource road;

(b) the multi-use of the resource road will be a term

of the permit; and

(c) their access permit may be suspended or

terminated if the primary permit for the

resource road is suspended or terminated.

### Applications must be combined

26 If an applicant is applying for a primary permit under

section 8 and an access permit under section 25, the

applications must be combined and submitted to the

director together as a package.

### Security

27 (1) The Minister may require that an applicant for an

access permit give security if the director determines

that the applicant’s use of the resource road increases

the maintenance costs or reclamation costs respecting

the resource road beyond the amount that has been

provided in security by the holder of the primary permit.

(2) The amount of security that the Minister may

require that the applicant for the access permit give is

the amount the director calculates to be the additional

maintenance or reclamation costs.

(3) The Minister may require either

(a) that a payment in the total amount calculated

under subsection (2) be made before the

issuance of the access permit; or

(b) that an initial payment in an amount determined

by the Minister be made before the issuance of

the access permit and, in addition, subsequent

annual payments of security be made after the

permit is issued such that the total amount of

security is paid over the projected period of use

of the resource road.

(4) A security payment, whether a payment in the total

amount or an initial or subsequent payment, must be

made in cash or in another form determined by the

Minister to be sufficiently liquid, accessible and secure.

### Decision regarding access permit

28 (1) When the director determines that an application for

an access permit is complete, the director must make

one of the following decisions:

(a) to issue the permit in accordance with this

section if satisfied that the requirements of the

Act or this Regulation, including the criteria set

out in subsection (3), have been met;

(b) to refuse to issue the permit.

(2) The director must consider the following

information in deciding whether to issue an access

permit and in setting the terms and conditions of the

permit:

(a) information in the application;

(b) the results of consultation with First Nations, and

if applicable the Inuvialuit;

(c) comments from public engagement;

(d) whether the proposed use is consistent with the

operational plan;

(e) the applicant’s past compliance with permits or

authorizations issued by the Government of

Yukon, including

(i) whether the applicant has previously been

non-compliant with the terms of a resource

use authorization or of a primary permit,

access permit or access authorization,

(ii) whether there is an outstanding issue

respecting remediation or reclamation

requirements on another project for which a

resource use authorization or primary permit

has been issued to the applicant,

(iii) whether the applicant has been found guilty

of an offence

(A) respecting an activity undertaken without

a resource use authorization when a

resource use authorization was required,

(B) respecting unauthorized use of a resource

road, or

(C) in contravention of a resource use

authorization, primary permit, access

permit or access authorization.

(3) The director may, if the following criteria are met,

issue an access permit:

(a) the director determines that the application is

complete;

(b) the applicant holds a resource use authorization

that provides that, within the geographical

boundaries set out in the resource use

authorization a person may construct a road or

use a vehicle;

(c) the director determines that there is no

practicable alternative to the use of the resource

road for the applicant to access the resource in

respect of which they hold a resource use

authorization or to access a resource road in

respect of which they hold or have applied for a

primary permit;

(d) the director is satisfied that the proposed use

conforms to the operational plan for the

resource road;

(e) the director is satisfied that any adverse effects

on the environment caused by the use of the

resource road by the applicant can be

eliminated, reduced or controlled;

(f) the director is satisfied that any adverse impacts

on existing or asserted Aboriginal or treaty

rights caused by the use of the resource road by

the applicant can be reasonably accommodated;

(g) the director is satisfied that the proposed use is

consistent with any approved land use plan in

place for any area in which the resource road is

located;

(h) the director determines that issuing a permit is

consistent with any decision document issued

under the Yukon Environmental and Socio-

Economic Assessment Act (Canada);

(i) if the Minister determines that security is

required to be given, the applicant has made the

security payment required under

subsection 27(3);

(j) the applicant has paid the fee established by the

director and published on a Government of

Yukon website.

(4) After making a decision with respect to the

application, the director must provide the applicant with

notice in writing of the decision and the reasons for the

decision.

(5) If the director decides to issue the access permit,

the director must notify, in writing, the holder of the

primary permit, each holder of an access permit and

each First Nation, and if applicable the Inuvialuit,

consulted under section 18 of the issuance of the access

permit and the terms and conditions of the authorized

use.

### Terms of access permit

29 (1) The access permit must set out the date on which it

expires, which must not be later than the earlier of

(a) the date on which the primary permit for the

resource road expires; and

(b) the date on which the resource use authorization

held by the applicant expires.

(2) The director may include in the permit any other

term or condition that they consider appropriate.

### Statutory terms of access permit

30 It is a term of each access permit that the holder of the

permit

(a) must not hinder the use of the resource road by

a person authorized by the director to use the

resource road if the use is in accordance with

the terms and conditions of the primary permit,

access permit or an access authorization issued

by the director;

(b) must work in good faith with the holder of the

primary permit and each holder of an access

permit to ensure road safety and to prevent

environmental damage;

(c) must use the resource road in conformance with

the operational plan for the resource road; and

(d) must give to the Minister the security required

by the Minister under the Act and the regulations

and make any security payments required under

subsection 27(3).

### Access authorization

31 The following persons may apply, in the form required

by the director, to the director for authorization to use a

resource road:

(a) a person who intends to use the resource road

to exercise an existing or asserted Aboriginal or

treaty right;

(b) a person who holds a licence issued under the

Scientists and Explorers Act and intends to use

the resource road to undertake the activities

authorized by the licence;

(c) a person who holds a permit under the

Archaeological Sites Regulation and intends to

use the resource road to undertake the activities

authorized by the permit;

(d) a person who resides in Yukon and intends to

use the resource road exclusively to undertake

scientific or exploration activities not including

any activities that are political or commercial in

nature.

### Decision to issue access authorization

32 (1) The director may issue an access authorization if the

director is satisfied that the applicant is a person

referred to in section 31.

(2) The director may make the access authorization

subject to terms and conditions that the director

determines are reasonable in the circumstances.

(3) In deciding whether to issue an access

authorization, the director may consider whether the

applicant is or has been non-compliant with an access

authorization.

(4) The director must notify the applicant in writing of

the decision to issue the access authorization or not and,

if the director refuses to issue the access authorization,

the reasons for the refusal.

(5) If the director decides to issue the access

authorization, the director must notify, in writing, the

holder of the primary permit, each holder of an access

permit and each First Nation, and if applicable the

Inuvialuit, consulted under section 18 of the issuance of

the access authorization and the terms of the authorized

use.

### Terms of access under an authorization

33 It is a term of an access authorization that all access

conforms to the operational plan for the resource road.

## PART 5

## DIRECTIONS AND SUSPENSIONS

### Power to issue direction

34 (1) The director or an enforcement officer may issue a

direction, in writing, to the holder of a primary permit

or an access permit to take specified measures

(a) to come into compliance with the terms and

conditions of their permit, with the operational

plan, the road construction plan, the

decommissioning and reclamation plan or this

Regulation;

(b) to address a matter that is causing, or creating

a risk of, harm to human health or safety; or

(c) to address a matter that is causing, or creating

a risk of, environmental damage.

(2) A direction must

(a) describe the non-compliance or the matter that

must be addressed;

(b) set out the grounds on which the director or

enforcement officer believes

(i) that the holder of the permit is not in

compliance with the terms and conditions of

their permit, with the operational plan, the

road construction plan, the decommissioning

and reclamation plan or this Regulation,

(ii) that there is a matter that is causing, or

creating a risk of, harm to human health or

safety, or

(iii) that there is a matter that is causing, or

creating a risk of, environmental damage;

(c) identify specified measures that the holder of

the permit must take to address the non-

compliance or matter;

(d) state the time within which the specified

measures must be taken;

(e) state that failure to comply with the direction

constitutes an offence; and

(f) state that if the holder of the permit fails to

comply with the direction, the Minister may use

the security provided under section 4.5 of the

Act to take the specified measures.

(3) If the direction is issued to the holder of a primary

permit, the direction may include a requirement that the

holder of the permit immediately stop work on the

resource road and cease all use of the resource road

except as necessary to carry out the specified measures.

(4) If the direction is issued to the holder of an access

permit, the direction may include a requirement that the

holder of the access permit cease all use of the resource

road.

(5) A holder of a primary permit or an access permit to

whom a direction is issued under this section must

comply with the direction.

(6) A direction may be amended by the person who

issued it.

(7) A direction may be rescinded by the person who

issued it if they determine either that the holder of the

permit has taken the specified measures or that the

direction is no longer required because the matter is no

longer causing or creating a risk of harm to human

health or safety or causing or creating a risk of

environmental damage.

### Suspension of permits

35 (1) The director may suspend a primary permit or an

access permit for a specified number of days in each of

the following circumstances:

(a) the director or enforcement officer has issued a

direction under section 34 and the holder of the

permit has not, within the time set out in the

direction, taken the specified measures;

(b) the director reasonably believes that the holder

of the permit is not in compliance with the terms

and conditions of their permit, with the

operational plan, the road construction plan the

decommissioning and reclamation plan or this

Regulation and given the circumstances (such as

a history of non-compliance by the holder of the

permit) the director determines that it is

reasonable to suspend the permit;

(c) the director reasonably believes that using the

resource road is unsafe, or is causing, or is likely

to cause, harm to human health or safety or is

causing, or is likely to cause, environmental

damage.

(2) If the director suspends a primary permit or access

permit under paragraph (1)(b) or (c), the director must

provide to the holder of the permit the grounds for either

determining that a suspension is reasonable in the

circumstances or believing that using the resource road

is unsafe, or is causing, or is likely to cause, harm to

human health or safety or is causing, or is likely to cause,

environmental damage, as the case may be.

(3) The director must provide notice in writing of the

suspension of a primary permit or access permit under

subsection (1) to each holder of a permit or access

authorization that authorizes the use of the resource

road or the part of the resource road for which the

primary permit or access permit is suspended.

(4) The director must provide notice in writing of the

suspension of a primary permit or access permit under

subsection (1) to each First Nation, and if applicable the

Inuvialuit, consulted under section 18 respecting the

resource road.

(5) Notice under subsection (3) or (4) must include the

paragraph of this section under which the permit is

being suspended and, if the permit is being suspended

under paragraph (1)(b) or (c), the grounds for the

suspension as referred to in subsection (2).

(6) If a permit is suspended under paragraph (1)(c), the

director must suspend each permit or authorization that

authorizes the use of the resource road if the authorized

use is with respect to the part of the resource road that

the director believes is unsafe to use or where the use

of the road is causing, or is likely to cause, harm to

human health or safety or is causing, or is likely to cause,

environmental damage, as the case may be, and must

notify, in writing, the holder of each such permit or

authorization of the suspension.

(7) Despite a permit being suspended under this section,

the holder of the permit that was suspended may take

specified measures as necessary to comply with a

direction and may for that purpose use the resource

road.

### Extension of suspension

36 (1) If a holder of a primary permit or access permit that

is suspended under section 35 has been given a direction

under section 34 and does not take the measures

specified in the direction within the time specified for

the suspension in the direction, the director may extend

the period for which the permit is suspended for an

additional period of up to 30 days.

(2) If, after an additional period of suspension made

under subsection (1), the holder of a primary permit or

access permit has not taken the specified measures, the

director may extend the period for which the permit is

suspended for an additional period or periods of up to

12 months in total from the start of the additional period

of suspension referred to in subsection (1).

(3) If the permit has been suspended under

paragraph 35(1)(c), the director may suspend the

permit for an additional period or suspend the permit

indefinitely.

(4) If the director extends the suspension of a permit

under this section, the director must notify, in writing,

the holder of the primary permit and each holder of an

access permit or access authorization.

## PART 6

## TERMINATION OF PERMITS AND

## AUTHORIZATIONS

### Termination of permit

37 (1) The director may terminate a primary permit or an

access permit

(a) in the case of the holder of a primary permit or

the holder of an access permit that has given

security under section 4.5 of the Act, if the

holder of the permit has applied to have the

permit terminated and a letter of clearance has

been issued under section 43 to the holder of the

permit;

(b) in the case of the holder of a primary permit or

the holder of an access permit that was not

required to give security under section 4.5 of the

Act, upon request of the holder of the permit;

(c) in the case of a permit that has been suspended,

if the holder of the permit does not take the

specified measures within the time required by

the director or enforcement officer in a direction

issued under section 34 or, where the

suspension period has been extended for an

additional period or periods by the director

under section 36, within that additional period

or periods;

(d) in the case of the holder of a primary permit or

the holder of an access permit that has been

required by the Minister to give an additional

amount of security under section 4.5 of the Act,

if the holder of the permit has failed to pay the

additional amount;

(e) in the case of the holder of a primary permit or

the holder of an access permit that is required

by the Minister to make an annual security

payment, if the holder of the permit does not

make the security payment;

(f) if the director determines that the holder of the

permit no longer holds a valid resource use

authorization; or

(g) if the director determines that the use of the

resource road as authorized by the permit

(i) poses a safety risk for the users of the

resource road that the director reasonably

believes is unlikely or unable to be reduced to

an acceptable level of risk,

(ii) poses a risk to human health or safety that

the director reasonably believes is unlikely or

unable to be reduced to an acceptable level of

risk,

(iii) poses a risk of environmental damage that

the director reasonably believes is unlikely or

unable to be reduced to an acceptable level of

risk, or

(iv) is causing a serious and unjustifiable adverse

impact on existing or asserted Aboriginal or

treaty rights that can not be reasonably

accommodated.

(2) If a primary permit or an access permit has been

suspended under subsection 36(2) for a period or

periods of 12 months in total and the holder of the

permit has not within that time taken the specified

measures the director must terminate the permit.

(3) If the director terminates a primary permit and the

permit is not reissued under section 41, the director

must terminate each access permit and access

authorization that authorizes the use of the resource

road.

(4) Despite anything in this section, the director may

determine that a primary permit or access permit

should not be terminated if there is no practicable

alternative to the use of the resource road by the holder

of the permit in order for them to comply with

monitoring requirements or other requirements relating

to environmental protection under another Act or under

an authorization issued under another Act.

### Termination of access authorization

38 The director may terminate an access authorization

(a) upon the request of the holder of the access

authorization;

(b) if the director reasonably believes that the

holder of the access authorization has not

complied with the terms and conditions of the

access authorization or the operational plan; or

(c) if the director reasonably believes that continued

use of the resource road by the holder of the

access authorization

(i) poses a safety risk for the users of the

resource road,

(ii) poses a risk to human health or safety that is

unlikely or unable to be reduced to an

acceptable level of risk,

(iii) poses a risk of environmental damage that is

unlikely or unable to be reduced to an

acceptable level of risk, or

(iv) is causing a serious and unjustifiable adverse

impact on existing or asserted Aboriginal or

treaty rights that cannot be reasonably

accommodated.

### Notice of termination

39 (1) If the director terminates a primary permit under

subsection 37(1), the director must notify, in writing,

each holder of an access permit or access authorization

that authorizes the use of the resource road of the

termination of the primary permit and inform them as

to whether the Minister is intending to assume

temporary responsibility under subsection 40(1) for the

maintenance of the resource road.

(2) The director must, when notifying a holder of an

access permit under subsection (1), ask the holder of an

access permit whether they would be willing to hold the

primary permit on the same terms and conditions as the

holder whose permit is being terminated.

(3) A holder of an access permit who receives notice

under subsection (2) must, within 30 days of receiving

the notice, respond to the director indicating whether

they would be willing to hold the primary permit on the

same terms and conditions as the holder whose permit

is being terminated.

(4) The director must provide notice in writing of the

termination of an access permit or access authorization

to the following:

(a) the holder of the access permit or access

authorization which is being terminated;

(b) the holder of the primary permit for the resource

road for which use had been authorized;

(c) every other user of the resource road, if any.

(5) The director must provide notice in writing of the

termination of a primary permit, access permit or

access authorization to each First Nation, and if

applicable to the Inuvialuit, consulted under section 18

respecting the resource road.

### Maintenance where primary permit suspended or terminated

40 (1) If a primary permit is suspended or terminated, the

Minister may maintain the resource road on a temporary

basis until one of the following events occurs:

(a) the primary permit is no longer suspended;

(b) the director terminates each access permit and

access authorization with respect to the

resource road; or

(c) a new primary permit is issued with respect to

the resource road.

(2) The Minister may, for the purpose of maintaining

the resource road, use the security given under

section 4.5 of the Act.

(3) The Minister may, if security is used for

maintenance under subsection (2), require that the

holder of the resource road permit pay to the Minister

the amount of security that the Minister used for the

maintenance.

(4) If the amount that the Minister uses under

subsection (2) is more than the total amount of security

paid by the holder of the resource road permit to date,

the Minister may require that the holder of the permit

pay to the Minister the total amount of security used for

the maintenance.

### Reissue primary permit

41 (1) Subject to subsections (2) and (3), the director may,

if the director has terminated a primary permit, reissue

the primary permit, on the same terms and conditions

to a holder of an access permit authorizing them to use

the resource road as long as the reissuance of the permit

is made within 12 months of the termination under

subsection 37(1).

(2) The director may only reissue the primary permit

under subsection (1) in relation to the portion of the

resource road for which the holder of the access permit

is authorized access under their permit.

(3) The Minister must not return any security held with

respect to a primary permit to the holder of the primary

permit unless an equivalent amount is paid to the

Minister by the holder of the access permit.

(4) If the Minister requires that the holder of the access

permit give additional security under section 4.5 of the

Act, the director must not reissue the primary permit

until the holder of the access permit gives security in the

amount requested by the Minister.

(5) The director must provide notice in writing of the

reissuance of a primary permit under this section to

each holder of an access permit or access authorization

authorizing use of the resource road and each First

Nation, and if applicable the Inuvialuit, consulted under

section 18 respecting the resource road.

### Ongoing obligations

42 (1) Notwithstanding the termination or expiry of their

permit, a holder of a primary permit or a holder of an

access permit is responsible for the obligations arising

under the terms and conditions of their permit until the

director issues a letter of clearance respecting the

resource road.

(2) For greater certainty, the holder of a permit that has

been terminated or has expired must comply with all

outstanding requirements under the operational plan,

the road construction plan and the decommissioning and

reclamation plan and may use the resource road as

necessary for that purpose.

### Letter of clearance

43 When the director is satisfied that a holder of a primary

permit or a holder of an access permit has complied with

all of the terms and conditions of their permit, the

director must issue a letter of clearance to the holder of

the permit.

### Return of security

44 The Minister must, after the director issues a letter of

clearance, return the security to the holder of a primary

permit or the holder of an access permit who has given

security under section 4.5 of the Act.

### Closure of resource road

45 (1) The director must take the actions set out in

subsection (2) to close a resource road if

(a) the director terminates the primary permit; and

(b) either

(i) the Minister does not assume responsibility

for the maintenance of the resource road, or

(ii) the Minister assumes temporary

responsibility for the maintenance of the

resource road under section 40 and either

(A) no holder of an access permit indicates in

their response under subsection 39(3) that

they are willing to hold the primary permit

on the same terms and conditions as the

holder of the primary permit, or

(B) despite a holder of an access permit

indicating in their response under

subsection 39(3) that they are willing to

hold the primary permit on the same terms

and conditions as the holder of the primary

permit, the primary permit is not reissued

under section 41.

(2) The actions the director must take are the following:

(a) to terminate each access permit and access

authorization respecting the resource road;

(b) to inform the holder of the primary permit that

they must decommission the resource road and

reclaim the lands in compliance with the

decommissioning and reclamation plan;

(c) if the holder of the primary permit is not willing

or able to take actions to decommission the

resource road and reclaim the lands in

compliance with the decommissioning and

reclamation plan.

## PART 7

## AMENDMENT OF PLANS AND PERMITS

### Director may amend permit or plan

46 (1) The director may, in accordance with this section,

amend a primary permit, an access permit, an

operational plan, a road construction plan or a

decommissioning and reclamation plan

(a) if requested to do so by the holder of the primary

permit or the holder of an access permit;

(b) if the director reasonably believes the

amendment is necessary

(i) to protect the safety of the users of the

resource road,

(ii) to protect human health or safety,

(iii) to protect the environment, or

(iv) to address a serious and unjustifiable adverse

impact on existing or asserted Aboriginal or

treaty rights;

(c) if an approved land use plan has been amended

and the operational plan, road construction plan

or decommissioning and reclamation plan allows

use or an activity that are not consistent with

the approved land use plan as amended;

(d) if, due to the issuance of one or more access

permits or access authorizations, the director is

of the view that a change in the permit or the

plan is required to better manage the multi-use

of the resource road; or

(e) if the holder of the permit has made a request

to extend the term of their permit under

paragraph (a) in order to comply with

monitoring requirements or other requirements

relating to environmental protection under

another Act or under an authorization issued

under another Act and the director is of the view

that a change in the permit or the plan is

required due to the change in the use of the

resource road.

(2) The director must, prior to amending a primary

permit or an operational plan, determine whether the

circumstances require any of the following:

(a) consultation with First Nations or the Inuvialuit;

(b) engagement with the holders of access permits;

(c) engagement with holders of access

authorizations.

(3) The director must

(a) provide notice in writing of the proposed

amendment

(i) to the holder of the primary permit respecting

the resource road, and

(ii) to each holder of an access permit or an

access authorization who may be affected by

the amendment to the permit or plan; and

(b) if the director determines that consultation and

engagement is required, consult with

(i) each First Nation whose existing or asserted

Aboriginal or treaty rights could potentially be

adversely impacted by the amendment, and

(ii) the Inuvialuit, if their treaty rights could

potentially be adversely impacted by the

amendment.

(4) The holder of the primary permit and each holder of

an access permit or access authorization have a period

of 30 days to provide comments in writing to the

director.

(5) A First Nation or the Inuvialuit consulted under

paragraph (3)(b) has a period of 30 days to provide

comments in writing to the director.

(6) The director must consider all comments received

under subsections (4) and (5) before deciding whether

to amend the primary permit or the operational plan.

(7) The director may extend the period for comments

provided for under subsections (4) and (5).

(8) Despite determining that any consultation with First

Nations or the Inuvialuit, engagement with holders of

access permits or engagement with holders of access

authorizations is required in the circumstances, the

director may, if the director considers it necessary for

the purpose of protecting the safety of road users or the

environment or to address an issue adversely impacting

existing or asserted Aboriginal or treaty rights,

immediately amend the terms and conditions of a

permit or immediately amend an operational plan, a

road construction plan or a decommissioning and

reclamation plan prior to the consultation and

engagement.

(9) The director must notify, in writing, the holder of

the primary permit, each holder of an access permit or

access authorization and each First Nation or the

Inuvialuit consulted under paragraph (3)(b) of an

amendment to a permit or an operational plan, a road

construction plan or a decommissioning and reclamation

plan and the details of the amendment and the reasons

for the amendment.

### Extend term

47 (1) If the term of a resource use authorization held by

the holder of a primary permit or of an access permit is

extended or if the resource use authorization is renewed

such that the resource use authorization expires at a

later date than the date on which it expired at the time

the permit was issued, the holder of the primary permit

or access permit may apply for an amendment to their

permit under section 46.

(2) If a holder of a primary permit or an access permit

applies under section 46 for an amendment to extend

the term of their primary permit or access permit, the

new term must not extend beyond

(a) the date on which the resource use authorization

expires; or

(b) in the case of an access permit, the date on

which the primary permit expires.

(3) Despite subsection (2), the new term of a primary

permit or an access permit may be extended beyond the

date set out in paragraph (2)(a) if there is no practicable

alternative to the use of the resource road by the holder

of the permit in order for them to comply with

monitoring requirements or other requirements relating

to environmental protection under another Act or under

an authorization issued under another Act.

## PART 8

## ASSIGNMENT OF PRIMARY PERMIT

### Assignment of primary permit by holder

48 (1) A holder of a primary permit may apply in writing

to the director for permission to assign the permit to

another person.

(2) An application under subsection (1) must include

(a) a written agreement between the holder of the

primary permit and the assignee in which the

assignee agrees to assume responsibility for the

primary permit and comply with the terms and

conditions of the permit, the operational plan

and the decommissioning and reclamation plan;

and

(b) any fees for an assignment established by the

director and published on a Government of

Yukon website.

(3) Upon receiving an application to assign a primary

permit for a resource road, the director must notify, in

writing, each holder of an access permit or access

authorization whose permit or authorization authorizes

them to use the resource road.

(4) The director must, upon receiving an application to

assign a primary permit for a resource road, consult

with

(a) each First Nation whose existing or asserted

Aboriginal or treaty rights could potentially be

adversely impacted by the assignment; and

(b) the Inuvialuit, if their treaty rights could

potentially be adversely impacted.

(5) Each person who is notified under subsection (3)

and each First Nation, and if applicable the Inuvialuit,

consulted under subsection (4) has a period of 30 days

to provide comments in writing to the director

respecting their position on the assignment and reasons

for their position.

(6) The director may approve the assignment of the

primary permit if the Minister holds security with

respect to the resource road in the amount requested by

the Minister.

(7) Before approving the assignment, the director must

consider the assignee’s past compliance with permits or

authorizations issued by the Government of Yukon,

including

(a) whether the assignee has previously been non-

compliant with the terms and conditions of a

resource use authorization or of a primary

permit, access permit or access authorization;

(b) whether there is an outstanding issue respecting

remediation or reclamation requirements on

another project for which a resource use

authorization or primary permit has been issued

to the assignee; and

(c) whether the assignee has been found guilty of

an offence

(i) respecting an activity undertaken without a

resource use authorization when a resource

use authorization was required,

(ii) respecting unauthorized use of a resource

road, or

(iii) in contravention of a resource use

authorization, primary permit, access permit

or access authorization.

(8) The Minister must not return any security held with

respect to a primary permit to the holder of the primary

permit unless an equivalent amount is paid to the

Minister by the assignee.

(9) The Minister may request that an additional amount

of security be given by the assignee and if the Minister

does, the director must not approve the assignment

unless the additional amount of security is paid to the

Minister.

(10) On approval of the assignment, the director must

(a) provide notice in writing of the approval to the

holder of the primary permit, the assignee, each

holder of an access permit or an access

authorization respecting the resource road and

each First Nation, and if applicable the Inuvialuit,

consulted under subsection (4); and

(b) issue a letter of clearance to the holder of the

primary permit.

## PART 9

## ENFORCEMENT

### Powers of enforcement officer

49 (1) An enforcement officer may, for the purposes of

ensuring compliance with the terms and conditions of a

primary permit, access permit or access authorization

or enforcing this Regulation, stop a person who is on or

using a resource road and request that the person

(a) show a primary permit, access permit or access

authorization that permits their access to or use

of the resource road; and

(b) identify themselves.

(2) If a person is in a vehicle, an enforcement officer

may, for the purpose of carrying out subsection (1),

signal to the person operating the vehicle that they must

stop and turn off the vehicle.

(3) An enforcement officer may, for the purpose of

ensuring compliance with the terms and conditions of a

primary permit, access permit or access authorization

or enforcing this Regulation,

(a) inspect a vehicle to determine whether the use

of the vehicle in the circumstances conforms to

the permit or authorization, to the operational

plan, the road construction plan, the

decommissioning and reclamation plan and this

Regulation;

(b) require the production of any document or

information relevant to determining whether the

person’s access to or use of the resource road in

the circumstances conforms to the permit or

authorization, the operational plan, the road

construction plan the decommissioning and

reclamation plan and this Regulation;

(c) enter any place or premises on territorial lands

under the ownership or occupation of a holder

of a permit, other than a private dwelling, and

inspect or investigate as the enforcement officer

considers necessary; or

(d) seize, during an inspection or an investigation,

any thing in plain view of the officer that the

officer believes is evidence of an offence under

this Regulation.

### Duty to comply

50 (1) A person accessing or using a resource road must,

if requested or indicated to do so by an enforcement

officer,

(a) stop and identify themselves to the enforcement

officer; and

(b) show to the enforcement officer the primary

permit, access permit or access authorization

permitting their access or use.

(2) A holder of a primary permit or an access permit

must permit an enforcement officer to enter any place

or premises on territorial lands under their ownership

or occupation, other than a private dwelling, and to

make the inspections or investigations the enforcement

officer considers necessary.

### Obstruction of enforcement officer

51 (1) A person must not hinder or obstruct an

enforcement officer in carrying out their duties.

(2) A person must not provide false information to an

enforcement officer who is enforcing this Regulation.

## PART 10

## WINTER ROADS

### Construction, etc. of winter road

52 Despite anything in this Regulation, a person does not

require a primary permit to construct, modify, maintain,

close or decommission a winter road if they do so in

accordance with a permit issued under

paragraph 24(1)(a) or 26(a) of the Land Use Regulation .

### Use of winter road

53 (1) A person may enter on or use a winter road to

access a resource without a primary permit or an access

permit if they do so in accordance with the terms and

conditions of a permit issued under paragraph 24(1)(a)

or 26(a) of the Land Use Regulation .

(2) If a winter road is constructed and maintained under

the authority of a permit issued under

paragraph 24(1)(a) or 26(a) of the Land Use Regulation ,

a person may, without an access authorization, use the

road for recreational purposes or to exercise an asserted

or existing Aboriginal or treaty.

### Reclamation of lands

54 A person may reclaim lands affected by the construction,

modification or use of a winter road without a primary

permit if they do so in accordance with the terms and

conditions of a permit issued under paragraph 24(1)(a)

or 26(a) of the Land Use Regulation that provides for the

restoration of the lands.

### Application for primary permit under this regulation

55 (1) If a person applies under section 8 for a primary

permit authorizing the construction of a winter road

(a) consultation under section 18 must consider

whether, in the circumstances, the regulation of

the construction and use is appropriate under

this Regulation ; and

(b) public engagement under section 19 must

consider whether, in the circumstances, the

regulation of the construction and use is

appropriate under this Regulation.

(2) The director may issue a primary permit for a winter

road only if, in addition to determining that the criteria

set out in subsection 20(3) have been met, the director

determines that it is appropriate in the circumstances to

regulate the construction and use of the winter road

under this Regulation.

## PART 11

## MISCELLANEOUS

### Powers of director

56 The director may approve operational plans, road

construction plans and decommissioning and

reclamation plans based on criteria determined by the

director.

### Delegation by director

57 The director may delegate, in writing, to a person who

is a member of the public service any power the director

has under this Regulation, including a discretionary

power and a decision-making power.

## PART 12

## TRANSITIONAL

### Definitions

58 In this Part

“ lease ” means a lease entered into with the Government

of Yukon; « bail »

“ permit ” means a permit issued under the Land Use

Regulation. « permis »

### Authorization to use road

59 Despite anything in this Regulation, a person who,

immediately before the coming into force of this

Regulation, was authorized, under a lease or under a

permit to enter on, construct, modify, use, maintain,

close or decommission a road or reclaim lands affected

by the construction, modification or use of a road that,

on the coming into force of this Regulation, is a resource

road may continue to do so on the terms and conditions

of the lease or permit until

(a) if the person does not apply for a primary permit

or an access permit before the day on which the

lease or permit expires, the earlier of

(i) the day on which the lease or permit expires,

and

(ii) the day on which the lease is terminated or

the permit is cancelled; or

(b) if the person applies for a primary permit or an

access permit before the day on which the lease

or permit expires, the earlier of

(i) the day on which the application is withdrawn

or is considered under section 17 to have

been withdrawn, and

(ii) the day on which the primary permit or

access permit is issued or is denied.

### Application for permit submitted

60 (1) A person may, on the issuance of a permit, enter on,

construct, modify, use, maintain, close or decommission

a road or reclaim lands affected by the construction,

modification or use of a road in accordance with the

terms and conditions of the permit as if the permit had

been issued immediately before the coming into force of

this Regulation if the person, before the coming into

force of this Regulation,

(a) submitted an application for the permit under

subsection 21(1) of the Land Use Regulation ; and

(b) complied with the requirements of

subsection 21(2) of the Land Use Regulation .

(2) For greater certainty, section 59 applies to a permit

referred to in subsection (1).

### Suspension

61 For greater certainty, a person may not enter on,

construct, modify, use, maintain, close or decommission

a road or reclaim lands affected by the construction,

modification or use of a road under section 59 if a

permit authorizing the person to do so is suspended.

### Amendment to lease or permit

62 For greater certainty, the terms and conditions of a lease

or permit referred to in section 59 may be amended

(a) in the case of a lease, in accordance with the

terms and conditions of the lease; and

(b) in the case of a permit, in accordance with the

Land Use Regulation.

### No extension

63 Despite a right to renew a lease or extend the term of a

lease under the terms and conditions of the lease, the

Government of Yukon must not renew a lease or extend

the term of a lease beyond a day that is one year after

the day on which this Regulation comes into force.

### Use of road without lease or permit

64 Despite anything in this Regulation, on the coming into

force of this Regulation, a person may use a resource

road for which authorization under a permit or lease is

not otherwise required until the day that is one year

after the day on which this Regulations comes into force.

## PART 13

## COMING INTO FORCE

### Coming into force

65 This regulation comes into force on the later of the

following days:

(a) the day on which the Act to amend the Territorial

Lands (Yukon) Act (2021) comes into force;

(b) the day that is 60 days after the day on which it

is filed with the registrar of regulations under

the Regulations Act .
