YOIC 2023/135

*Made under the Missing Persons Act*

### Definitions

1 (1) In the Act and this Regulation

“ minor ” has the same meaning as in the Age of Majority

Act . « mineur »

(2) In this Regulation

“ commanding officer ” means the individual designated

as the Commanding Officer of RCMP “M” Division under

the Royal Canadian Mounted Police Act (Canada);

« commandant divisionnaire »

“ family violence ” has the same meaning as in the

Family Violence Prevention Act ; « violence familiale »

“ guardian ” means a guardian, alternate guardian or

temporary guardian appointed under the Adult

Protection and Decision Making Act , or the Public

Guardian and Trustee when exercising authority under

section 54 of that Act; « tuteur »

“ near relative ”, in relation to a missing person who is a

vulnerable person, means

(a) a parent, grandparent, adult child or adult

sibling of the vulnerable person, or

(b) an adult, other than one described in

paragraph (a), who

(i) is related to the vulnerable person by blood,

or is or was related to the vulnerable person

through a spousal relationship or through

adoption, and

(ii) was residing with the vulnerable person at the

time immediately before the person was

missing; « proche parent »

“ parent ”, in relation to a missing person who is a minor,

means

(a) a mother or father of the minor who has

custody of the minor,

(b) a mother or father of the minor who does not

have custody of the minor but who regularly

exercises or attempts to exercise rights of

access,

(c) a mother or father of the minor who provides

financial support for the minor,

(d) an individual to whom custody of the minor

has been granted by a court of competent

jurisdiction or by an agreement, or

(e) an individual with whom the minor resided

immediately before the minor was missing

and who stands in place of the minor’s mother

or father; « parent »

“ spouse ”, in relation to a missing person who is a

vulnerable person, means

(a) an individual to whom the vulnerable person

is legally married,

(b) an individual who is united to the vulnerable

person by a marriage that, although not a

legal marriage, is valid by common law, or

(c) an individual with whom the vulnerable

person was cohabiting as a couple for a period

of at least 12 months immediately before the

vulnerable person was missing. « conjoint »

### Criteria respecting elevated risk of harm

2 For the purposes of sections 5 and 7 of the Act, the

following prescribed criteria are to be taken into account

in determining whether there are reasonable grounds to

believe that a missing person is at an elevated risk of

harm:

(a) whether there is reason to believe that the

person provided sexual services for

consideration shortly before being missing;

(b) whether there is reason to believe that the

person hitchhiked or shared a trip in a motor

vehicle with a stranger shortly before being

missing;

(c) whether there is reason to believe that the

person experienced or fled violence, including

family violence, shortly before being missing;

(d) whether there is reason to believe that the

person had caused self-harm shortly before

being missing;

(e) whether there is reason to believe that the

person had a substance use problem shortly

before being missing;

(f) whether there is reason to believe that the

person has a chronic medical condition that

requires medication;

(g) whether there is reason to believe that, shortly

before being missing, the person had a medical

condition that posed a serious or immediate

threat to their health;

(h) whether there is reason to believe that the

person had not been prepared to deal with the

weather at, or the terrain of, the location where

they may have gone missing.

### Application for record access order, third party record access order or search order

3 (1) Applications for the following orders must be in the

following forms:

(a) for a record access order, the form set out in

Form 1 of the Schedule;

(b) for a third party record access order, the form

set out in Form 3 of the Schedule;

(c) for a search order, the form set out in Form 5 of

the Schedule.

(2) For the purpose of section 3 of the Act, the

prescribed manner for applying for an order referred to

in subsection (1) is

(a) in person; or

(b) by means of telecommunication described in

subsection 487.1(1) or (5) of the Criminal Code

(Canada), as if the application were an

application set out in subsection 487.1(1) of the

Criminal Code (Canada).

(3) The procedure in respect of an application made in

accordance with paragraph (2)(b) must comply with

section 487.1 of the Criminal Code (Canada), with any

necessary modifications.

### Record access order, third party record access order and search order

4 The following are prescribed as the form and content for

the following orders:

(a) for a record access order, the form and content

set out in Form 2 of the Schedule;

(b) for a third party record access order, the form

and content set out in Form 4 of the Schedule;

(c) for a search order, the form and content set out

in Form 6 of the Schedule.

### Emergency demand for records

5 (1) For the purpose of subsection 9(2) of the Act, the

prescribed form and the content of an emergency

demand for records are the form and content set out in

Form 7 of the Schedule.

(2) For the purpose of subsection 9(3) of the Act, the

prescribed form for a report under that subsection is the

following:

(a) the form set out in Form 8 of the Schedule;

(b) a copy of the signed emergency demand for

records.

(3) For the purpose of subsection 9(3) of the Act, the

prescribed manner of filing a report under that

subsection is by mail, fax, email or any other method

accepted by the commanding officer.

### Order requiring compliance with emergency demand for records

6 (1) An application for an order requiring compliance

with an emergency demand for records must be in the

form set out in Form 9 of the Schedule.

(2) An application for an order requiring compliance

with an emergency demand for records must be made

in person.

(3) The form and content of an order requiring

compliance with an emergency demand for records are

prescribed to be the form and content set out in Form 10

of the Schedule.

### Disclosure of information relating to minor without consent

7 (1) For the purpose of paragraph 15(2)(f) of the Act, if

a missing person who has been found alive is a minor,

the following individuals may make a request to the

RCMP, in the form set out in Form 11 of the Schedule,

for disclosure of information collected pursuant to a

search order or from a record to which access was given

under the Act, or from a copy of such a record, relating

to the minor:

(a) a parent of the minor;

(b) an individual who has, or had, a parent-like

relationship with the minor, including the

following individuals:

(i) a foster parent,

(ii) a step-parent,

(iii) an individual who stands or has stood in the

role of parent to the minor on a short- or

long-term basis or is an adoptive parent of

the minor, according to laws or customs of

(A) a First Nation, Inuit or Métis group,

community or people that holds rights

recognized and affirmed by section 35 of

the Constitution Act, 1982 , or

(B) a council, government or other entity that

is authorized to act on behalf of a First

Nation, Inuit or Métis group, community or

people that holds rights recognized and

affirmed by section 35 of the Constitution

Act, 1982 ,

(iv) an individual, other than one described in

subparagraph (i), (ii) or (iii), who stands or

has stood in the role of parent to the minor

on a short- or long-term basis.

(2) Subject to subsection (3), on receiving a request

under subsection (1), a member of the RCMP may

disclose the information to the individual who requested

disclosure if the member of the RCMP has reasonable

grounds to believe that

(a) the missing person who has been found alive is

a minor;

(b) the individual who requested disclosure is an

individual described in subsection (1);

(c) the disclosure will protect the minor’s safety,

having considered the circumstances, including

the relationship of the minor with the individual

who requested disclosure, any relevant

convictions or court orders and any evidence of

abuse, neglect, exploitation or violence,

including family violence; and

(d) one or more of the following applies in respect

of the minor:

(i) the minor is not able to understand the nature

of the information,

(ii) the minor is not able to understand the

consequences of the information not being

disclosed to the individual who requested

disclosure,

(iii) the minor

(A) is able to understand the nature of the

information,

(B) is able to understand the consequences of

the information not being disclosed to the

individual who requested disclosure, and

(C) has not objected or would not be expected

to object to the disclosure of the

information.

(3) A member of the RCMP may disclose the information

to the individual who requested disclosure only if the

commanding officer or a member of the RCMP

designated by the commanding officer for the purpose

of this subsection agrees that there are reasonable

grounds to believe that the disclosure will protect the

minor ’ s safety.

(4) The commanding officer may, in writing, designate

another member of the RCMP to act in the commanding

officer ’ s place for the purpose of subsection (3).

### Disclosure of information relating to vulnerable person without consent

8 (1) For the purpose of paragraph 15(2)(f) of the Act, if

a missing person who has been found alive is a

vulnerable person, the following individuals may make a

request to the RCMP, in the form set out in Form 11 of

the Schedule, for disclosure of information collected

pursuant to a search order or from a record to which

access was given under the Act, or from a copy of such

a record, relating to the vulnerable person:

(a) a guardian of the vulnerable person;

(b) the spouse of the vulnerable person;

(c) an individual who is an associate decision-maker

or representative under the Adult Protection and

Decision Making Act in respect of the vulnerable

person, respecting information reasonably

required for the individual to exercise the

authority given to them under that Act;

(d) a near relative of the vulnerable person.

(2) Subject to subsection (3), on receiving a request

under subsection (1), a member of the RCMP may

disclose the information to the individual who requested

disclosure if the member of the RCMP has reasonable

grounds to believe that

(a) the missing person who has been found alive is

a vulnerable person;

(b) the individual who requested disclosure is an

individual described in subsection (1);

(c) the disclosure will protect the vulnerable

person ’ s safety, having considered the

circumstances, including the relationship of the

vulnerable person with the individual who

requested disclosure, any relevant convictions or

court orders and any evidence of abuse, neglect,

exploitation or violence, including family

violence; and

(d) one of the following applies in respect of the

vulnerable person:

(i) the vulnerable person is not able to

understand the consequences of the

information not being disclosed to the

individual who requested disclosure,

(ii) the vulnerable person is able to understand

the consequences of the information not

being disclosed to the individual who

requested disclosure and has not objected or

would not be expected to object to the

disclosure of the information.

(3) A member of the RCMP may disclose the information

to the individual who requested disclosure only if the

commanding officer or a member of the RCMP

designated by the commanding officer for the purpose

of this subsection agrees that there are reasonable

grounds to believe that the disclosure will protect the

vulnerable person ’ s safety.

(4) The commanding officer may, in writing, designate

another member of the RCMP to act in the commanding

officer ’ s place for the purpose of subsection (3).

### Service requirements

9 (1) Subject to subsection (2), the prescribed or required

manner for service of an order, an emergency demand

for records or an application for an order requiring

compliance with an emergency demand for records is as

follows:

(a) on an individual

(i) by leaving a copy of it with the individual, or

(ii) by fax or email, if the fax number or email

address of the individual is known and the

sender receives confirmation that the

transmission was successfully completed;

(b) on a corporation

(i) by leaving a copy of the document with an

officer, director or agent of the corporation,

or with an individual at any place of business

or registered office of the corporation, or with

an individual who appears to be in control or

management of the place of business,

(ii) by fax or email, to the corporation’s last

known fax number or email address, if the

sender receives confirmation that the

transmission was successfully completed, or

(iii) by mail;

(c) on an entity other than an individual or a

corporation

(i) by leaving a copy of the document with an

officer or administrator of the entity who

appears to have control or management

responsibilities with respect to the entity,

(ii) by fax or email to the entity’s last known fax

number or email address, if the sender

receives confirmation that the transmission

was successfully completed, or

(iii) by mail.

(2) A judge, on application and on being satisfied that

service cannot be made effectively in accordance with

subsection (1), may, by order, authorize another

manner of service.

### Matter, practice or procedure

10 Where a matter, practice or procedure respecting an

application is not provided for in the Act or this

Regulation, the matter, practice or procedure may be

determined with reference to the Supreme Court Rules,

and at any stage in a proceeding, the judge may give

any direction or make any order that the judge considers

appropriate.

### Defect in form

11 No proceeding on an application under the Act is

defeated by reason only of an alleged defect of form.

### Limiting access to court records

12 A judge who hears an application under the Act may

order that any records in respect of the application be

placed in a sealed confidential file if the judge considers

it necessary for the protection of the best interests of the

missing person or any other person.

### Annual report respecting emergency demands for records

13 (1) For the purpose of paragraph 13(2)(c) of the Act, the

following information is prescribed information to be

included in the annual report respecting emergency

demands for records for a given year:

(a) a list of the types of records set out in section 10

of the Act that were the subject of an emergency

demand for records in that year;

(b) the number of times an emergency demand for

records was used in that year because of a belief

of a risk of imminent serious bodily harm or

death to the missing person;

(c) the number of times an emergency demand for

records was used in that year because of a belief

of a likelihood that the records would be

destroyed;

(d) the number of orders requiring compliance with

an emergency demand for records applied for

and the number of applications granted.

(2) For the purpose of paragraph 13(3)(b) of the Act, the

annual report respecting emergency demands for

records for a given year must be published by July 31 of

the next year.

### Annual report respecting record access orders, third party record access orders and search orders

14 (1) The RCMP must prepare an annual report respecting

its use of record access orders, third party record access

orders and search orders in a given year.

(2) The annual report must include the following:

(a) the number of missing person occurrences

reported in that year;

(b) the sex or gender identity, and the age or age

range of the persons reported missing in that

year;

(c) the number of investigations into the

whereabouts of missing persons in respect of

which the missing person was found in that

year;

(d) the number of applications made for each type

of order in that year;

(e) the number of times an application for an order

was refused in that year.

(3) The annual report for a given year must be provided

to the Minister by July 31 of the next year.

(4) Within 30 days of receiving the annual report, the

Minister must cause the report to be published on the

website of the Minister ’ s department or by any other

means that the Minister considers appropriate.

### Storage, retention and disposal of information or records

15 (1) This section applies only to the extent that the

Government of Canada does not have exclusive

jurisdiction in respect of the storage, retention and

disposal of information collected pursuant to a search

order or from a record to which access was given under

the Act, or from a copy of such a record.

(2) Information that was collected pursuant to a search

order or from a record to which access was given under

the Act, or from a copy of such a record, must be stored

securely.

(3) Access to the information or record or copy referred

to in subsection (2) must be limited to the members of

the RCMP who, as determined by the commanding

officer or the commanding officer’s delegate, require

access.

(4) Subject to subsection (5), if a missing person is

found alive, the RCMP must ensure that the information,

record or copy referred to in subsection (2) is disposed

of within 90 days after the date on which the missing

person is found.

(5) If a missing person is found alive and a further

investigation related to the disappearance has been

initiated or is likely to be initiated, the information,

record or copy referred to in subsection (2) may be

retained until all related investigations are concluded.

(6) The RCMP may retain the information, record or

copy referred to in subsection (2) if the missing person

is not found or is found dead.

(7) For the purpose of subsection (5), an investigation

includes an investigation or inquest conducted by a

coroner under the Coroners Act.

### Coming into force

16 This Regulation comes into force on the later of the

following days:

(a) the day on which the Missing Persons Act comes

into force;

(b) the day on which it is filed with the registrar of

regulations under the Regulations Act.
