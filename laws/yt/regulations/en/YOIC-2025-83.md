# Enforcement of Security Agreement by Sheriff Regulation

Always check the official version before relying on it.

YOIC 2025/83

*Made under the Financial Administration Act*

### Definitions

1 “ collateral ” has the same meaning as in the Personal

Property Security Act ; « bien grevé »

“ debtor ” has the same meaning as in the Personal

Property Security Act ; « débiteur »

“ personal property security registry ” means the

personal property security registry established under

section 40 of the Personal Property Security Act ;

« réseau d’enregistrement des sûretés mobilières »

“ secured party ” has the same meaning as in the

Personal Property Security Act ; « partie garantie »

“ security agreement ” has the same meaning as in the

Personal Property Security Act ; « contrat de sûreté »

“ sheriff ” means a sheriff or deputy sheriff appointed

under the Judicature Act and includes any person

discharging the duties of a sheriff or deputy sheriff in a

particular case at a particular time. « shérif »

### Power of sheriff to enforce security agreement

2 (1) Upon written request by a secured party, a sheriff is

authorized to act on behalf of the secured party to seize

collateral or otherwise enforce a security agreement that

is enforceable under section 56 of the Personal Property

Security Act .

(2) The sheriff is authorized, for the purposes of seizing

collateral or otherwise enforcing the security agreement,

to exercise any rights or remedies available to the

secured party under section 56 of the Personal Property

Security Act .

(3) The sheriff must provide to the debtor or the person

who was in possession of seized collateral a notice of

seizure indicating the date of the seizure and an

inventory of seized collateral.

### Required documents

3 A secured party requesting the services of a sheriff to

seize collateral or otherwise enforce a security

agreement must provide the sheriff with the following:

(a) proof of registration in the personal property

security registry;

(b) a letter of instruction;

(c) a letter of indemnification, including a term

stating that the secured party is responsible for

(i) any fees, recoverable costs or travel expenses

incurred or anticipated to be incurred by the

sheriff during the seizure of collateral or other

enforcement of the security agreement,

(ii) any costs or expenses claimed or anticipated

to be claimed by a third party arising from

the seizure of collateral or other enforcement

of the security agreement, and

(iii) any claims for damages, including claims

made by the debtor or a third party, arising

from the seizure of collateral or other

enforcement of the security agreement;

(d) any other document that the sheriff considers

necessary to establish that the secured party is

entitled to act under section 56 of the Personal

Property Security Act .

### Authority to refuse or discontinue action

4 (1) A sheriff may refuse to initiate or may discontinue

any seizure of collateral or any other enforcement of a

security agreement unless a secured party

(a) provides the required documents referred to in

section 3; and

(b) provides an indemnification deposit sufficient to

indemnify the sheriff

(i) in respect of fees, recoverable costs or travel

expenses incurred or anticipated to be

incurred by the sheriff during the seizure of

collateral or other enforcement of the security

agreement,

(ii) in respect of any costs or expenses claimed or

anticipated to be claimed by a third party

arising from the seizure of collateral or other

enforcement of the security agreement, and

(iii) against any claims for damages, including

claims made by the debtor or a third party,

arising from the seizure of collateral or other

enforcement of the security agreement.

(2) The sheriff may refuse to initiate or may discontinue

any seizure of collateral or any other enforcement of the

security agreement if the sheriff reasonably determines

that the seizure or enforcement would pose a significant

risk to the safety of any person.

(3) If the sheriff refuses to initiate or discontinues a

seizure or enforcement, they must notify the secured

party within a reasonable time and provide a brief

explanation of the reasons for the refusal or

discontinuation.

(4) The amount and form of the indemnification deposit

are determined by the sheriff, subject to the approval of

the sheriff’s manager.

### Assignment of indemnification deposit

5 (1) A sheriff may assign an indemnification deposit

provided to the sheriff under section 4 to any person

other than the debtor who claims an interest in the

seized collateral or who incurs damages, costs or

expenses arising from the seizure of collateral or other

enforcement of the security agreement.

(2) The sheriff may apply the indemnification deposit to

(a) cover fees, recoverable costs or travel expenses

incurred by the sheriff during the seizure of

collateral or other enforcement of the security

agreement;

(b) cover costs or expenses claimed by a third party

arising from the seizure of collateral or other

enforcement of the security agreement; or

(c) satisfy claims for damages, including claims

made by the debtor or a third party, arising

from the seizure of collateral or other

enforcement of the security agreement.

### Recoverable costs

6 (1) A secured party requesting the services of a sheriff

to seize collateral or otherwise enforce a security

agreement is liable for all recoverable costs incurred by

the sheriff in connection with the seizure or

enforcement.

(2) Recoverable costs include

(a) operational expenses directly related to the

seizure or enforcement of the security

agreement;

(b) administrative costs; and

(c) costs associated with the handling, storage or

disposal of seized collateral.

(3) Recoverable costs do not include travel expenses or

fees.

### Travel expenses

7 (1) A secured party requesting the services of a sheriff

to seize collateral or otherwise enforce a security

agreement is liable for all travel expenses incurred by

the sheriff for a seizure or enforcement conducted

outside of a 50-kilometre radius of the sheriff’s office.

(2) Travel expenses include

(a) mileage, meals and per diem expenses, at rates

established under the Government Travel

Management Board Directive 13/84, as amended

from time to time;

(b) air transportation; and

(c) accommodation.

### Fees

8 A request by a secured party to a sheriff to seize

collateral or otherwise enforce a security agreement is

subject to the following fees:

(a) an administrative fee of $600 per request;

(b) a service fee of $90 per hour per sheriff for all

activities relating to the seizure or enforcement,

including the repossession of collateral and all

post-seizure activity.

### Expense and fee splitting

9 (1) A sheriff may coordinate the seizure of collateral or

other enforcements of multiple secured parties in the

same region to minimize service fees and travel

expenses.

(2) The sheriff may apportion service fees and travel

expenses among multiple secured parties based on the

distances that were travelled and the amount of time

and extent of resources that were required to complete

each seizure or other enforcement.

### Limitation of liability

10 After a sheriff transfers possession of seized collateral to

a secured party or to any person authorized by the

secured party to receive the collateral or otherwise

completes the enforcement of a security agreement, the

sheriff is not liable for any

(a) loss of or damage to the collateral; or

(b) interference with the rights of the debtor or any

other person in relation to the collateral.
