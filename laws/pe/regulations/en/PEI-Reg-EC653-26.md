PEI Reg EC653/26

*Made under the Employment Standards Act*

Pursuant to section 100 of the Employment Standards Act R.S.P.E.I. 1988, Cap. E-6.3, Council made

the following regulations:

## APPLICATION OF ACT

### Application of Act to salespersons and farm labourers

1.(1) The Act, except sections 36 to 39, and Parts 7 and 8 as they relate to those sections, does not

apply to

(a) salespersons whose income is derived primarily from commission on sales; or

(b) farm labourers employed in an undertaking that, in the opinion of the Board, is not a

commercial undertaking.

### Act provisions that do not apply to private or live-in caregivers

(2) Sections 15, 20 and 21 of the Act, and any orders made pursuant to section 15 or 20 of the Act,

do not apply to

(a) employees solely employed to care for a child, a person with a disability or an older

person in a private home; or

(b) employees of a non-profit organization who are required to live in a facility operated

by the organization in the course of their employment.

Act provisions that do not apply to returning officers, election clerks or election officers

(3) Sections 17, 27 and 28 of the Act do not apply to returning officers, election clerks or election

officers appointed pursuant to section 9, 10 or 14.1, respectively, of the Election Act R.S.P.E.I.

1988, Cap. E-1.1.

### Act provisions that do not apply to athletes

(4) Sections 15, 20 to 35, and Part 6 of the Act, and any order made pursuant to section 15 or 20

of the Act, do not apply to persons employed as athletes.

## HOURS OF WORK

### Standard weekly hours

2. When making an order pursuant to subsection 15(2) of the Act establishing a standard number

of hours of work that exceeds 44 hours in a work week, the Board shall consider the following

additional factors:

(a) operational requirements, including production schedules, service demands and any

requirements for continuous operations;

(b) fair treatment of employees, including adequate rest periods, reasonable scheduling

and protection from exploitation;

(c) any benefit to the economy, public services or community needs;

(d) any special circumstances, including unforeseen events, urgent requirements and

emergencies.

## PAY

3. Notice of withholding, deduction or return of pay – group benefit or savings plan

For the purpose of clause 38(2)(a) or (b) of the Act, an employer shall give an employee notice

in writing of the employer’s intention to withhold, deduct or require the return of all or part of

an employee’s pay for the purpose of contributing to a group benefit plan or savings plan, which

shall include

(a) the required or requested amount of the contribution;

(b) the required or requested contribution schedule; and

(c) the required or requested method of contribution.

4. Notice of withholding, deduction or return of pay – advance, overpayment or cash shortage

(1) For the purpose of clause 38(2)(c) or (d) of the Act, an employer shall give an employee notice

in writing of the employer’s intention to withhold, deduct or require the return of all or part of

an employee’s pay to reconcile an advance or overpayment of pay or to cover a cash shortage,

which shall include

(a) the amount of the advance, overpayment or cash shortage;

(b) the reason for the advance, overpayment or cash shortage;

(c) the proposed repayment schedule; and

(d) the method of repayment.

### Opportunity to review

(2) An employer shall give an employee a reasonable opportunity to review and discuss the

proposed repayment schedule.

### Agreement

(3) The employer and the employee may enter into an agreement providing for the employer to

withhold or deduct or the employee to return all or part of the employee’s pay in accordance

with a repayment schedule.

### No agreement

(4) Where the employer and the employee do not enter into an agreement, the employer may

recover the advance, overpayment or cash shortage in accordance with the proposed repayment

schedule in the notice, provided that

(a) the advance, overpayment or cash shortage occurred within the past 12 months;

(b) the employment has not been terminated by the employer or the employee;

(c) not more than 10 per cent of the employee’s gross wages is withheld, deducted or

returned per pay period;

(d) the repayment period does not exceed 12 months; and

(e) the repayment period is reasonable considering

(i) the amount of the advance, overpayment or cash shortage,

(ii) the employee’s gross earnings per pay period, and

(iii) any financial hardship of the employee.

### Request to modify repayment schedule

(5) An employee may request a modification of the repayment schedule based on financial

hardship and the employer shall consider the request in good faith.

### No interest, administrative fees or penalties

(6) An employer shall not charge interest, administrative fees or penalties in respect of an advance

or overpayment of pay, or a cash shortage.

## LEAVE OF ABSENCE

### Qualified practitioner

5. A person who is registered or licensed under an enactment to practice a health profession is a

qualified practitioner for the purposes of Part 5 of the Act and these regulations when providing

a diagnosis that the person is educated, authorized and competent to provide in accordance with

the enactment and applicable regulations.

### Calculation of pay for paid sick leave or bereavement leave

6. An employer shall pay an employee for a day of paid sick leave under subsection 43(3) of the

Act or a day of paid bereavement leave under subsection 52(4) of the Act, in an amount equal

to:

(a) where an employee is paid hourly, the employee’s regular hourly wage multiplied by

the number of hours the employee would have worked if the employee had not

requested the leave of absence;

(b) where an employee is salaried, the employee’s pay for a day of work;

(c) where an employee is paid piecemeal, the employee’s total pay, including vacation

pay, earned in the 30 calendar days before the day of paid leave divided by the number

of days worked during those 30 days.

### Evidence in support of emergency leave

7.(1) Where requested by an employer, an employee shall provide evidence that is reasonable in the

circumstances, as soon as is reasonably practicable, to support the employee’s entitlement to

emergency leave under section 55 of the Act, which may include

(a) a copy of an order, notice or direction issued by a public health authority requiring the

employee to quarantine, isolate or refrain from attending the workplace;

(b) a written communication from a public health authority advising the employee to

quarantine or isolate;

(c) documentation establishing that the employee is subject to travel restrictions or

quarantine requirements issued by a government authority;

(d) documentation confirming that the employee has been advised by an employer or a

public health official not to attend the workplace due to a risk of exposure to a

communicable disease; or

(e) a written statement from the employee describing the circumstances preventing the

employee from performing work for the employer due to an emergency.

### Nature of evidence

(2) Evidence may be provided under this section in paper or electronic form, including copies,

screenshots or electronic communications.

### No confirmation required from qualified practitioner

(3) No employer shall require confirmation from a qualified practitioner that an employee is or

was unable to work due to illness or injury for the purpose of establishing entitlement to

emergency leave.

## ENFORCEMENT

### Administrative penalties

8.(1) For the purpose of clause 72(1)(f) of the Act, the following administrative penalties are

prescribed for contraventions of specified provisions of the Act:

(a) $500 for a contravention of the following provisions of the Act:

(i) subsections 16(1), (2), (3), (4) or (5), 17(1), (2), (3), (4) or (5), 18(2), 19(1),

25(4), (5) or (6), 26(2), (3), (5), (6), (7) or (9), 27(1), (2) or (3), 28(1), 30(1),

(2), (3), (4) or (5), 31(3), 32(3), 33(3), 37(1) or (2) and 39(1),

(ii) sections 23 and 34;

(b) $750 for a contravention of the following provisions of the Act:

(i) subsections 24(1), (3) or (4), 36(1), (3), (4) or (5), 38(1), (6) or (7), 39(2),

41(1), (3), (4) or (5) and 59(1), (4), (5) or (6),

(ii) sections 21 and 22;

(c) $1,000 for a contravention of subsection 61(1) or (2) of the Act;

(d) $1,500 for a contravention of section 92 of the Act.

### Subsequent contravention within three-year period

(2) Where a contravention of the Act set out in subsection (1) occurs within three years of a

previous contravention of the Act by the same employer, an inspector may apply an

administrative penalty that is twice the amount specified for the contravention in subsection

(1).

### Payment of administrative penalty

(3) An administrative penalty is payable to the Minister of Finance and Affordability.

### Interest on amount owed to complainant

9.(1) For the purpose of subsection 80(1) of the Act, the prescribed rate of interest on an amount

owed under an order of an inspector or the Board is as follows:

(a) from the date the complaint is delivered to the chief inspector to the date of the order,

the applicable prejudgment interest rate published in the Gazette pursuant to subsection

56(2) of the Judicature Act R.S.P.E.I. 1988, Cap. J-2.1;

(b) from the date of the order to the date of payment, the applicable post-judgement interest

rate published in the Gazette pursuant to subsection 56(2) of the Judicature Act.

### Interest on amount held in trust

(2) For the purpose of subsection 80(3) of the Act, the prescribed rate of interest on an amount

held in trust is the rate applied by the savings institution in which the amount is deposited.
