PEI Reg EC699/26

*Made under the Interprovincial Trade And Mobility Act*

Pursuant to section 4 of the Interprovincial Trade and Mobility Act R.S.P.E.I. 1988, Cap. I-9.01,

Council made the following regulations:

## PART 1 - DEFINITIONS

### Definitions

1. In these regulations,

(a) “Act” means the Interprovincial Trade and Mobility Act R.S.P.E.I. 1988, Cap. I-9.01;

(b) “Agreement” means the Canadian Free Trade Agreement between the Government of

Canada and the governments of all the provinces and territories of Canada effective on

July 1, 2017, as amended from time to time;

(c) “enactment” means an Act or regulation or a portion of an Act or regulation of the

province;

(d) “measure” has the same meaning as in the Agreement.

## PART 2 - GOODS

### Definition – “regulatory body”

2. In this Part, “regulatory body” means a person or body, whether or not a government entity,

that has been authorized under an enactment to establish or implement measures to regulate

goods in the province.

### Factors respecting approval of goods for use in the province

3.(1) For the purpose of subsection 3(2) of the Act, the following factors apply to the approval of

goods for use or sale in the province.

### Use of good subject to provincial measures, etc.

(2) Where a good from a reciprocating jurisdiction is to be treated as if it has met the standards and

approvals of the province, the good may be used in the province, subject to any provincial

measures related to the manner and circumstances of the use and sale of a good in the province.

### Clarification, approval by reciprocating jurisdiction

(3) For greater certainty, the fact that a good has met the standards and received the approvals of a

reciprocating jurisdiction does not exempt it from the requirement to meet provincial standards

and approvals relating to spot-testing, functioning, installation, maintenance or monitoring of

the good when it is in use in the province.

### Prohibition

(4) A good shall not be treated as approved for use and sale in the province if

(a) the entry, sale, possession or use of the good in the province is prohibited by an

enactment;

(b) the good is subject to measures taken in the province to achieve a legitimate objective

as defined in the Agreement;

(c) the good is subject to a party-specific exception taken by the province or a General

Exception under the Agreement, or under an exception taken by the province under a

mutual recognition agreement between the province and another jurisdiction.

### Standards and approvals of reciprocating jurisdictions

4.(1) Where a good is treated as if it has been manufactured, produced or approved for use in the

province under subsection 3(2) of the Act, a regulatory body may treat the requisite standards

and approvals of the reciprocating jurisdiction as if they are standards and approvals of the

province.

### Relevant information may be requested

(2) For greater certainty, where a good has met the standards and received approval for use in a

reciprocating jurisdiction, a regulatory body may request that the reciprocating jurisdiction

provide any information relevant to understanding and verifying the basis upon which the

standards and approvals for use were granted by the reciprocating jurisdiction, for the purpose

of understanding the basis of their approval.

### Provision of information is not condition

(3) The receipt of the information described in subsection (2) shall not be a condition for treating

the good as if it has met the standards and received approval for use in the province. (

## PART 3 – SERVICES

### Definitions

5.(1) In this Part,

(a) “applicant” means a person or entity applying for an authorization for the provision

of a service in the province under section 3 of the Act, based on an authorization issued

to the person or entity in a reciprocating jurisdiction;

(b) “authorization” in relation to the provision of a service means a certificate, license,

registration or other form of official recognition, including any restrictions or other

limitations on it, issued to the person or entity who provides the service by a regulatory

body of a reciprocating jurisdiction, that attests that the person or entity is qualified or

authorized to provide the service in that jurisdiction;

(c) “equivalent authorization” means the scope of the authorization for an applicant to

provide a service that was issued in a reciprocating jurisdiction has been determined

by a regulatory body to be substantially equivalent to the standards applicable to the

provision of that service in the province with respect to public health, safety and

consumer protection;

(d) “regulatory body” in relation to the provision of a service means a person or other

body, whether or not a government entity, that has been authorized under an enactment

to establish or implement measures to regulate the provision of the service in the

province or, where the context requires, in a reciprocating jurisdiction.

### Application of sections

(2) Subsection 12(2) and section 13 apply in respect of applications for authorization under this

Part.

### Services

6.(1) All services supplied from or approved for use in a reciprocating jurisdiction that have met the

requisite standards and approvals of that jurisdiction shall be treated as if those services were

supplied from or approved for use in the province and shall not be subject to any additional

testing by the province due to the services having been supplied from or approved for use in

another jurisdiction.

### Authorization may be required

(2) Where a person or entity is required under an enactment to obtain an authorization from a

regulatory body in order to provide a service in the province, and the person or entity has

obtained an equivalent authorization to provide the service in a reciprocating jurisdiction, the

person or entity is entitled to the authorization from the regulatory body to provide the service

in the province.

### Enactments apply

(3) For greater certainty, whether or not a person or entity is required to have an authorization to

provide a service in the province pursuant to subsection (2),

(a) the person or entity is subject to any enactment that applies to the provision of the

service in the province; and

(b) in particular, nothing in the Act or these regulations removes any requirement for the

person or entity to register with the Director of Corporations that is imposed under any

provincial enactment respecting the operation of a business in the province, including

but not limited to

(i) the Companies Act R.S.P.E.I 1988, Cap. C-14,

(ii) the Business Corporations Act R.S.P.E.I. 1988, Cap. B-6.01,

(iii) the Extra-provincial Corporations Registration Act R.S.P.E.I 1998, Cap. E-

14,

(iv) the Partnership Act R.S.P.E.I. 1988, Cap. P-1, and

(v) the Limited Partnerships Act R.S.P.E.I. 1988, Cap. L-13.

### Exceptions

(4) A regulatory body is not required to provide an equivalent authorization to a person or entity

of a reciprocating jurisdiction where

(a) the provision of the service is prohibited in the province;

(b) the authorization would normally include substantive eligibility requirements related

to specific projects, events, locations, premises, facilities, sites or geographic areas that

the person or entity does not meet;

(c) the provision of the service is subject to any measures taken by the province to achieve

a legitimate objective as defined in the Agreement; or

(d) the provision of the service is subject to a party-specific exception of the province or a

general exception under the Agreement, or any mutual recognition agreement between

the province and another jurisdiction.

### Information that a regulatory body may require

7.(1) For the purposes of subsection 3(4) of the Act, a regulatory body may require from an applicant

(a) proof of authorization in all Canadian jurisdictions in which the applicant is currently

authorized to provide the service;

(b) any restrictions, limitations, or conditions imposed by the regulatory body of the

reciprocating jurisdiction upon the applicant;

(c) demonstration that the applicant has specific knowledge about the provision of the

service in the province;

(d) evidence respecting the applicant’s standing with the regulatory body of the

reciprocating jurisdiction, including prior complaints, whether criminal or disciplinary,

and insolvency or other proceedings against the applicant;

(e) any additional information reasonably required by the regulatory body to assess the

applicant’s qualifications in relation to the standards governing the provision of the

service in the province;

(f) proof satisfactory to the regulatory body that the applicant holds adequate professional

liability insurance, if required; and

(g) payment of required fees for authorization as set by the regulatory body.

### Requirements respecting legitimate objective

(2) For the purpose of subsection 3(4) of the Act, a regulatory body may impose additional material

requirements necessary to achieve a legitimate objective, as defined in the Agreement, where

a significant gap exists in the applicant’s knowledge or ability to provide the service in the

province.

### Evidence not normally required

8. A regulatory body is not required to request the evidence described in subsection 3(4) of the

Act if it did not require that evidence in its authorization process prior to the coming into force

of the Act.

## PART 4 – MATTERS RELATING TO LABOUR MOBILITY

### Definitions

9. In this Part,

(a) “applicant” means a person applying for an equivalent licence or certification under

section 3 of the Act;

(b) “equivalent licence or certification” means that a licence or certification attests that

the holder is authorized to engage in a scope of practice in the reciprocating jurisdiction

that is circumscribed by occupational or service standards intended to achieve similar

objectives with respect to public health, safety, and consumer protection as the scope

of practice attested to by a licence or certification issued by a regulatory body in the

province;

(c) “licence or certification”, in relation to a person, means a certificate, licence,

registration or other form of official recognition, including any restrictions or other

limitations, issued to the person by a regulatory body of a Canadian jurisdiction that

attests that the person is qualified or authorized to

(i) practise a particular occupation in the Canadian jurisdiction, or

(ii) use in the Canadian jurisdiction a particular occupational title, designation or

abbreviated title or designation;

(d) “person”, in relation to labour mobility, means a natural person and does not include

a corporation or other legal entity, except where these regulations explicitly provide

otherwise;

(e) “regulatory body” means a person or other body, whether or not a government entity,

that has been authorized under an enactment to set or implement measures related to

(i) the establishment of occupational standards or certification requirements,

(ii) the assessment of the qualifications of applicants against established

occupational standards or certification requirements, or

(iii) official recognition that an applicant meets established occupational standards

or certification requirements.

### Information that a regulatory body may require

10.(1) For the purposes of subsection 3(4) of the Act, a regulatory body may require from an applicant

(a) information respecting

(i) all jurisdictions in which the applicant has obtained a licence or certification

from a regulatory body,

(ii) proof of licensing or certification in all Canadian jurisdictions in which the

applicant is currently certified, and

(iii) any restrictions, limitations, or conditions imposed by the regulatory body of

the reciprocating jurisdiction upon the applicant;

(b) the applicant’s name and other administrative information;

(c) the applicant’s consent to the sharing of the applicant’s information with other

regulatory bodies in Canada;

(d) demonstration that the applicant has specific knowledge about the practise of the

profession or occupation in the province;

(e) any additional information reasonably required by the regulatory body to assess the

applicant’s qualifications in relation to the standards of the profession or occupation in

the province;

(f) evidence of good character; and

(g) payment of reasonable processing fees as set by the regulatory body.

### Requirements respecting legitimate objective

(2) For the purpose of subsection 3(4) of the Act, a regulatory body may impose additional material

requirements necessary to achieve a legitimate objective, as defined in the Agreement, where

a significant gap exists in the applicant’s knowledge or ability.

### Evidence not normally required

11. A regulatory body is not required to request the evidence described in subsection 3(4) of the

Act if it did not require that evidence in its licensing or certification process prior to the coming

into force of the Act.

### Determining equivalency

12.(1) For the purposes of assessing the equivalency of a license or certification of an applicant, a

regulatory body may require any or all of the following from a regulatory body of a

reciprocating jurisdiction:

(a) the scope of practice attested to by the licence or certification issued by the

reciprocating jurisdiction, including any restrictions, conditions, terms or other

limitations imposed on it;

(b) the educational requirements for the licence or certification in the reciprocating

jurisdiction;

(c) the experience, continuing educational, or professional currency requirements for the

licence or certification in the reciprocating jurisdiction.

### Authority of regulatory body

(2) The issuance of a licence or certification under subsection 3(5) of the Act does not prevent the

regulatory body from subsequently taking action in accordance with its enabling legislation,

including suspending, amending, or revoking a licence or certification, if it is later determined

that

(a) the applicant provided false, misleading, or materially incomplete information; or

(b) there exists a material difference in scope of practice, restriction, or disciplinary history

that would have affected the regulatory body’s decision had it been known at the time

of issuance.

### Restrictions on licences, certifications

13.(1) For greater certainty, pursuant to subsection 3(4) of the Act, where an applicant’s licence or

certification in the reciprocating jurisdiction is subject to a practice limitation, restriction or

condition, a regulatory body may apply an equivalent practice limitation, restriction or

condition on the applicant’s licence or certification.

### Restriction shall be proportional

(2) Where an authorizing body places a practice limitation, restriction or condition on a licence or

certification due to differences between the scope of practice or provision of a service in the

province and in the reciprocating jurisdiction, the limitation, restriction or condition shall be

proportional to the differences in the occupational and service standards and the underlying

public health, safety, and consumer protection objectives governing the scope of practice or

provision of the service.

### Application of Chapter Seven of the Canadian Free Trade Agreement

14. For greater certainty, an application from an applicant who does not hold a licence or

certification from a reciprocating jurisdiction shall be evaluated in accordance with applicable

laws in the province, including but not limited to the Labour Mobility Act R.S.P.E.I. 1988, Cap.

L-1.1.

## PART 5 – ADOPTION OF SCHEDULE, COMMENCEMENT

### Schedule adopted

15. The Schedule to these regulations is adopted and forms part of these regulations.

### Commencement

16. These regulations come into force on July 11, 2026.

## SCHEDULE

EXEMPT PERSONS

1. The following persons are exempt from the operation of the Act, pursuant to clause 2(2)(c) of

the Act:

(a) a person applying for registration under the Psychologists Act R.S.P.E.I. 1988, Cap. P-

27.2;

(b) a person applying to be registered and licensed as a denturist under the Denturists Act

R.S.P.E.I. 1988, Cap. D-6.1;

(c) a person applying to be registered and licensed as a chiropractor under the Chiropractic

Act R.S.P.E.I. 1988, Cap. C-7.1.
