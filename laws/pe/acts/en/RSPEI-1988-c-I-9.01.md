RSPEI 1988, c I-9.01

BE IT ENACTED by the Lieutenant Governor and the Legislative Assembly of the Province of Prince

Edward Island as follows:

### Purpose

1. The purpose of this Act is to remove barriers to trade in goods, services and investment between

the province and other jurisdictions within Canada.

### Act prevails

2.(1) Subject to this section and the regulations, in the event of a conflict between this Act and

another enactment, this Act prevails.

### Exceptions

(2) Notwithstanding subsection (1), this Act does not apply to a person seeking to practise in, or a

regulatory authority of,

(a) a regulated health profession as defined in the Regulated Health Professions Act

R.S.P.E.I. 1988, Cap. R-10.1, or a registered health profession as defined in the

Registered Health Professions Act R.S.P.E.I. 1988, Cap. R-8.01;

(b) a member of the Law Society of Prince Edward Island under the Legal Profession Act

R.S.P.E.I. 1988, Cap. L-6.1; or

(c) a profession or occupation specified in the regulations.

### Definition

3.(1) In this Act, “reciprocating jurisdiction” means a signatory to the Canadian Free Trade

Agreement, other than the province, that, as determined by the Lieutenant Governor in Council,

has in force legislation similar to and corresponding with this Act.

### Goods from reciprocating jurisdiction

(2) Goods manufactured, produced or approved for use in a reciprocating jurisdiction that have

met the requisite standards and approvals of that jurisdiction shall be treated as if those goods

were manufactured, produced or approved for use in the province and shall not be subject to

any additional fees or testing by the province due to the goods having been manufactured,

produced or approved for use in another jurisdiction.

### Persons licensed or certified in reciprocating jurisdiction

(3) Subject to subsection (4), where a person is required under an Act or regulation to obtain a

licence or certification from a regulatory body of the province, the person is entitled to be issued

that licence or certification if the person holds an equivalent licence or certification issued by

a regulatory body of a reciprocating jurisdiction and the regulatory body of the province is

satisfied that the requirements of subsection (4) have been met.

### Requirements for licensing or certification

(4) A regulatory body shall, before issuing a licence or certification to a person entitled under

subsection (3) to receive the licence or certification, require

(a) evidence satisfactory to the regulatory body that

(i) the person is in good standing with the relevant regulatory body of the

reciprocating jurisdiction and any other jurisdiction where the person is

licensed or certified,

(ii) there are no outstanding complaints against the person in the reciprocating

jurisdiction or any other jurisdiction where the person is licensed or certified,

(iii) there are no restrictions on practise imposed on the person in the reciprocating

jurisdiction or any other jurisdiction where the person is licensed or certified,

(iv) the person’s disciplinary history in the reciprocating jurisdiction or any other

jurisdiction where the person is licensed or certified discloses no incidents that

would disqualify the person from being licensed or certified by the regulatory

body,

(v) the person has provided a satisfactory criminal sector or vulnerable sector

check, or both, if required by the regulatory body, and

(vi) the person holds professional liability insurance, if required, satisfactory to the

regulatory body; and

(b) any other evidence specified in the regulations that is necessary to assess the

application.

### Issuance within 10 business days

(5) Where a person entitled under subsection (3) to receive a licence or certification applies to a

regulatory body of the province for the licence or certification, the regulatory body shall issue

the licence or certification to the person within 10 business days after

(a) receipt of the evidence from the reciprocating jurisdiction or other jurisdiction

specified in subsection (4); and

(b) receipt of the completed application by the person, together with the applicable fee.

### Regulations

4. The Lieutenant Governor in Council may make regulations

(a) specifying professions, occupations and Acts to which subsection 2(1) does not apply;

(b) establishing criteria for reciprocity for the purpose of subsection 3(2);

(c) respecting the evidence that a regulatory body may require under subsection 3(4);

(d) defining any term used but not defined in this Act;

(e) respecting any matter or thing the Lieutenant Governor in Council considers necessary

or advisable to carry out the intent or purpose of this Act.

### Protection from liability

5.(1) Subject to subsection (2), no cause of action and no legal proceeding lies or may be brought or

continued against Government, a regulatory body or another person to enforce or determine a

right or obligation that is claimed or arises solely under or by virtue of this Act, unless

Government, the regulatory body or the person has not acted in good faith.

### Exception

(2) Subsection (1) does not apply to a proceeding that is provided for under Chapter Ten of the

Canadian Free Trade Agreement.

### Commencement

6. This Act comes into force on a date that may be fixed by proclamation of the Lieutenant

Governor in Council.
