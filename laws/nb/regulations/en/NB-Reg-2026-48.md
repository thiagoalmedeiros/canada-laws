NEW BRUNSWICK REGULATION 2026-48

under the Local Governance Act (O.C. 2026-188)

Filed September 15, 2026

Under subsection 191(1) of the Local Governance Act, the Lieutenant-Governor in Council makes the following Regulation:

### Citation

1 This Regulation may be cited as the Local Rate Stabilizer Regulation – Local Governance Act.

### Definitions

2 The following definitions apply in this Regulation.

“Act” means the Local Governance Act.(Loi)

“current year” means the year for which a local government rate stabilizer is to be determined for a local government, a rural community or a regional municipality or for which a rural district rate stabilizer is to be determined for a rural district.(année en cours)

“heavy industrial property” means heavy industrial property as defined in the Assessment Act.(biens industriels lourds)

“local government cost index” means local government cost index as defined in the Community Funding Act. (indice des coûts des gouvernements locaux)

“local government sub-unit” means an area or a portion of an area of a local government for which the local government fixes a rate under subsection 99(9) or (10) of the Act.(sous-unité d’un gouvernement local)

“local government warrant” means the amount referred to in paragraph 99(2)(b) of the Act to be raised on a local government tax base. (mandat d’un gouvernement local)

“new construction” means, with respect to real property, the construction of a building or structure or the renovation of an existing building or structure that is undertaken after the date the relevant local government tax base or rural district tax base is computed for the previous year that (nouvelle construction)

(a) causes a structural alteration, addition or improvement to the real property, or

(b) causes a change in use of the real property.

“non-residential property” means non-residential property as defined in the Assessment Act but does not include heavy industrial property. (biens non résidentiels)

“previous year” means the year before the year for which a local government rate stabilizer is to be determined for a local government, a rural community or a regional municipality or for which a rural district rate stabilizer is to be determined for a rural district. (année précédente)

“regional municipality warrant” means the amount referred to in paragraph 110(1)(b) of the Act to be raised on a regional municipality tax base. (mandat d’une municipalité régionale)

“residential property” means residential property as defined in the Assessment Act. (biens résidentiels)

“rural community or regional municipality sub-unit” means an area or a portion of an area of a rural community or regional municipality for which the Minister fixes a rate under subsection 109(2) of the Act. (sous-unité d’une communauté rurale ou d’une municipalité régionale)

“rural community warrant” means the amount referred to in paragraph 110(1)(b) of the Act to be raised on a rural community tax base. (mandat d’une communauté rurale)

“rural district sub-unit” means an area or a portion of an area of a rural district for which the Minister fixes a rate under subsection 176.71(2) of the Act. (sous-unité d’un district rural)

“rural district warrant” means the amount referred to in paragraph 176.8(1)(b) of the Act to be raised on a rural district tax base.(mandat d’un district rural)

### Revenue growth components of a local government

3 The revenue growth components of a local government referred to in paragraph 99.01(1)(a) of the Act are

(a) the revenue growth components for the residential property located within the local government,

(b) the revenue growth components for the non-residential property located within the local government, and

(c) the revenue growth components for the heavy industrial property located within the local government.

### Determination of revenue growth components – local governments

A × B ÷ 100

1 where

1 B is the tax rate component referred to in paragraph 9(a).

E × F ÷ 100

3 where

3 F is the tax rate component referred to in paragraph 9(c).

### Determination of tax rate components for residential property located within a local government

10(1) The tax rate components referred to in paragraph 9(a) are determined for each local government sub-unit by the formula

(A + 1) × B

where

A is the coefficient applied to the tax rates fixed under subparagraph 5(2)(a)(i) or (a.1)(i) of the Real Property Tax Act, as the case may be, for the previous year for the residential property located within the local government; and

B is the tax rate fixed under subparagraph 5(2)(a)(i) or (a.1)(i) of the Real Property Tax Act, as the case may be, for the previous year for the residential property located within the local government sub-unit.

10(2) For the purpose of subsection (1), variable “A” is determined by the formula

(C – D) ÷ D

where

C is the weighted average of the tax rates fixed under subparagraph 5(2)(a)(i) or (a.1)(i) of the Real Property Tax Act, as the case may be, for the previous year for the residential property located within the local government, adjusted for growth; and

D is the weighted average of the tax rates fixed under subparagraph 5(2)(a)(i) or (a.1)(i) of the Real Property Tax Act, as the case may be, for the previous year for the residential property located within the local government.

10(3) For the purpose of subsection (2), variable “C” is determined by the formula

E ÷ F × 100

where

E is the portion of the local government warrant related to residential property, adjusted for growth; and

F is the portion of the municipal tax base for the current year related to residential property, excluding the assessed value of new construction related to residential property.

10(4) For the purpose of subsection (2), variable “D” is determined by the formula

G ÷ H × 100

where

G is the portion of the local government warrant for the previous year related to residential property; and

H is the portion of the municipal tax base for the previous year related to residential property.

10(5) Subject to subsection (6), for the purpose of subsection (3), variable “E” is determined by the formula

I × (1 + J)

where

I is the portion of the local government warrant for the previous year related to residential property; and

J is the local government revenue growth index.

10(6) For the purpose of subsection (5), variable “J”

(a) is the lesser of

(i) the local government cost index, and

(ii) the assessment growth rate from the previous year to the current year for the property liable to taxation under the Assessment Act located within the local government, or

(b) is deemed to have a value of zero if the index or rate referred to in subparagraph (a)(i) or (ii), as the case may be, has a value of less than zero.

### Time for determining rate stabilizers and publication

24(1) For the purpose of subsection 99.01(1) of the Act, on or before October 15, the Minister shall determine the revenue growth components of each local government in accordance with section 4 and the tax rate components of each local government in accordance with sections 10 to 13.

24(2) For the purpose of subsection 110(2) of the Act, on or before October 15, the Minister shall determine the revenue growth components of each rural community or regional municipality in accordance with section 6 and the tax rate components of each rural community or regional municipality in accordance with sections 15 to 18.

24(3) For the purpose of subsection 176.8(3) of the Act, on or before October 15, the Minister shall determine the revenue growth components of each rural district in accordance with section 8 and the tax rate components of each rural district in accordance with sections 20 to 23.

24(4) The Minister shall publish, on the same date, the revenue growth components and the tax rate components of a local government rate stabilizer or of a rural district rate stabilizer on the Government of New Brunswick’s website.

### Contents of explanations by local governments and their publication

25(1) For the purposes of subsections 99.01(3) and (4) of the Act, an explanation shall include, by budget category, any factors contributing to a need for additional revenue, including

(a) types of services,

(b) changes in service levels,

(c) acquisition or construction of new assets, and

(d) changes or reductions in revenues.

25(2) If a local government is required to provide an explanation referred to in subsection (1), the local government shall publish it, in addition to the form referred to in subsection 99(2) of the Act, within 14 days after the form is approved by the Minister.

25(3) The explanation and form referred to in subsection (2) shall be published on the local government’s website for a period of at least one year.

25(4) A local government may also publish the explanation and form referred to in subsection (2) on a social media platform.
