*Enabling act: Public Health Act*

### Definitions

1 In this Regulation,

(a) “arboviral infections” means diseases caused by arthropod-borne viruses (arboviruses), which are transmitted to humans through the bites of infected arthropods like mosquitoes, ticks or sandflies, including, but not limited to,

(i) chikungunya virus,

(ii) dengue virus,

(iii) eastern equine encephalitis virus,

(iv) flavivirus (other),

(v) Jamestown Canyon virus,

(vi) Japanese encephalitis virus,

(vii) Powassan encephalitis virus,

(viii) St. Louis encephalitis virus,

(ix) snowshoe hare virus,

(x) West Nile virus,

(xi) western equine encephalitis virus,

(xii) yellow fever virus, and

(xiii) Zika virus;

(b) “case” means a person who has a communicable disease as determined by clinical, laboratory or epidemiological criteria;

(c) “contact” means a person suspected to have been in association with a case, infected animal or contaminated environment to a degree sufficient to become infected;

(d) “health care worker” means

(i) a health practitioner, or

(ii) a person who, because of employment, study or volunteer activities, is at risk of exposure to or transmission of a communicable disease in an acute care setting, a continuing care setting, a community care setting or another clinical or diagnostic location;

(e) “monitor” means the supervision, as determined by a medical officer of health, of contacts in order to promote prompt recognition of infection with or illness from a communicable disease;

(f) “notifiable disease” means

(i) a communicable disease listed in Schedule 1, or

(ii) a disease, illness or health condition referred to in section 26 of the Act;

(g) “outbreak” means a distribution of cases that is unusual in terms of time, place or persons affected;

(h) “rare or emerging communicable disease” means a communicable disease that the Chief Medical Officer identifies, in accordance with section 14, as requiring special public health management due to the disease’s novelty, severity or epidemiological characteristics;

(i) “sensitive situations or occupations” means work or attendance in environments where persons may transmit communicable diseases through direct contact with food, patients or vulnerable populations, including food handling, health care and child care settings;

(j) “susceptible” means to be at risk of infection because the person has not developed protective immunity through appropriate immunization or previous infection, or both;

(k) “viral haemorrhagic fevers” means a group of severe, often life-threatening illnesses caused by viruses that can lead to fever, bleeding and multi-organ failure, typically transmitted through contact with infected animals, vectors or bodily fluids, including, but not limited to,

(i) Argentine haemorrhagic fever,

(ii) Crimean-Congo haemorrhagic fever,

(iii) Ebola virus disease,

(iv) Lassa fever,

(v) Marburg virus disease, and

(vi) Rift Valley fever;

(l) “vulnerable” means to be at increased risk of severe disease or complications of a disease due to social, behavioural or medical reasons;

(m) “zoonotic disease” means a notifiable disease that is transmitted directly or indirectly from an infected animal.

### Provision of health services, etc. by Minister

2 (1) The Minister may

(a) provide health promotional, preventive, diagnostic, treatment, rehabilitative or palliative services, supplies, equipment and care and any drugs, medicines and biological agents for the prevention, treatment or modification of communicable diseases, and

(b) with respect to clause (a), determine

(i) the persons eligible to receive the services, things or care,

(ii) the persons who may administer the services, things or care,

(iii) the conditions under which the services, things or care may be provided and administered, and

(iv) the methods and protocols respecting the distribution, storage and handling of the services, things and care, as applicable.

(2) A person must not impose any charge in respect of the supply or administration of a service, thing or care provided under this section unless authorized by the Minister.

### Provision of health services, etc. by provincial health agencies or corporations

3 (1) A provincial health agency or provincial health corporation must, as directed by the Minister, provide the following for the prevention, treatment or modification of communicable diseases:

(a) health promotional, preventive, diagnostic, treatment, rehabilitative or palliative services, supplies, equipment and care;

(b) any drugs, medicines and biological agents as provided under section 2(1)(a).

(2) A provincial health agency or provincial health corporation must, with respect to the provision of the services, things and care referred to in subsection (1),

(a) implement distribution, storage and handling methods, as applicable, and protocols as directed by the Minister,

(b) provide data, records or reports at the times and in the form and manner required by the Minister,

(c) create and maintain the data and records required by the Minister, and

(d) oversee the health and safety of persons to whom the services, things or care are provided, as directed by the Minister.

(3) A provincial health agency or provincial health corporation must ensure that its employees and persons who provide or administer the services, things and care referred to in subsection (1) are trained to do so in accordance with any requirements established by the Minister.

(4) A provincial health agency or provincial health corporation must not, unless authorized to do so by the Minister, charge for the services, things or care referred to in subsection (1) that are provided by

(a) the provincial health agency or provincial health corporation, its employees or other persons acting under this section, or

(b) the Minister under section 2(1)(a).

### Disputes

4 If there is a dispute as to the diagnosis of a disease in respect of which action may be taken under section 29 of the Act, the medical officer of health’s decision about the diagnosis of the disease is final, subject only to a review by the Chief Medical Officer.

### Prescribed communicable diseases under Act

5 (1) The diseases set out in Schedule 1 are the communicable diseases prescribed for the purposes of sections 20(1) and (2), 22(1) and (3) and 23 of the Act.

(2) The diseases set out in Schedule 3 are the communicable diseases prescribed for the purposes of sections 39(1), 44(1) and 47(1) of the Act.

### Provision of assistance by community health nurses and executive officers

6 A medical officer of health may, in exercising their powers and carrying out their duties under the Act and this Regulation, use the assistance of community health nurses and executive officers.

### Investigation by medical officer of health

7 A medical officer of health must, in accordance with Schedule 2,

(a) investigate occurrences of notifiable diseases, including, where applicable,

(i) determining the source of infection,

(ii) identifying the persons who have been exposed to the source, and

(iii) identifying contacts of the case,

and

(b) carry out special measures, as applicable.

### Medical officer of health may offer testing

8 A medical officer of health may offer testing to a person if the medical officer of health considers the testing necessary for the purposes of communicable disease investigation, prevention, control or management to determine whether the person has

(a) a communicable disease, or

(b) immunity from a communicable disease.

### Medical officer of health may offer prophylaxis

9 A medical officer of health may offer any eligible person exposed to a communicable disease an appropriate prophylaxis, as the medical officer of health considers necessary.

### Notification of parents or guardians by child care facility operators

10 If a medical officer of health receives notification of a communicable disease under section 22(1) of the Act from the operator of a child care facility, the medical officer of health may, by order, require the operator to notify the parents or guardians of other children attending the facility that their children may have been exposed to the communicable disease.

### Provision of advice and assistance to child care facility operators

11 Under the direction of a medical officer of health, a provincial health agency or provincial health corporation may offer advice and assistance to child care facilities to minimize the spread of communicable diseases to

(a) children attending that facility,

(b) staff caring for the children, or

(c) family or similar contacts of the children.

### Measures relating to outbreaks

12 If a medical officer of health confirms the existence of a communicable disease outbreak, the medical officer of health must attempt to

(a) determine the source of the outbreak,

(b) identify persons who are at risk of infection or who have been exposed to the source of the outbreak, and

(c) implement control measures to protect the public.

### Notification to blood procurement agencies

13 A medical officer of health must notify blood procurement agencies within 48 hours of the identification of a notifiable disease that may be transmitted through blood in a person with a history of donating or receiving blood or blood products in Canada.

### Notice regarding rare or emerging communicable diseases

14 The Chief Medical Officer must, by notice, set out which communicable diseases are rare or emerging communicable diseases for the purposes of this Regulation.

### Foodborne and waterborne diseases

15 (1) When a medical officer of health reasonably suspects or identifies a foodborne or waterborne disease, the medical officer of health may, by order, require any or all of the following:

(a) the abatement or elimination of the source of contamination;

(b) modifications to food handling or water treatment;

(c) the boiling of water before its use for human consumption;

(d) the cessation of distribution of implicated foods or the recall or destruction of implicated foods;

(e) the use of alternate sources of food or water;

(f) the doing or refraining from doing of any other things that, in the opinion of the medical officer of health, may assist in preventing others from becoming infected.

(2) The medical officer of health may attempt to obtain the following samples and submit the samples to a medical diagnostic laboratory for examination:

(a) samples of food or water that are or may be contaminated;

(b) samples of feces and vomitus from persons known or suspected to be infected.

(3) The medical officer of health may

(a) order that any suspected contaminated food must be held in secure storage facilities to prevent consumption pending the results of the laboratory examination referred to in subsection (2), and

(b) order the destruction of any food that laboratory examination has proven to be contaminated.

### Zoonotic diseases

16 (1) When a medical officer of health reasonably suspects or identifies a zoonotic disease in a person, the medical officer of health may, subject to subsection (2), direct that one or more of the following measures be taken with respect to animals, animal products or animal environments:

(a) investigation;

(b) observation;

(c) testing;

(d) isolation;

(e) quarantine;

(f) treatment;

(g) disinfection;

(h) destruction;

(i) sale restriction.

(2) The medical officer of health must consult the Chief Provincial Veterinarian or designate before providing a direction under subsection (1) to test, isolate, quarantine, treat or destroy a live domestic animal or to restrict the sale of a live domestic animal.

(3) For the purpose of subsection (1), the medical officer of health may have an animal secured alive and uninjured and cause it to be confined in a secure place at the owner’s expense until the resolution of an investigation.

### Repeal

17 The Communicable Diseases Regulation (AR 238/85) is repealed.

### Expiry

18 For the purpose of ensuring that this Regulation is reviewed for ongoing relevancy and necessity, with the option that it may be repassed in its present or an amended form following a review, this Regulation expires on September 30, 2036.

### Coming into force

19 This Regulation comes into force on September 1, 2026.
