# Preventative Health Testing Services Regulation

Alta Reg 193/2026

*Enabling act: Health Professions Act*

### Definitions

1 In this Regulation,

(a) “clinical finding of cancer” means a clinical finding of cancer as defined in the Preventative Health Testing Services Benefits Regulation ;

(b) “testing facility” means a testing facility as defined in section 4 of the Preventative Health Testing Services Benefits Regulation .

### Eligibility requirements re self-referrals

2 (1) For the purposes of section 1(1)(m.01) of the Act, an individual is eligible for a self-referral if the following requirements are met:

(a) the individual is

(i) 18 years of age or older, or

(ii) under 18 years of age and the individual’s guardian, as determined in accordance with the Family Law Act or Child, Youth and Family Enhancement Act , has provided consent for the individual to obtain the preventative health testing service;

(b) the individual has been assessed by a regulated member and the regulated member determines that it is safe and clinically appropriate for the regulated member to provide the individual with the preventative health testing service based on the factors set out in subsection (2);

(c) the individual or a person referred to in section 5(4)(b) or (c) provides written consent for the disclosure of the individual’s health information in accordance with section 5.

(2) In determining whether it is safe and clinically appropriate for the regulated member to provide the preventative health testing service to an individual, the regulated member must consider the following:

(a) the individual’s health history;

(b) any risk factors relating to the individual’s health;

(c) contraindications;

(d) whether, in the opinion of the regulated member, the individual has an informed understanding of the preventative health testing service the individual is seeking to obtain, including any limitations, side effects and risks relating to the provision of the preventative health testing service.

(3) Despite the requirements in subsection (1) being met, an individual is not an eligible individual if the self-referral would contravene a condition, restriction or limitation under the Act or the regulations made under the Act that apply to the provision of the preventative health testing service the individual is seeking to obtain.

### Conditions, restrictions and limitations re preventative health testing services

3 (1) A regulated member may provide a preventative health testing service to an eligible individual only if the service is provided in accordance with any applicable standards of practice or code of ethics established by the college of the regulated member.

(2) A regulated member must not provide a preventative health testing service if the regulated member determines that the preventative health testing service would be duplicative or clinically inappropriate in accordance with any applicable standards of practice.

(3) Before providing a preventative health testing service, a regulated member must assess the individual seeking to obtain the preventative health testing service and determine whether the individual is eligible to receive the preventative health testing service under section 2.

### Requirement to inform

4 (1) In this section, “insured health services” means insured health services as defined in the Alberta Health Care Insurance Act .

(2) Before providing a preventative health testing service, a regulated member must, in accordance with any applicable standards of practice or code of ethics established by the college of the regulated member, inform an individual seeking to obtain the preventative health testing service of the following:

(a) the nature and purpose of the preventative health testing service and whether the service requested will address the individual’s reason for self-referral;

(b) any foreseeable limitations, side effects and risks relating to the provision of the preventative health testing service;

(c) that the preventative health testing service is not an insured health service and the individual is responsible for paying the costs associated with obtaining the preventative health testing service;

(d) that the costs referred to in clause (c) are not guaranteed to be reimbursed by the Government of Alberta.

### Consent to disclose information

5 (1) In this section,

(a) “insurer” means an insurer as defined in section 26 of the Alberta Health Care Insurance Act ;

(b) “operator” means a person who is responsible for the management, control or operation of a testing facility.

(2) A regulated member must provide a preventative health testing service to an individual only if the individual or a person referred to in subsection (4)(b) or (c) provides written consent to the disclosure of the individual’s information, including personal information as defined in the Protection of Privacy Act and health information as defined in the Health Information Act .

(3) For the purposes of subsection (2), the written consent must include the following:

(a) an authorization for the regulated member or operator to disclose the individual’s information specified in the consent, including the following:

(i) the individual’s name;

(ii) the individual’s date of birth;

(iii) the individual’s contact information, including the individual’s phone number, mailing address and email address, if any;

(iv) the individual’s valid personal health number;

(v) the type of preventative health testing service provided to the individual;

(vi) the diagnostic testing results identified as the result of the preventative health testing service and any clinical finding of cancer;

(vii) the date the preventative health testing service was provided;

(viii) the individual’s insurer, if any;

(ix) the individual’s invoice or record of payment for the preventative health testing service;

(b) the purpose for which the information may be disclosed;

(c) the identity of the persons to whom the information may be disclosed, including

(i) the individual,

(ii) a person referred to in subsection (4)(b) or (c) who is providing consent on behalf of the individual,

(iii) any regulated member that is responsible for the resident’s follow-up care after a preventative health testing service is provided to the individual, and

(iv) the Minister;

(d) an acknowledgment that the individual or the person referred to in subsection (4)(b) or (c) who is providing the consent on the individual’s behalf has been made aware of

(i) the reasons for collecting the information, and

(ii) the risks and benefits to the individual if consent is provided or refused;

(e) the date the consent is effective and the date, if any, the consent expires;

(f) a statement that the consent may be revoked at any time by the individual or the person referred to in subsection (4)(b) or (c) who is providing consent on the individual’s behalf.

(4) For the purposes of this section, consent may be provided

(a) subject to clause (c), if the individual is 18 years of age or older, by the individual,

(b) if the individual is under 18 years of age, by the individual’s guardian, as determined in accordance with the Family Law Act or Child, Youth and Family Enhancement Act , or

(c) if the individual is 18 years of age or older, but does not have capacity as defined in the Adult Guardianship and Trusteeship Act to provide consent, by

(i) the individual’s guardian as defined in the Adult Guardianship and Trusteeship Act , or

(ii) a person authorized under another enactment of Alberta or another jurisdiction to provide consent on behalf of the individual.

(5) A regulated member may only disclose information related to an individual in accordance with the terms of the consent provided by the individual under this section.

### Obligations re diagnostic testing results

6 (1) In this section, “authorized person” means a person that is authorized in accordance with the applicable standards of practice to act on behalf of a regulated member at a testing facility.

(2) If a regulated member provides a preventative health testing service to an eligible individual that is a computed tomography, magnetic imaging, ultrasound imaging or x-ray service, the regulated member must communicate the diagnostic testing results as a result of the service to the eligible individual

(a) in-person,

(b) at the testing facility, and

(c) within 2 hours after the preventative health testing service is provided to the eligible individual.

(3) If a regulated member provides a preventative health testing service to an eligible individual other than a computed tomography, magnetic imaging, ultrasound imaging or x-ray service, the regulated member must communicate the diagnostic testing results as a result of the service to the eligible individual in accordance with the applicable standards of practice.

(4) If there are diagnostic testing results identified by the regulated member as the result of a preventative health testing service and the regulated member determines based on the results and in accordance with the applicable standards of practice that follow-up care or additional clinical assessments are required, the regulated member or an authorized person must, in accordance with the applicable standards of practice,

(a) provide the eligible individual with information respecting the eligible individual’s follow-up care after the preventative health testing service is provided, and

(b) facilitate consultations with other regulated members and referrals for health services with other regulated members that are related to the eligible individual’s follow-up care and continuity of care.

### Expiry

7 For the purposes of ensuring that this Regulation is reviewed for ongoing relevancy and necessity, with the option that it may be repassed in its present or an amended form following a review, this Regulation expires on May 31, 2031.

### Coming into force

8 This Regulation comes into force on the coming into force of section 6(4) and (5) of the Health Statutes Amendment Act, 2026 .
