Her Excellency the Governor General in Council, on the recommendation of the Minister of the Environment, under subsections 27(1.1) and (1.2) of the [Species at Risk Act](../../acts/en/S-15.3.md)[^fn_81000-2-4632_hq_29687],

**(a)** decides not to add the Striped Bass (Morone saxatilis) Southern Gulf of St. Lawrence population to the List of Wildlife Species at Risk set out in Schedule 1 to that Act; and

**(b)** approves that the Minister of the Environment include in the public registry established under section 120 of that Act the statement that is attached as the annex to this Order and that sets out the reasons for the decision not to add that species to that List.

## ANNEX — Statement Setting Out the Reasons for the Decision Not to Add the Striped Bass Southern Gulf of St. Lawrence Population to the List of Wildlife Species at Risk

**Striped Bass (Morone saxatilis) Southern Gulf of St. Lawrence Population**

The Minister of the Environment has recommended, on the advice of the Minister of Fisheries and Oceans, that the Striped Bass Southern Gulf of St. Lawrence population not be added to the List of Wildlife Species at Risk (the “List”) set out in Schedule 1 to the [Species at Risk Act](../../acts/en/S-15.3.md) (“SARA”).

Scientific advice provided through the Committee on the Status of Endangered Wildlife in Canada’s (“COSEWIC”) 2012 Assessment and Status Report for Striped Bass indicated that the main threats to the species were linked to fishing activities, i.e., bycatch and illegal fishing, both of which are being addressed. The current management regime, described below, has promoted an improvement of the population abundance. Furthermore, the addition of this species to the List would not have results that are significantly different from the results of the current management regime or enhance protection measures to manage the main threats faced by the species. The non-listing option received support from the majority of the provinces, Indigenous organizations and potentially impacted stakeholders who responded during consultations.

Conservation management measures implemented since the mid-1990’s, including the closure of fisheries for 13 years, have resulted in population gains and common occurrence of the species throughout this population’s range. The population has been estimated at 547,300 spawners in 2025. This is a significant increase in abundance from the low indices of the late 1990s. This population of Striped Bass is currently distributed widely and abundantly throughout its historical range from the Gaspé Peninsula to Cape Breton Island.

Since the COSEWIC assessment and status report were published in 2012, evidence gathered by the Department of Fisheries and Oceans (“DFO”) indicates that the Striped Bass Southern Gulf of St. Lawrence population has sustained higher spawner abundance. Conservation management measures implemented since the mid-1990s, including the closure of the commercial fishery in 1996 and the 13-year closure of food, social and ceremonial and recreational fisheries, have resulted in consistent increases in abundance over the last three generations. The population is assessed annually and is currently in the cautious zone as identified in the DFO’s Precautionary Approach Framework.

Not listing is recommended because listing under SARA would not significantly result in different or enhanced protection measures for this population of Striped Bass. Although listing would incur minimal cost to Canadians, it would create an undue administrative burden for its management. Unlike other populations, where a SARA management plan would provide the opportunity for addressing other threats, in this case it would simply require the implementation of the current fisheries management plan, updated yearly.

With a decision not to list this species under SARA, DFO will continue to monitor and manage the population under the [Fisheries Act](../../acts/en/F-14.md) to maintain the population and protect the unique spawning area, while managing a sustainable fishery incorporating the principles of the Precautionary Approach.
