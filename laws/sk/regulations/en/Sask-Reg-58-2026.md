Sask Reg 58/2026

*Made under The Heritage Recognition (From Many Peoples, Strength) Act*

### Title

1 These regulations may be cited as The Heritage Recognition (Ukrainian Heritage

Month) Regulations.

### Designation

2 The month of September is designated as Ukrainian Heritage Month.

### Recognition

3 The designation of Ukrainian Heritage Month is in recognition of the following:

(a) the first official Ukrainian immigrants, Wasyl Eleniak and Ivan Pylypiw,

arrived in Canada on September 7, 1891;

(b) the first permanent Ukrainian settlements in what is now

Saskatchewan were in the areas of Montmartre-Candiac (1895-96) and

Grenfell-Yorkton (1896-97);

(c) over the decades, tens of thousands of Ukrainian immigrants have arrived

and settled in Saskatchewan;

(d) at the start of the 20th Century, many Ukrainian immigrants were

unjustly labelled enemy aliens and imprisoned during Canada’s first national

internment operations of 1914-1920, following the implementation of the War

Measures Act (Canada);

(e) generations of Ukrainian immigrants have been able to find refuge and

opportunity in Saskatchewan despite having experienced oppression and war

throughout their history at the hands of various foreign aggressors;

(f) in 2008, Saskatchewan, by way of The Ukrainian Famine and Genocide

(Holodomor) Memorial Day Act, recognized the genocide by the man-made

famine that killed millions in Ukraine from 1932 to 1933 under the Soviet

Communist regime of Joseph Stalin;

(g) on August 24, 1991, the Parliament of Ukraine declared Ukraine as an

independent democratic state from the Soviet Union, and Canada was the first

nation in the Western world to recognize Ukraine’s independence;

(h) Saskatchewan and Ukraine share a long history of friendship and cultural

and educational exchange as well as a commitment to the universal values of

human rights, democracy and respect for international law;

(i) many Saskatchewan residents trace all or part of their ancestry to Ukraine

and Ukraine is one of the major sources of immigration to Saskatchewan;

(j) the contributions of Saskatchewan Ukrainians to the province’s cultural

identity, architecture, arts and education, as well as its economic, linguistic,

political and social fabric;

(k) the contributions of Ukrainians in the development of Saskatchewan and

Canada’s diverse and multicultural society.

### Coming into force

4(1) Subject to subsection (2), these regulations come into force on the day

on which section 1 of The Heritage Recognition (From Many Peoples, Strength)

Act comes into force.

(2) If these regulations are filed with the Registrar of Regulations after the day

on which section 1 of The Heritage Recognition (From Many Peoples, Strength)

Act comes into force, these regulations come into force on the day on which they

are filed with the Registrar of Regulations.
