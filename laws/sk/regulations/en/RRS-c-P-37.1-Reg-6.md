RRS c P-37.1 Reg 6

*Made under The Public Health Act, 1994*

### Title

1 These regulations may be cited as The Public Health Officers Regulations.

### Interpretation

2 In these regulations:

(a) “Act” means The Public Health Act, 1994;

(b) “board of certification” means the Board of Certification of Public

Health Inspectors of the Canadian Institute of Public Health Inspectors;

(c) “medical health officer” means a person who is registered as a member

of the College of Physicians and Surgeons of the Province of Saskatchewan

and who possesses:

(i) a certificate or fellowship in community medicine from the Royal

College of Physicians and Surgeons of Canada;

(ii) a master’s degree in public health or community health; or

(iii) an equivalent combination of experience and qualifications that is

acceptable to the minister;

(d) “public health inspector” means a person who holds:

(i) a Certificate in Public Health Inspection (Canada); or

(ii) a certificate that, in the opinion of the board of certification, is

equivalent to the certificate mentioned in subclause (i).

### Classes prescribed as public health officers

3 For the purposes of subclause 2(gg)(ii) of the Act, the following classes of persons

are prescribed as public health officers:

(a) medical health officers;

(b) public health inspectors;

(c) registered nurses who are working under the direction of a designated

public health officer.

### Certification of public health officers

4(1) For the purposes of section 9 of the Act, a person who is not a member of one

of the classes set out in section 3 may apply to the minister requesting that he or

she be certified as a public health officer.

(2) An application must be in the form required by the minister and must include:

(a) information and supporting materials setting out the person’s educational

background, qualifications and experience with respect to environmental

health, control of communicable diseases and abatement of health hazards;

(b) a statement in writing from a local authority indicating that the local

authority intends to delegate specified powers of the local authority to the

person if certification is granted;

(c) information indicating the duties that the local authority intends the

person to perform as a public health officer; and

(d) any other information or material that the minister considers relevant

to the application.

(3) On receipt of an application, the minister shall consider the application and:

(a) certify the person as a person who is qualified to act as a public health

officer if the minister is satisfied with the education, qualifications and

experience of the person; or

(b) refuse to certify the person.

(4) The minister shall provide reasons to a person whose application is refused.

### Qualifications of designated public health officers

5 For the purposes of clause 11(2)(b) of the Act, the qualifications of a medical health

officer are prescribed as the qualifications required for a person to be designated as

a designated public health officer.

### Consequential amendment

6 Clause 2(2)(a) of The Communicable Disease Control Regulations is

amended by striking out “The Designation and Certification of Public Health

Officers Regulations” and substituting “The Public Health Officers Regulations”.

### Coming into force

7 These regulations come into force on the day on which section 46 of The Public

Health Act, 1994 comes into force.
