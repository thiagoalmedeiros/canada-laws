RRS c S-63.1 Reg 3

*Made under The Summary Offences Procedures Act, 1990*

### Title

1 These regulations may be cited as The Fine Option Program Regulations, 2026.

### Definitions

2 In these regulations:

“Act” means The Summary Offences Procedure Act, 1990;

“community service work” means an activity that:

(a) is normally performed by volunteers and does not affect employment

opportunities in the community;

(b) benefits:

(i) a non-profit organization;

(ii) a department, ministry, corporation or agency of the

Government of Canada or the Government of Saskatchewan;

(iii) a municipality;

(iv) a First Nation, Indian band or Tribal Council;

(v) a Métis community or organization;

(c) creates benefits visible in the community;

(d) has the offender work with other citizens rather than alone; and

(e) does not include treatment or counselling;

“completion date” means the day on which an offender registered in the

program is expected to settle the fine;

“conviction notice” means a notice given to an offender pursuant to

subsection 22(3), 32.61(6) or 32.63(6) of the Act;

“court” means the court that assessed the fine;

“fine” includes, with respect to the same offence:

(a) a late payment charge imposed pursuant to subsection 28(1) of the

Act;

(b) a surcharge imposed pursuant to section 10 of The Victims of Crime

Act, 1995; and

(c) a victim surcharge imposed pursuant to section 737 of the Criminal

Code;

“fine option agency” means an agency that is designated by the program

director pursuant to section 5 and that enters into an agreement to administer

the program;

“fine option program registration form” means a fine option program

registration form in the form required by the minister;

“fine settlement form” means a fine settlement form in the form required

by the minister;

“immediate relative” means any of the following with respect to the offender:

(a) the spouse;

(b) a child;

(c) a parent or legal decision-maker;

(d) a sibling;

(e) a grandparent;

(f) a grandchild;

(g) an aunt or uncle;

(h) a niece or nephew;

(i) a cousin;

“ministry” means the ministry over which the minister presides;

“notice of fine” means a notice of fine and surcharge in the form required

by the minister;

“offender” means a person who has been convicted by a court in Saskatchewan

of an offence against:

(a) the Criminal Code;

(b) any other Act of the Parliament of Canada;

(c) an Act of the Legislative Assembly of Saskatchewan;

(d) a regulation made pursuant to any of the Acts mentioned in

clauses (a) to (c);

(e) a municipal bylaw;

(f) NYF. 24 Jly 2026 cS-63.1 Reg 3 s2;

and includes a person who has been convicted pursuant to section 22 of The

Summary Offences Procedure Act, 1990, but does not include a young person

with respect to whom a sentence is imposed by a youth court in accordance

with the Youth Criminal Justice Act (Canada);

“offender’s work record” means an offender’s work record in the form

required by the minister;

“program” means the fine option program continued pursuant to section 4;

“program director” means the director of the program in the ministry;

“spouse” means:

(a) the person who is married to the offender; or

(b) the person with whom the offender is cohabiting and has cohabited

as a spouse continuously for at least 2 years;

“work placement” means community service work assigned to an offender;

“work placement agency” means any of the following that provides

supervised community service work:

(a) a corporation incorporated pursuant to:

(i) The Non-profit Corporations Act, 2022;

(ii) The Cities Act;

(iii) The Municipalities Act; or

(iv) The Northern Municipalities Act, 2010;

(b) a non-profit co-operative incorporated pursuant to The Co-operatives

Act, 1996;

(c) a department, ministry, corporation or agency of the Government of

Canada or the Government of Saskatchewan;

(d) a First Nation, Indian band or Tribal Council;

(e) a Métis community or organization;

“young person” means a young person as defined in the Youth Criminal

Justice Act (Canada);

“youth court” means a youth justice court as defined in the Youth Criminal

Justice Act (Canada).

### Non-application

3 These regulations do not apply to any fine that is:

(a) assessed against a corporation, company, society or association; or

(b) ordered as a sentence by a youth court in accordance with the Youth

Criminal Justice Act (Canada).

### Program continued

4 For the purposes of section 5 of the Act and section 718.1 of the Criminal Code,

the fine option program is continued.

### Agreement

5(1) For the purposes of carrying out the program, the program director may:

(a) designate a fine option agency; and

(b) enter into an agreement with a fine option agency.

(2) An agreement entered into pursuant to clause (1)(b) must set out the duties of

the fine option agency in accordance with these regulations.

### Fine option agency

6 The fine option agency is responsible for the following:

(a) administering the program in accordance with these regulations;

(b) developing and maintaining an adequate supply of work placements.

### Notice of fine

7 If a court grants an offender a period in which to pay a fine, the court shall issue

to the offender a notice of fine.

### Registration in program

8(1) Subject to subsections (2) and (3) and section 9, an offender who is issued a

notice of fine or a conviction notice may apply to be registered in the program if the

offender provides the fine option agency with the notice of fine or conviction notice

within the time indicated on the notice of fine or conviction notice.

(2) No offender may be registered pursuant to subsection (1) if the fine is the subject

of an appeal by the offender or the Crown.

(3) If an appeal is commenced after the offender’s registration, the fine option

agency shall immediately suspend the offender’s registration in the program until

the result of the appeal is known.

### Refusal of registration in program and notice to program director

9(1) The fine option agency may refuse to register an offender in the program if

the offender was previously registered in the program and was terminated from the

program pursuant to section 22.

(2) If the fine option agency refuses to register an offender in the program pursuant

to subsection (1), the fine option agency shall, within 2 days after the date of refusal:

(a) give the offender written reasons for its decision and inform the offender

that, within 30 days after receiving the decision, the offender may make

written submissions to the program director as to why the offender should be

registered in the program; and

(b) notify the program director.

(3) After reviewing the offender’s written submissions, if any, made pursuant to

clause (2)(a), the program director may:

(a) confirm the refusal to register the offender in the program; or

(b) register the offender in the program if the program director is satisfied

that the offender will comply with the terms and conditions of the program

and that it is in the public interest to register the offender.

### Responsibilities of offender

10 An offender who is registered in the program shall:

(a) personally perform the community service work identified for the offender

by the work placement agency;

(b) if the offender is receiving employment insurance benefits or benefits

pursuant to The Saskatchewan Assistance Act and the regulations made

pursuant to that Act while participating in the program, be available for

employment and make reasonable efforts to obtain work;

(c) obey the lawful instructions of the work placement agency and the fine

option agency; and

(d) act in accordance with the general standards of behaviour required of

employees in a place of employment.

### Required hours of work

11(1) For each fine, the number of hours of community service work required of

an offender is to be determined by the fine option agency having regard to:

(a) the amount of the fine that the offender intends to settle by community

service work through the program;

(b) the minimum wage per hour as established pursuant to Part 9 of The

Employment Standards Regulations, 2025; and

(c) any direction from the court.

(2) All work performed in the program is of equal value regardless of the tools,

equipment or skills that the offender may use to perform the work.

### Court to be notified of registration

12 The fine option agency shall, within 2 days after the date of registration of an

offender, notify the court of the registration by providing the fine option program

registration form to the court.

### Completion date

13 For the purposes of establishing a completion date, the fine option agency shall

consider:

(a) the circumstances of the offender;

(b) the supply of supervised community service work; and

(c) the number of hours required to settle the fine.

### Completion date unsatisfactory

14 If the court is not satisfied with the completion date, the court shall immediately

notify the fine option agency and, if a satisfactory date cannot be agreed on, the

agency shall immediately terminate the offender’s registration.

### Change in completion date

15(1) If the fine option agency considers it to be appropriate, it may make a request

to the court to extend a completion date.

(2) If the fine option agency makes a request pursuant to subsection (1) and the

court is satisfied that an offender has been unable, for a reason that the court

considers valid, to complete the required hours of community service work by the

completion date, the court may set a new completion date.

### Work placement for offender

16(1) The fine option agency shall:

(a) select an appropriate work placement agency for the offender; and

(b) inform the offender in writing of:

(i) the location of the work placement agency;

(ii) the starting date and time;

(iii) the number of hours required; and

(iv) the completion date.

(2) Subject to section 22, if the offender is unable to complete the required hours

of community service work with a work placement agency, the fine option agency

may select another appropriate work placement agency for the offender.

(3) If the fine option agency selects another work placement agency for an offender

pursuant to subsection (2), the fine option agency shall inform the offender of the

new work placement agency in the manner set out in clause (1)(b).

### Conflict of interest

17(1) No work placement agency shall accept an offender who is:

(a) an employee, volunteer, officer or director of the work placement agency; or

(b) a person who, while working at the work placement agency, will be

supervised by an immediate relative.

(2) If an offender who is registered in the program is an employee, volunteer, officer

or director of a fine option agency:

(a) the offender’s work placement requires the prior approval of the program

director; and

(b) the supervising and documenting of the offender’s work placement must

be done by a person who:

(i) is not an immediate relative; and

(ii) is approved in writing by the program director to supervise the

offender.

### Program director’s veto

18 If the program director is not satisfied with the work placement for an offender,

the program director may direct that an alternative work placement be arranged.

### Fine settlement

19(1) On the completion or termination of a work placement, the work placement

agency shall provide the fine option agency with the offender’s work record.

(2) The fine option agency shall, with respect to the fine settlement form:

(a) provide the form to the court within 2 weeks after the completion date,

as payment of the fine;

(b) retain a copy of the form for its records; and

(c) provide a copy of the form to the offender, as a receipt.

(3) The court shall credit the amount earned by the offender in the program against

the amount of the offender’s fine in the same manner as a cash payment and, if the

fine is partially settled, shall reduce any period of imprisonment on a pro rata basis.

### 150 hours or more of community service work

20(1) For the purposes of this section, “total number of hours of community

service work required of an offender” means the total number of hours of

community service work that the offender is required to complete for all of the

offender’s fines determined pursuant to subsection 11(1), plus the total number of

hours of community service work, if any, that the offender is required to complete

pursuant to the following:

(a) a community service order made by the court;

(b) a community youth justice services program pursuant to The Youth Justice

Administration Act, 2019;

(c) a community service order made pursuant to an Alternative Measures

program pursuant to section 717 of the Criminal Code.

(2) Subject to subsection (3), if the total number of hours of community service

work required of an offender is 150 hours or more:

(a) the fine option agency shall:

(i) obtain the approval of the program director before making the work

placement for the offender; and

(ii) after the work placement has been made, submit the offender’s work

record to the program director on a monthly basis; and

(b) the program director shall monitor the offender’s progress in completing

the required hours of community service work.

(3) If the total number of hours of community service work required of an offender

is 150 hours or more, the program director may at any time terminate the registration

of an offender in the program if, in the opinion of the program director, the time

required to complete the community service work is unreasonable.

### Termination of work placement

21(1) A work placement agency may terminate an offender’s work placement if:

(a) the community service work done by the offender is, in the opinion of the

work placement agency, unsatisfactory;

(b) the offender fails to report to the work placement agency on the day on

which the offender was required to report to it;

(c) the offender voluntarily withdraws from the program;

(d) the offender, in the opinion of the work placement agency, failed to obey

the lawful instructions of the work placement agency; or

(e) the offender, in the opinion of the work placement agency, failed to act

in accordance with the general standards of behaviour required of employees

in a place of employment.

(2) If a work placement agency terminates an offender’s work placement pursuant

to subsection (1), the work placement agency shall, within 2 days after the date of

the termination, notify:

(a) the offender; and

(b) the fine option agency.

### Termination of registration in program

22(1) The fine option agency may terminate the offender’s registration in the

program before the completion date if:

(a) the community service work done by the offender is, in the opinion of the

fine option agency, unsatisfactory;

(b) the offender fails to report to the work placement agency on the day on

which the offender was required to report to it;

(c) the offender voluntarily withdraws from the program;

(d) the offender, in the opinion of the fine option agency, failed to obey the

lawful instructions of the fine option agency or the work placement agency; or

(e) the offender, in the opinion of the fine option agency, failed to act in

accordance with the general standards of behaviour required of employees in

a place of employment.

(2) If the fine option agency terminates an offender’s registration in the program

pursuant to subsection (1), the fine option agency shall, within 2 days after the date

of its decision to terminate:

(a) give the offender written reasons for its decision and inform the offender

that, within 30 days after receiving the decision, the offender may make

written submissions to the program director as to why the offender should be

registered in the program; and

(b) notify the program director.

(3) After reviewing the offender’s written submissions, if any, made pursuant to

clause (2)(a), the program director may:

(a) confirm the termination of the offender’s registration in the program; or

(b) reinstate the offender in the program if the program director is satisfied

that the offender will comply with the terms and conditions of the program and

it is in the public interest to reinstate the offender’s registration in the program.

### Responsibilities of work placement agency

23 A work placement agency shall:

(a) provide meaningful community service work for the offenders;

(b) supervise and maintain daily records of the community service work

performed by offenders;

(c) complete the work record for each offender placed in a work placement

and return the offender’s work record to the fine option agency at the

completion date; and

(d) establish a confidentiality policy and keep all records and matters

associated with the work placement confidential in accordance with that policy.

### Records

24 Any records, papers, documents or other file materials related to the registration

and work placement of offenders held by the fine option agency or work placement

agency shall be made available to the program director at reasonable times for the

purpose of maintaining the proper administration of the program.

### Delivery of documents

25 Any document that is required to be delivered pursuant to these regulations

is, unless otherwise provided for in another enactment, to be delivered:

(a) personally;

(b) by ordinary mail or registered mail to the last known address of the

person or office;

(c) by email to:

(i) an email address provided by the person or office; or

(ii) a publicly available email address associated with the person or

office; or

(d) by fax to:

(i) a fax number provided by the person or office; or

(ii) a publicly available fax number associated with the person or office.

### RRS c S-63.1 Reg 1 repealed

26 The Fine Option Program Regulations, 1991 are repealed.

### Transitional

27(1) In this section, “former regulations” means The Fine Option Program

Regulations, 1991 as those regulations existed on the day before the coming into

force of these regulations.

(2) On the coming into force of these regulations, all designations made and

agreements entered into by the program director pursuant to section 5 of the former

regulations are to be terminated at the discretion of the program director.

(3) Work placements entered into pursuant to the former regulations and in

existence on the coming into force of these regulations are continued and are to be

dealt with in accordance with these regulations.

(4) Notwithstanding the repeal of the former regulations, any fee for service that

was owing to a fine option agency pursuant to section 27 of the former regulations for

a work placement that was made by the fine option agency before these regulations

came into force shall be paid by the ministry in accordance with section 27 of the

former regulations as if that section had not been repealed.

### Coming into force

28(1) Subject to subsection (2), these regulations come into force on the day on

which they are filed with the Registrar of Regulations.

(2) Clause (f) of the definition of “offender” in section 2 comes into force on

the day on which section 3 of The Summary Offences Procedure Amendment

Act, 2023 comes into force.
