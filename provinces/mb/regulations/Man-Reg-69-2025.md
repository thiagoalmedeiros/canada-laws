M.R. 69/2025

The Education Administration Act, C.C.S.M. c. E10

### Definitions

1 The following definitions apply in this regulation.

"Act" means The Education Administration Act. (« Loi »)

"clinician" means a person certified as a clinician under the regulations to the Act. (« spécialiste »)

"competence standards" means the standards set out in the Schedule to this regulation. (« normes d'aptitude »)

"program" means a program as defined in subsection 1(1) of The Public Schools Act. (« programme »)

### Teacher competence standards

2 A teacher must meet the competence standards in order to be issued and maintain their teaching certificate.

### Interpretation — professional misconduct

3 The competence standards must not be interpreted as limiting the generality of the definition "professional misconduct" in section 8.1 of the Act and are made in addition to and not in substitution for or in derogation of the application of that definition or any regulation that prescribes conduct that constitutes professional misconduct under clause (c) of that definition.

### Application — clinicians

4(1) For certainty, the competence standards apply, with necessary changes, to a clinician in addition to and not in substitution for or in derogation of any enactment regulating the standards of practice of the clinician.

### Commissioner discretion

4(2) The commissioner appointed under section 8.2 of the Act is to use discretion when applying the competence standards to a clinician in order to account for occupational differences between teachers and clinicians.

### Coming into force

5 This regulation comes into force on September 2, 2025.

## SCHEDULE

(Section 1)

### COMPETENCE STANDARDS

### Competence standards

1 The competence standards are set out in Column 1 of the following table. Listed opposite in Column 2 are examples of how each competence standard may be demonstrated.

| Column 1 Competence Standard | Column 2 Examples Demonstrating Competency |
| --- | --- |
| 1 The teacher has professional knowledge and implements professional instructional methods and practices | The teacher consistently delivers instruction that (a) demonstrates professional knowledge and professional instructional methods or practices; and (b) fosters student well-being and understanding to promote academic growth and success. |
| The teacher consistently demonstrates knowledge and understanding of the curriculum and methodologies in the subject areas that they teach. |  |
| The teacher adapts and utilizes various instructional strategies, learning experiences, resources, materials and technologies to teach and be responsive to their students and address the diverse learning strengths and needs of the students. |  |
| The teacher assesses the work of each student and uses a variety of assessments and evaluation practices in a manner that aligns with identified learning outcomes. |  |
| The teacher utilizes proactive and responsive classroom management strategies and techniques to create and promote a positive and supportive learning environment in which students are able to feel respected and safe and have a sense of belonging. |  |
| The teacher uses professional instructional methods and practices to address diverse learning needs in ways that respect the cultural and linguistic identities of their students. |  |
| The teacher engages in continuing competency and professional development and actively challenges themselves to enhance their skills and knowledge and foster their ongoing professional growth. |  |
| The teacher actively participates in professional learning related to subject-area content, pedagogy, assessment, technology and appropriate instructional strategies. |  |
| The teacher collaborates formally and informally with their colleagues to improve learning and outcomes for students. |  |
| 2 The teacher is committed to student success, well-being and learning | The teacher displays an understanding of the developmental characteristics of their students (including cognitive, socio-emotional and behavioural characteristics) and the factors that impact their development. |
| The teacher holds each of their students to expectations appropriate to the individual student's learning needs and consistently acts in the student's best interest to support their well-being, academic growth and success. |  |
| The teacher engages students in meaningful participation in their own learning. |  |
| 3 The teacher fosters a safe and inclusive environment | The teacher builds positive relationships with students and creates a culturally safe classroom in which all cultures and backgrounds are respected. |
| The teacher fosters a sense of belonging, self-esteem and self-determination in all students. |  |
| The teacher understands the need to uphold human rights and actively challenge racism, discrimination and oppression in all forms and incorporates those principles in their teaching. |  |
| The teacher understands the department's philosophy of inclusion and incorporates it into their teaching. |  |
| The teacher designs lessons that are accessible and engaging and uses differentiated instruction to provide learning experiences that support learning for all students. |  |
| The teacher promotes meaningful participation and equitable access to learning by removing barriers to participation. |  |
| The teacher understands the context and history of the program in which they teach (Français, French Immersion, English or Senior Years Technology Education) and how the context and history of the program may affect a student's learning experience. |  |
| 4 The teacher is engaged with Indigenous students, families and communities | The teacher values, respects and fosters a deeper understanding of the languages, heritages, cultures and ways of knowing and being of First Nations, Inuit and Métis. |
| The teacher integrates the perspectives and worldviews of First Nations, Inuit and Métis into learning environments. |  |
| The teacher upholds the principles outlined in the department's First Nations, Inuit and Métis education policy framework. |  |
| The teacher builds and sustains respectful relationships with Indigenous students, families, Elders, Knowledge Keepers and communities. |  |
| The teacher is committed to understanding treaties, the legacy of colonization (including residential schools) and the responsibilities of reconciliation while building relationships rooted in respect, reciprocity and accountability. |  |
| 5 The teacher fosters professional relationships in ways that are consistent with principles of equity, fairness and respect for others | The teacher fosters positive, productive and professional relationships with students, parents and caregivers, peers, colleagues and others in the school and local community to support student well-being. |
| The teacher acknowledges the inherent power imbalance in favour of the teacher in their professional relationships with students, parents and caregivers. |  |
| The teacher recognizes the skill, knowledge, judgment and roles of others involved in the school and local community and communicates effectively and in a manner that is consistent with principles of equity, fairness and respect for others. |  |
| The teacher supports, mentors and encourages other teachers and those preparing to enter the teaching profession. |  |
| The teacher works collaboratively and cooperatively with colleagues in mutually supportive ways. |  |
