## XIII — PROSECUTION OF OFFENCES

**345** A notice of prosecution shall state sufficiently the alleged offence.

A notice of prosecution may contain more than one offence. Each alleged offence shall be set out in a separate count.

**346** A notice of prosecution shall be signed by the racing judges’ secretary, by the veterinarian of the Régie des alcools, des courses et des jeux, the paddock judge or the secretary of the board and it orders a person to appear at the time and place mentioned. The notice shall be received at least 24 hours before the date fixed for the holding of the hearing, unless the parties agree to a shorter timeframe.

The notice may be made on the prescribed form.

**347** A person summoned to a hearing to answer a notice of prosecution may waive the hearing by entering a guilty plea.

**348** A person summoned to a hearing may appear personally or by counsel.

**349** Where a person summoned to a hearing is present, the substance of the offence shall be stated to him and he shall be asked whether he pleads guilty or not guilty.

Where the person summoned pleads guilty, the board or the racing judges, as the case may be, convict him after he has been allowed to make representations.

**350** Where the person summoned pleads not guilty, the board or the racing judges shall proceed with the hearing on the notice of prosecution, hear the witnesses and examine the exhibits and documents for the prosecutor and the defendant.

The deposition of a witness is taken under oath and in the presence of the person summoned or, if he is absent, in the presence of his counsel.

**351** After the evidence for the prosecution has been given, the defence presents its own and the prosecution may adduce evidence in rebuttal.

**352** The notice of prosecution may be amended at any time on the conditions necessary to safeguard the rights of the parties. However, an amendment which would result in an entirely new notice of prosecution having no connection with the original notice may not be granted unless the parties agree to it.

**353** The board or the racing judges, when they impose a fine or adjudge costs, shall fix a time period for the payment. Such time period shall be not less than 10 days and not exceed 90 days.

A person condemned to pay a fine or costs may request, before the time period has expired, an extension.

**354** A driver who is suspended for a period of 5 days or less may, during his suspension, drive in a special race the horses assigned to him.

In that case, his suspension shall be prolonged 1 day for each day he drives a horse.

## XIV — APPEAL

**355** (Replaced).

**356** (Replaced).

**357** A person appeals to the board by producing the prescribed notice of appeal form duly completed with a racing judge or the secretary of the board within 7 days after he is informed of the decision being appealed. That time period is mandatory.

**358** Upon receipt of the notice of appeal, the racing judge who receives it shall send it to the secretary of the board.

**359** Upon receipt of the notice of appeal by the board, the secretary of the board shall forward an acknowledgement of receipt to the appellant.

**360** An appeal shall suspend the enforcement of a suspension imposed by the racing judges, unless the board orders a temporary enforcement thereof.

**361** The appelant may discontinue his appeal at any time before the hearing is held by producing to the board a written notice signed by him or his representative.

The decision which is appealed becomes enforceable as soon as the discontinuance is received at the board. Where the penalty imposed by the racing judges consists in a suspension for a period of time of the exercise of the privileges conferred by the licence, the board shall then fix the date of the beginning of the period of suspension without however extending the duration of the period fixed by the racing judges in their decision.

**362** The record in appeal before the board is constituted of the following documents:

(1) the notice of appeal signed by the appellant;

(2) the notice of prosecution;

(3) the decision of the racing judges;

(4) the record of the sound track of the hearing held by the racing judges, if any;

(5) the video recording when the offence appealed was committed during a race, if any;

(6) the certificate of positive analysis when the alleged offence relates to the administration of drugs.

**363** The appellant shall establish that his appeal is well-founded. He is the first to argue.

**364** Where the ends of justice so require, the board may, *ex officio* or on application by a party, hear witnesses who testified before the racing judges.

The board may also require or authorize additional evidence.

**365** Where the board hears witnesses or additional evidence is presented, each party may examine or cross examine the witnesses assigned, adduce evidence in rebuttal and present his arguments.

**366** Where a decision of the racing judges is appealed in which there is an error in writing or calculation or any clerical error, the board in appeal may correct the decision.
