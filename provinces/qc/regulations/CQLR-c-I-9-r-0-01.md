# Regulation respecting the professional activities that may be engaged in by a designer employed by a lightweight wood-frame manufacturer

> **Unofficial version, for personal and non-commercial use only.** © Gouvernement du Québec. Not endorsed by or affiliated with the Government of Quebec. Text obtained from the [official source](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cr/I-9,%20r.%200.01) via the [A2AJ Canadian Laws dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/a2aj/canadian-laws). Always check the official version before relying on it.

*CQLR c I-9, r 0.01*

*Enabling statute: Engineers Act (chapter I-9, s. 10)*

*Updated to 2026-04-23*

## I — GENERAL

**1** This Regulation determines, among the professional activities reserved to engineers, those that, in accordance with the conditions specified herein, may be engaged in by a designer employed by a lightweight wood-frame manufacturer.

For the purposes of this Regulation,

**designer** means a person responsible for the design of lightweight wood framing;

**lightweight wood framing** means a construction system composed of small-scale wood components that ensure the structural stability of a building.

## II — AUTHORIZED PROFESSIONAL ACTIVITIES

**2** The designer may, on behalf of the lightweight wood-frame manufacturer by which the designer is employed, prepare, modify and sign a plan, drawing or specification relating to a lightweight wood framing that meets the conditions set out in Division III.

A designer who is a member of the Ordre des technologues professionnels du Québec may also seal a plan, drawing or specification prepared or modified by the designer under this Regulation.

The designer may perform the calculations required to carry out one of the professional activities referred to in the first paragraph.

**3** The preparation or modification of plans, drawings or estimates referred to in the first paragraph of section 2 and the calculations required for those purposes must be engaged in using modelling and design software that has been certified in writing by an engineer to have equations that generate results which comply with the standards of the Construction Code (chapter B-1.1, r. 2).

The only input data that the designer may enter in the software are

(1) the location of the building and the characteristics of the surrounding land;

(2) the standards applicable to the design of the lightweight wood framing;

(3) the loads on the surface of a floor or roof, when the loads are distributed evenly per surface area or have been calculated by an engineer; and

(4) the dimensions and other characteristics of the building construction systems and materials used.

However, when the forces exerted on a lightweight wood framing have been determined by an engineer or provided by the software, the use of the latter is not required for the selection of a metal tie to assemble the lightweight wood framing or to fasten it to another structural component of the building.

**4** The designer must sign any plan, drawing or specification prepared pursuant to this Regulation.

## III — LIGHTWEIGHT WOOD FRAMES

**5** A lightweight wood frame relating to a professional activity referred to in section 2 must meet the following conditions:

(1) it is intended for a building regulated by Part 9 of the National Building Code, as incorporated into the Construction Code (chapter B-1.1, r. 2), other than a medium hazard industrial occupancy;

(2) the structure of the building for which it is intended is composed of a series of small structural wood components with a maximum spacing of 600 mm;

(3) it is not exposed to adverse weather conditions once integrated to the building;

(4) all beams that form part of the framing meet the following conditions:

(a) its free span does not exceed 12.20 m;

(b) the height-to-width ratio does not exceed 4:1; and

(c) lateral bracing at each support prevents lateral displacement and rotation.

**6** When designed to support a roof covering, the lightweight wood framing referred to in section 5 must also meet the following conditions:

(1) it supports only the roof covering, snow loads, ceiling loads and attic live loads;

(2) the 1-in-50 hourly wind pressure on the framing is under 0.8 kPa;

(3) the supported roof covering is asphalt shingles, wood shingles or shakes, metal cladding or a waterproof membrane.

When the lightweight wood-frame component is a roof truss, it must also

(1) consist of a single section;

(2) be assembled using metal connecter plates;

(3) have a maximum free span of 12.20 m;

(4) be fully triangulated when more than 3 m long; and

(5) be intended for a building with an unusable roof space.

**7** When intended to support a floor, the lightweight wood framing referred to in section 5 must also meet the following conditions:

(1) its joists consist of single sections;

(2) it supports only the live load of the floor, the dead load of the floor and the dead load of the ceiling;

(3) the specified live load on supported subfloors and floor framing does not exceed 2.4 kPa; and

(4) the thickness of the concrete topping it supports does not exceed 51 mm.

**8** When the lightweight wood framing referred to in section 5 forms a wall, it must have columns whose height does not exceed 3.6 m or, in the case of interior columns, 4.2 m.

## IV — REQUIRED TRAINING

**9** To engage in the professional activities referred to in section 2, the designer must meet the following conditions:

(1) the designer has successfully completed the training offered by the Comité sectoriel de main-d’œuvre du bois sur la construction à ossature de bois, except the training modules on buildings that are not covered by this Regulation and those on repairing structural components, building envelopes and standards applicable outside Canada;

(2) the designer has undergone training on the software used to engage in the professional activities concerned that is offered by the software provider or a person authorized by that provider; and

(3) the designer has completed, under the direction of an engineer or another person authorized to engage in those professional activities, 12 months of full-time practical training to acquire the skills necessary to use the software and become proficient in the standards applicable to the design of lightweight wood framing.

A designer who is a member of the Ordre des technologues professionnels du Québec and whose competency is in architectural technology or engineering technology is exempt from undergoing the training referred to in subparagraph 1 of the first paragraph.

## VI — TRANSITIONAL AND FINAL

**11** The designer is exempt from undergoing the practical training provided for in subparagraph 3 of the first paragraph of section 9 if, in the 5 years preceding the coming into force of this Regulation, the designer acquired 12 months of full-time experience in designing lightweight wood framing.

**12** (Omitted).
