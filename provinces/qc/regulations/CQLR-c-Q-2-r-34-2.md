*Enabling statute: Environment Quality Act (chapter Q-2, s. 46.0.21, 2nd par., s. 46.0.22, pars. 6, 8, 9, 10, 12, 15 and 16, s. 95.1, 1st par., subpars. 7, 13, 16, 20, 21 and 25.1, and 2nd par., s. 124.1)*

## I — GENERAL

**1** This Regulation applies to all works satisfying the following conditions:

(1) the works was built or modified to limit the natural expansion of lake or watercourse waters and to prevent flooding;

(2) the works was built to be permanent;

(3) the works is designed to protect a group of persons and properties in a community;

(4) the works does not create a permanent reservoir.

The following are not flood protection covered by this Regulation applies:

(1) dams subject to the Dam Safety Act (chapter S-3.1.01);

(2) ice control works that help to minimize flooding;

(3) storm water management works;

(4) agricultural dikes, including a sluice, that protect against flooding only of land subject to the Act respecting the preservation of agricultural land and agricultural activities (chapter P-41.1);

(5) stabilization works within the meaning of section 313 of the Regulation respecting the regulatory scheme applying to activities on the basis of their environmental impact (chapter Q-2, r. 17.1);

(6) retaining walls;

(7) works built for purposes other than to prevent flooding and not converted with a view to flood protection.

This Regulation applies to reserved areas and agricultural zones established under the Act respecting the preservation of agricultural land and agricultural activities.

**2** For the purposes of this Regulation, unless the context indicates otherwise,

**actual protection level** means, if the flood protection works borders a lake, the water level, expressed in metres, at which an overflow or bypass cannot occur, and a rupture of the works is unlikely. For a flood protection works bordering a watercourse, the actual protection level is expressed as a flow rate in cubic metres per second and is represented by a longitudinal profile of the water levels associated with the protection level. In both cases, a recurrence interval level may be associated with the protection level; (*niveau de protection réel*)

**ancillary works** means any works, building or equipment necessary for the proper operation of a flood protection works; (*dispositif connexe*)

**apparent protection level** means, if the flood protection works borders a lake, the water level, expressed in metres, at which an overflow or bypass occurs. For a flood protection works bordering a watercourse, the apparent protection level is expressed as a flow rate in cubic metres per second and is represented by a longitudinal profile of the water levels associated with the protection level. In both cases, a recurrence interval level may be associated with the protection level; (*niveau de protection apparent*)

**building** means a fixed, mobile or floating structure that is not designed for ground, rail or water traffic, has a roof and is used or intended to be used to shelter, house or receive persons, animals, foodstuffs or any other thing, except a structure that is part of or attached to a dam; a structure, works or equipment adjoining a building, such as a terrace, a garage or a balcony, is considered to be part of the building; (*bâtiment*)

**downstream** means the side of a flood protection works facing persons or property the works is intended to protect from flooding; (*aval*)

**exposed zone** means the area downstream of a flood protection works that is likely to be flooded if a failure, overflow or bypass of the works occurs during a flood period; (*zone exposée*)

**freeboard** means the vertical distance between the design flood level elevation and the crest elevation of the flood protection works; (*revanche hydraulique*)

**neutralization** means an action consisting in re-establishing a permanent hydraulic link between the environments upstream and downstream of the flood protection works to allow for flood expansion behind the works, without proceeding to its demolition; (*neutralisation*)

**non-residential building** means any building other than a residential building or a building accessory to a residential building; (*bâtiment non résidentiel*)

**public body** means a body to which the Government or a minister appoints the majority of the members, to which, by law, the personnel is appointed in accordance with the Public Service Act (chapter F‐3.1.1), or at least half of whose capital stock is derived from the Consolidated Revenue Fund; (*organisme public*)

**residential building** means a building at least one part of which is used or intended to be used as a main or secondary private residence by a natural person, including when the residence is occasionally offered for rent to tourists; (*bâtiment résidentiel*)

**segment** means a part of the flood protection works whose physical and structural characteristics or the characteristics of the environment where it is situated differ from those of the adjacent part or parts; (*tronçon*)

**toe of a flood protection works** means the point of intersection of the flood protection works with the natural terrain surface; (*pied d’un ouvrage de protection contre les inondations*)

**upstream** means the side of a flood protection works facing a lake or watercourse whose natural expansion the works is designed to limit. (*amont*)

**3** For the purposes of this Regulation,

(1) a flood protection works referred to in section 1 extends over a distance of 3 m from its downstream toe and upstream toe, calculated horizontally from the works, except in the following cases:

(a) where the upstream toe of the works is situated within the 2 year flood recurrence level, it ends at the upstream toe;

(b) where the upstream toe of the works is situated less than 3 m from the 2 year flood recurrence level, on that side, the works extends to the 2 year flood recurrence level;

(2) the presence of removable elements does not alter the permanent character of the flood protection works;

(3) all ancillary works of a flood protection works, including those described in paragraph 2, form a part of the works;

(4) every composite of works, infrastructures and elements of the environment, continuous or discontinuous, forming consistent protection of a territory and at least one part of which meets the conditions in the first paragraph of section 1 is considered to be a flood protection works;

(5) the construction of an infrastructure, works, building or equipment consists in its siting as well as its replacement, reconstruction, substantial modification, relocation, dismantling and demolition; the conversion of another type of works to a flood protection works is considered to be siting of a flood protection works;

(6) the maintenance of an infrastructure, works, building or equipment consists in inspection, refurbishment and repair not involving a change to its structural or functional characteristics and is carried out in the immediate vicinity of the infrastructure, works, building or equipment;

(7) substantial modification consists in a change to the structural or functional characteristics, or a reduction or increase in the size of an infrastructure, works, building or equipment;

(8) unless otherwise provided, vegetation management and minor soil grading required for the carrying out of an activity referred to in this Regulation are included in the activity;

(9) minor soil grading involves levelling the soil to create a uniform surface, free from depressions and irregularities, including the replacement of a thin layer of unconsolidated deposits; and

(10) the management of vegetation includes cutting, pruning, removing, planting and seeding vegetation, but excludes cultivation of non-aquatic plants and mushrooms, and forest development activities.

## II — STUDIES AND SPECIFIC FLOOD PROTECTION WORKS PLAN

**4** For the purposes of this Chapter, a local municipality may enter into an agreement with any municipality within the meaning of the Environment Quality Act (chapter Q-2), hereafter **the Act**, to have that municipality conduct the required study, in keeping with applicable law. If such an agreement is entered into, a notice to that effect must be sent to the Minister.

### I — CHARACTERIZATION STUDY OF FLOOD PROTECTION WORKS

**5** Every local municipality that has a flood protection works situated entirely or partly in its territory must conduct a characterization study in accordance with this Division for each such works. The main purpose of the study is to compile information regarding the flood protection works and to determine the exposed zone of the works.

If the flood protection works consists of a composite of works, infrastructures and elements of the environment, the study must cover that composite.

The local municipality must conduct a characterization study at least once every 10 years and send a copy to the Minister before the end of the calendar year in which the study must be conducted.

For a flood protection works situated in the territory of more than one local municipality, the municipalities concerned must send a single study for that works.

**6** The characterization study of a flood protection works must contain the following information and documents:

(1) a general description of the flood protection works;

(2) the location of the flood protection works and its boundaries;

(3) a history of actions carried out on the flood protection works;

(4) a review of the information available concerning the flood protection works, in particular design and refurbishing studies and documents;

(5) the results of the visual inspection;

(6) the apparent protection level of the flood protection works and a recommendation for the responder mobilization thresholds, and the warning thresholds for the specific flood protection works plan;

(7) identification of hazards other than high water that may create risks for the safety of the flood protection works;

(8) a description of the entry access and roadways allowing the flood protection works to be monitored and maintained and enabling timely response to an emergency;

(9) determination of the maximum boundaries of the exposed zone of the works;

(10) identification and characteristics of the vulnerable elements in the exposed zone of the works;

(11) a non-technical summary of the characteristics of the flood protection works;

(12) the elements enabling the local municipality to prepare the specific flood protection works plan;

(13) any recommendation considered relevant.

The characterization study must be signed by an engineer.

**7** The boundaries of the flood protection works must be established on the basis of the following elements:

(1) a topographical survey whose data includes the downstream and upstream toes and the crest of the works;

(2) the longitudinal profile of the crest and the upstream and downstream toes of each segment, as well as the cross-sectional profiles thereof;

(3) the location of any ancillary works in the form of a dot;

(4) the geometry and location of the works and segments of the works as a polygon representation;

(5) the layout of the segments of the works.

The elements in subparagraphs 1, 3 and 4 of the first paragraph must be created using the NAD83(CSRS)/Quebec Lambert system and made available as digital files.

In the absence of documentation showing the location of the toes of the flood protection works, their location must be determined by an engineer.

**8** The purpose of the visual inspection undertaken for the characterization study of the flood protection works is to detect any irregularities; it must be performed by an engineer. The results of the visual inspection of the flood protection works consist of the following elements in particular:

(1) an appraisal of the physical and structural condition of the flood protection works, each of its segments and its ancillary works, as well as the relevant rating as determined in Schedule I;

(2) a description of existing vegetation and, if applicable, any risks associated with it, and advice on the maintenance required to minimize the risks;

(3) a description of any encroachments that could create a risk for the works or restrict monitoring or maintenance of the works or the carrying out of work;

(4) any other irregularity noted.

**9** The maximum boundaries of the exposed zone of the flood protection works must be transmitted as a polygon representation created using the NAD83(CSRS)/Quebec Lambert system and made available as digital files. The files must be attached to the characterization study.

A map showing the areas characterized by deep waters in the exposed zone of the flood protection works must also be included in the characterization study.

**10** The identification and main characteristics of the vulnerable elements in the exposed zone of the flood protection works must include in particular

(1) the number of persons residing in the zone;

(2) the number of buildings, their use and their property value;

(3) the infrastructures, works, facilities and the public institutions and public security establishments within the meaning of the Regulation respecting activities in wetlands, bodies of water and sensitive areas (chapter Q-2, r. 0.2).

**11** The non-technical summary of the characteristics of the flood protection works consists of

(1) a description of the flood protection works and its ancillary works, including, for each segment, the method of construction, the year of construction, if known, the materials used and any other relevant information;

(2) an appraisal of the flood protection works and the condition of each of its segments, using the rating criteria in Schedule I;

(3) the maximum crest elevation of the works;

(4) the apparent protection level of the works;

(5) the actual protection level of the works, if known;

(6) the boundaries of the exposed zone of the works, established as provided in section 9;

(7) a description of the elements referred to in section 10;

(8) photos of the works; and

(9) a summary of any recommendations in the characterization study.

The non-technical summary of the characteristics of the flood protection works must be reviewed after any substantial modification of the works.

### II — SPECIFIC FLOOD PROTECTION WORKS PLAN

**12** Every local municipality that has a flood protection works situated entirely or partly in its territory must prepare a specific flood protection works plan. The plan must consider all the flood protection works situated entirely or partly in the municipality’s territory and contain the following elements:

(1) a description of possible failures of the flood protection works, including failures of ancillary works, as well as potential points of overflow or bypass;

(2) the boundaries of the extent of the exposed zone of the works established as provided in section 9;

(3) identification of the most vulnerable sectors, in particular because of water depth, should there be a failure, overflow or bypass of the flood protection works;

(4) identification and main characteristics of the vulnerable elements in the exposed zone of the works, determined as provided in section 10;

(5) determination of the responder mobilization thresholds and the thresholds for alerting the population and any other local municipality into whose territory the works’ exposed zone extends;

(6) the procedure for warning the population;

(7) the evacuation procedures in the event of a failure, overflow or bypass of the flood protection works and the resources to be deployed;

(8) the means hat will be used as a preventive measure to explain the risk to the citizens concerned and the instructions to be followed in the event of failure, overflow or bypass, as well as the frequency of communication;

(9) a timetable of implementation exercises for the plan.

The local municipality must integrate the plan into its emergency preparedness plan or append the plan to it.

If the exposed zone of a flood protection works extends into the territory of a local municipality in which the works is not situated, the local municipality in whose territory the works is situated must send the results of the characterization study to that other municipality to enable it to develop a specific plan of its own.

**13** A local municipality that has flood protection works situated entirely or partly in its territory must review its specific flood protection works plan in the following situations:

(1) after a characterization or performance study conducted pursuant to this Regulation;

(2) after work on the flood protection works that may impact the exposed zone of the works or the responder mobilization thresholds and the warning thresholds;

(3) if an inspection reveals an element affecting the safety of the works;

(4) when implementation exercises for the plan reveal deficiencies in the implementation.

The local municipality must complete the review of its specific plan within 6 months after the occurrence of any of the elements referred to in the first paragraph.

**14** The local municipality must inform the Minister in writing of the completion or review of its specific flood protection works plan within 30 days after the completion or review, and send the plan to the Minister on request, within the time and in the manner and form the Minister prescribes.

**15** Every local municipality that has flood protection works situated entirely or partly in its territory must take the necessary measures to ensure its personnel is trained in the measures contained in the specific flood protection works plan.

**16** Every local municipality that has flood protection works situated entirely or partly in its territory must set up a timetable for the holding of exercises to implement the specific flood protection works plan, and send the timetable to the Minister on request, within the time and in the manner and form the Minister prescribes.

### III — PERFORMANCE STUDY

**17** A municipality wishing to request from the Government, under section 46.0.13 of the Act, to be declared responsible for one or more flood protection works situated entirely or partly in its territory must conduct a performance study of the works. The performance study and the characterization study required under Division I may be conducted concurrently on the condition that each meets the requirements applicable to it.

**18** The performance study must be signed by an engineer and contain the following information and documents:

(1) a functional analysis of the components of the flood protection works;

(2) an analysis of encroachments;

(3) an operation and maintenance manual.

The performance study must be accompanied by a technical description drawn up by a land surveyor, which must include a plan showing

(1) the site of the flood protection works;

(2) the site of existing servitudes; and

(3) the lots on which the works is situated.

**19** The functional analysis of the components of a flood protection works must contain the following elements:

(1) stability studies of the works and underlying ground at the places where each type of component segment in the works is considered critical, including the calculations performed using best practices and the applicable design and performance standards, for potential failure modes;

(2) a notice establishing the actual protection level of the works based on the studies conducted pursuant to paragraph 1 and the apparent protection level of the works, as well as the associated recurrence intervals, determined under section 24;

(3) a future climate assessment of the works’ resistance in a 1:350 year flood event;

(4) an assessment of the capacity, reliability and operational condition of the ancillary works;

(5) an assessment of hazards other than high water that may create risks for the flood protection works if such an assessment is warranted in the opinion of the engineer in charge.

**20** The analysis of encroachments on the flood protection works must identify all the buildings, infrastructures and works that encroach on or in the works, and include an assessment of their impact on the works.

For the purposes of this section, every building, infrastructure or works other than an ancillary works that, in the opinion of the engineer performing the encroachment analysis, is likely to affect the safety of the flood protection works is considered to be an encroachment.

**21** An operation and maintenance manual must cover, for each flood protection works,

(1) the management of vegetation on the works;

(2) the operation and maintenance of removable works, and specify at what periods of the year they must be in place;

(3) the operation and maintenance of ancillary works; and

(4) the monitoring and maintenance of the works.

**22** A municipality that is responsible for a flood protection works under an order made pursuant to section 46.0.13 of the Act must review the performance study of the flood protection works at least every 10 years.

On review, the work’s actual protection level must be reassessed based on the data referred to in paragraph 1 of section 25.

**23** A municipality referred to in section 22 must send the review of the performance study to the Minister not later than 60 days after receiving it.

## III — DESIGN AND PERFORMANCE STANDARDS

**24** Sited flood protection works and flood protection works identified in an order made under section 46.0.13 of the Act, must have a future climate design and performance hazard reference that is at least a 1:100 year event.

A design and performance hazard reference corresponds to the flood event interval for which the flood protection works is able to prevent flooding of the upstream area and has a low probability of failure likely to compromise its stability.

**25** Assessment of the flood protection works’ design and performance hazard reference must satisfy the following elements:

(1) the recurrence interval used must be the same as the interval used to produce the flood zone maps approved by the Minister pursuant to subdivision 2 of Division V.1 of Chapter IV of Title I of the Act. If that data is not available, the rules referred to in the second paragraph of section 46.0.2.1 of the Act must be used;

(2) assessment of future climate must be made using the most critical period between the time the study is conducted and the year 2100 or the sea-level rise at 2100, as applicable, and using the rules referred to in the second paragraph of section 46.0.2.1 of the Act.

**26** Assessment of the design and performance hazard reference may be made considering a removable system if an engineer attests that the removable system satisfies the following conditions:

(1) it is a structural component of the flood protection works;

(2) it has the necessary stability and reliability and is able to be deployed in a timely manner in any season.

**27** A sited flood protection works, as well as a flood protection works identified in an order made under section 46.0.13 of the Act, must include a minimum freeboard in the design flood, determined by an engineer, taking into account in particular the uncertainties relating to sample size, methods and models used, precision of the calculations, elements affecting flood routing in the watershed, as well as the other hazards that may impact the operation of the flood protection works, including risks of watercourse obstruction, tides, storm surges and wave overwash.

**28** Every flood protection works sited must at all times have a future climate rupture hazard resistance for at least a 1:350 year flood event. The rupture hazard resistance is established by assessing how the works would behave during a future climate 1:350 year flood event.

The first paragraph also applies to a flood protection works undergoing substantial modification in order to meet design and performance standards set out in sections 24 and 27 and thus be covered by an order made pursuant to section 46.0.13 of the Act or a flood protection works undergoing substantial modification while covered by such an order.

A rupture hazard resistance corresponds to the flood event interval for which it is shown that, even during overflow or bypass, there is no rupture of the flood protection works.

Despite the first and second paragraphs, if the exposed zone of the works may be totally inundated during a less than a 1:350 year future climate flood event, the flood protection works must, at the minimum, have a rupture hazard resistance equal to the recurrence interval corresponding to total inundation of the exposed zone. In no case may that hazard be lesser than the design and performance hazard reference set out in section 24.

**29** The standards set out in this Division apply to the reconstruction of a flood protection works.

Despite the first paragraph, the reconstruction of a flood protection works that does not meet the design and performance standards set out in section 24 because of technical constraints is permitted if it is authorized pursuant to section 165.2 of the Regulation respecting the regulatory scheme applying to activities on the basis of their environmental impact (chapter Q-2, r. 17.1).

**30** For the purposes of this Division, siting excludes conversion of part of a composite of works, infrastructures and elements of the environment, continuous or discontinuous, forming consistent protection of a territory and at least one part of which meets the conditions set out in section 1.

## IV — ACTIVITIES CARRIED OUT ON FLOOD PROTECTION WORKS

### I — GENERAL STANDARDS

**31** No person may carry out work, construction or other activities on a flood protection works if they are likely to compromise the safety of the works.

**32** Construction of flood protection works is prohibited, unless it meets the following conditions:

(1) the construction is carried out by a municipality, a government department or a public body;

(2) there is no other appropriate way to ensure protection of persons and property;

(3) it is in the public interest, in particular because of the number of persons, infrastructures, buildings or works to be protected;

(4) in the case of the siting of a flood protection works, the works is intended to protect a territory in which at least 75% of the land in the protected territory is already occupied by a residential or non-residential building.

**33** The siting and reconstruction on a flood protection works of a non-residential building of an area exceeding 40 m^2, other than an ancillary building necessary for the proper operation of the works, are prohibited.

**34** An infrastructure or works giving access to an infrastructure, works, non-residential building, equipment or site, such as a vehicular entrance or a pedestrian walkway, may be sited or reconstructed on a flood protection works only if the siting or reconstruction cannot take place elsewhere on the lot without encroaching on the flood protection works.

**35** The following activities are prohibited on flood protection works:

(1) forest development activities;

(2) laying out a golf course or campground;

(3) composting farm animals that die on a farm and storage of compost.

**36** The following activities may be carried out on a flood protection works only by a municipality, a government department or a public body:

(1) the construction of an ancillary works necessary for the operation of the flood protection works;

(2) the development of land for recreational purposes on the flood protection works;

(3) the construction of structures other than a building, such as a sign or a fence.

**37** No person carrying out work on a flood protection works may

(1) prevent or impede access to the works; or

(2) prevent the monitoring and maintenance of the works or impede the carrying out of those activities.

Similarly, every person carrying out work on a flood protection works must, in the event of breakdown or incident affecting the flood protection works, inform as soon as possible the local municipality concerned and restore the works to its former condition in compliance with the instructions of an engineer to ensure the safety of the works.

**38** No fill or excavation work may be carried out on flood protection works.

The first paragraph does not apply to activities whose nature necessarily requires fill or excavation work, such as the construction of an infrastructure, works, building or equipment. In such a case, the activities must not alter the topography or the elevation of the flood protection works.

### II — CONDITIONS THAT APPLY TO THE CARRYING OUT OF CERTAIN ACTIVITIES

**39** This Division does not apply to activities covered by a ministerial authorization under sections 165.2 to 165.5 of the Regulation respecting the regulatory scheme applying to activities on the basis of their environmental impact (chapter Q-2, r. 17.1) or to activities for which the authorization is being amended or renewed. In addition, it does not apply to activities requiring a municipal permit under Division III of Chapter III of the Regulation respecting regulatory measures for activities under the responsibility of municipalities carried out in bodies of water and on flood protection works (chapter Q-2, r. 17.2).

#### 1 — Restoration and management of vegetation

**40** On completion of an activity carried out on a flood protection works, the following measures must be applied:

(1) all temporary works must be dismantled and removed from the site;

(2) embankments must be stable and protected against erosion, using the technique most conducive to maintaining the initial conditions of the site; and

(3) the site must be restored within one year following completion of the activity, including, where applicable,

(a) soil restoration; and

(b) in a dewatered zone, revegetation of the areas affected if they have been stripped of vegetation or soil, except

(i) after drilling work; or

(ii) after sample taking, conducting surveys or technical surveys or taking measurements; or

(iii) if the revegetation compromises the stability or safety of the works.

**41** If soil restoration is required under section 40, it must comply with the following conditions:

(1) if water is present, the restoration must be carried out with the stabilized original substrate, unless those materials are not appropriate for ensuring the stability of the works;

(2) all debris and other residual materials must be removed;

(3) the restoration must be carried out with the topography of the flood protection works being preserved.

**42** If revegetation is carried out in accordance with this Regulation, it must comply with the following conditions:

(1) it must be carried out using native species that are adapted to the environment;

(2) the survival rate of the vegetation or cover must be 80% in the year following revegetation; that failing, dead vegetation must be replaced.

**43** Management of vegetation, including revegetation required under this Regulation, must not involve seeding or planting trees or shrubs.

**44** Seeding and planting of invasive exotic plant species within the meaning of the Regulation respecting the regulatory scheme applying to activities on the basis of their environmental impact (chapter Q-2, r. 17.1) on flood protection works are prohibited.

**45** If stump removal is carried out on a flood protection works, the root system must be replaced using materials similar to those in place on the flood protection works and fill and excavation work must be limited to what is necessary.

#### 2 — Operation and use of vehicles and machinery

**46** The operation of motorized vehicles and the use of machinery required to carry out work on a flood protection works must comply with the following conditions:

(1) the operation must take place only in dewatered parts of the flood protection works or if there is ice cover;

(2) if ruts are created, the area must be restored to its original condition, or a condition close thereto.

The condition in subparagraph 1 of the first paragraph does not apply if the operating is required for

(1) drilling work;

(2) construction of temporary works;

(3) conducting surveys and preliminary technical surveys;

(4) taking samples; or

(5) taking measurements.

**47** The refuelling and maintenance of vehicles or machinery must be carried out on the following conditions:

(1) the work is carried out only in dewatered or drained parts of the flood protection works or if there is ice cover;

(2) the vehicle or machinery is equipped with a system for collecting fluid leakage and spillage or with a spillage prevention device.

**48** Hydraulic fluids and drilling greases used for a drill on flood protection works must be degradable to more than 60% in 28 days.

On completion of the work, the following conditions must be met:

(1) all drill holes must be sealed so as to prevent contaminants from migrating from the surface towards an aquifer; and

(2) the tubing must be severed at ground level.

#### 3 — Dewatering on flood protection works

**49** Temporary dewatering on the upstream side of a flood protection works cannot take place more than twice in a 12-month period.

Dewatering work may in no case exceed a period of 30 consecutive days.

**50** Dewatering work on flood protection works must comply with the following conditions:

(1) the equipment and materials used must make it possible to limit the discharge of suspended matter into the lake or watercourse;

(2) if the pumped water contains suspended matter visible to the naked eye, it must be discharged into an area of vegetation located more than 30 m from the littoral zone and elsewhere than on the flood protection works, such as a field of grasses or forest litter, provided the point of discharge is regularly shifted to a new location.

**51** Works used for dewatering on a flood protection works must be dismantled by first removing the materials situated inside the dewatered area and then advancing from the area downstream of the works towards the upstream area.

#### 4 — Sending of information and documents

**52** A person carrying out work, construction or activities on a flood protection works must, at least 15 days before the start of the work, inform the relevant local municipality of the planned work, construction or activities, except in the following cases:

(1) the work, construction or activities require a municipal permit under the Regulation respecting regulatory measures for activities under the responsibility of municipalities carried out in bodies of water and on flood protection works (chapter Q-2, r. 17.2);

(2) the person must obtain an attestation from the municipality concerned confirming its agreement to have the work carried out under the Regulation respecting the regulatory scheme applying to activities on the basis of their environmental impact (chapter Q-2, r. 17.1).

**53** Not later than 90 days after completion of the construction of a flood protection works, except demolition and neutralization, the municipality, government department or public body must send to the Minister

(1) an attestation from an engineer stating that the construction was completed in compliance with the applicable terms and conditions;

(2) the boundaries of the works established as provided in section 7 or a revised version of the boundaries, as applicable;

(3) the non-technical summary of the characteristics of the works referred to in section 11, or a revised version of the summary, as applicable;

(4) the information required under subparagraphs 1 to 6 of the first paragraph of section 68; and

(5) in the case of a siting, a notice indicating that the specific flood protection works plan has been produced.

If the purpose of the work is to allow a municipality responsible for a flood protection works under an order made pursuant to section 46.0.13 of the Act, or wishing to request from the Government to be declared responsible for such a works under that provision, to meet the design and performance standards set out in sections 24 to 28, the operation and maintenance manual, as well as the technical description of the works referred to in section 18, or a revised version of those documents, as applicable, must also be sent to the Minister within the same time.

For the demolition or neutralization of a flood protection works, the municipality, government department or public body that carried out the work must send the attestation referred to in subparagraph 1 of the first paragraph to the Minister within the same time.

For the construction of an ancillary works necessary for the operation of a flood protection works or for an activity carried out on a flood protection works authorized pursuant to section 165.5 of the Regulation respecting the regulatory scheme applying to activities on the basis of their environmental impact (chapter Q-2, r. 17.1), the municipality, government department or public body must send the attestation referred to in subparagraph 1 of the first paragraph to the Minister within the same time.

## V — MONITORING AND MAINTENANCE

### I — PROVISIONS APPLICABLE TO ALL FLOOD PROTECTION WORKS

**54** Every local municipality that has a flood protection works situated entirely or partly in its territory must maintain the herbaceous and shrub vegetation on the works in a manner that allows access to the works, detection of irregularities and prevention of premature deterioration of the works.

**55** Unless a situation is likely to compromise the safety of a flood protection works, work on the works may not be carried out if the responder mobilization thresholds and the warning thresholds identified in the specific flood protection works plan have been reached.

**56** Every local municipality that has a flood protection works situated entirely or partly in its territory must maintain access to the works at all times, enabling the municipality to intervene in urgent circumstances or to prevent serious or irreparable harm or damage to human beings, ecosystems, other living species, the environment or property.

**57** Every local municipality that has a flood protection works situated entirely or partly in its territory must, in any situation likely to compromise the safety of the works, take the necessary measures to remedy the situation without delay.

**58** Every local municipality that has a flood protection works situated entirely or partly in its territory must install visual markers indicating the responder mobilization thresholds and the warning thresholds identified in the specific flood protection works plan.

**59** Every local municipality that has a flood protection works situated entirely or partly in its territory must monitor the works during flood periods. The monitoring must

(1) be consistent with the responder mobilization thresholds and the warning thresholds identified in the municipality’s specific flood protection works plan;

(2) include a log of water level measurements stating the date and time at which the measurements were taken; and

(3) be continuous if the actual protection level of the works has been reached.

### II — PROVISIONS APPLICABLE TO FLOOD PROTECTION WORKS IDENTIFIED IN AN ORDER MADE PURSUANT TO SECTION 46.0.13 OF THE ACT

**60** This Division applies to municipalities that have been declared responsible for a flood protection works under an order made pursuant to section 46.0.13 of the Act.

**61** A municipality referred to this Division must maintain the flood protection works identified in the order, as well as any ancillary works, in good condition.

**62** A municipality referred to this Division must, in respect of a flood protection works identified in the order, put together, keep up to date and apply an operation and maintenance manual in accordance with section 21 and provide the manual to the Minister, at the Minister’s request, within the time and in the manner and form the Minister prescribes.

**63** A municipality referred to this Division must, at least once per year, conduct a visit of the works identified in the order so as to obtain an overview of the condition of the works and a follow-up of any irregularities identified in previous years.

The visit must be conducted by an engineer or a person employed by the municipality with technical experience in the field of hydraulic structures. The person responsible for the visit must produce a written report containing, in particular,

(1) the name and contact information of the person responsible for the visit;

(2) the date of the visit;

(3) the person’s observations, in particular regarding the elements identified by the engineer during the visual inspection referred to in section 8 or section 64;

(4) the photos taken during the visit;

(5) any irregularities identified; and

(6) the follow-up on any irregularities identified during previous inspection visits.

The report must be provided to the Minister, at the Minister’s request, within the time and in the manner and form the Minister prescribes.

**64** A municipality referred to in this Division must have a visual inspection performed on the works identified in the order at least once every 5 years by an engineer who must produce a written report containing in particular,

(1) the engineer’s name and contact information;

(2) the date of the inspection;

(3) the observations made during the inspection;

(4) the photos taken during the inspection;

(5) any irregularities identified; and

(6) the measures to be taken in order to correct the irregularities or to ensure follow-up.

The report must be provided to the Minister at the Minister’s request within the time and in the manner and form the Minister prescribes.

The inspection referred to in the first paragraph is not required during a calendar year in which a performance study is conducted by the municipality referred to in the first paragraph.

**65** A municipality referred to in this Division must create and keep up to date a monitoring and maintenance log containing at least the following information:

(1) the date and a description of each monitoring and maintenance activity carried out, and any relevant comment pertaining to the activity;

(2) a description of each removable system deployment exercise, the problems observed, a course of action to remedy the situation and the corrective measures taken;

(3) if the water level reaches the first mobilization and warning threshold, the water levels and the date and time at which the measurements were taken.

The monitoring and maintenance log must be provided to the Minister, at the Minister’s request, within the time and in the manner and form the Minister prescribes.

**66** A municipality referred to in this Division must increase the monitoring of a flood protection works after a flood, earthquake, storm or ice jam, and during construction on a flood protection works.

The municipality must document in the monitoring and maintenance log each of the situations referred to in the first paragraph.

**67** A municipality referred to in this Division must maintain the works’ compliance with the design and performance standards set out in sections 24, 27 and 28.

## SCHEDULE I — ss. 8 and 11 — RATINGS

**Very good**: The segment of the works is considered to be “very good” if there are no irregularities or it shows only minor local deterioration considered to be normal or without consequence. The ancillary works are fully operational.

**Good**: The segment of the works is considered to be “good” if it shows only minor deterioration or irregularities that do not compromise the proper operation of its componants. The ancillary works show no risk of major malfunctioning and are able to operate as intended.

**Acceptable**: The segment of the works is considered to be “acceptable” if it shows deterioration requiring repair, without in the short term constituting a danger to the structure’s safety, but needs maintenance and restoration work in the short or medium term. It may also show irregularities that do not affect its short-term safety but require special monitoring and follow-up. The ancillary works show risks of malfunctioning and require repairs to ensure proper operation.

**Poor**: The segment of the works or the ancillary works are considered to be “poor” if they show one or more instances of serious deterioration that may compromise stability or render any of their components inoperative, or show serious irregularities likely to compromise the safety of the works.

**Undetermined**: It is impossible to form an opinion on the condition of the works and the ancillary works.
