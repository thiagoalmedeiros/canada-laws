# Code of ethics of physical therapists and physiotherapy technologists

*CQLR c C-26, r 197.1*

*Enabling statute: Professional Code (chapter C-26, s. 87)*

*Updated to 2026-01-01*

## I — GENERAL PROVISIONS

**1** This Code determines the duties and obligations that must be discharged by all members of the Ordre professionnel de la physiothérapie du Québec, regardless of the manner in which they engage in their professional activities and the circumstances in which they engage in them.

The duties and obligations under the Professional Code (chapter C-26) and its regulations are not modified in any manner owing to the fact that a member carries on professional activities within an organization, regardless of its legal form, or owing to the fact that a member uses information technologies.

**2** Members must take reasonable measures to ensure that persons who collaborate with them in the practice of the profession and any organization within which they carry on professional activities comply with the Professional Code (chapter C-26) and its regulations.

Members must not urge a person to perform an act that would contravene the Professional Code or one of its regulations or allow another person to do so.

## II — DUTIES AND OBLIGATIONS

### I — GENERAL DUTIES

**3** Members must act with dignity and refrain from using methods or adopting attitudes likely to tarnish the image of the profession.

**4** Members must have an irreproachable conduct on a physical, mental and emotional level toward all persons with whom they engage in the practice of their profession.

**5** Members must refrain from practising their profession if their state of health is an obstacle to doing so or in any conditions or circumstances that may compromise the quality of their professional services, or the dignity or image of the profession.

**6** Members must be respectful, courteous and moderate in their remarks, writings and acts, refrain from any form of harassment, and must not engage in physical or verbal abuse in the practice of their profession.

**7** Members must discharge their professional obligations with integrity.

To that end, members must, in particular, refrain from breaching the trust of others, voluntarily misleading them, betraying their good faith or using unfair practices.

Members must also refrain from taking credit for work that they did not perform.

**8** Members may not forgo or attempt to forgo their professional liability. It is prohibited in particular for members to:

(1) accept a waiver that relieves them, in whole or in part, of their professional liability for a fault committed in the practice of their profession;

(2) accept a waiver that relieves, in whole or in part, the organization through which they carry on their professional activities of the liability it may incur for a fault committed by them;

(3) invoke against a client the liability of the organization through which they carry on their activities.

**9** Members must refrain from performing any act involving collusion, corruption, malfeasance, breach of trust or influence peddling.

### II — DUTIES AND OBLIGATIONS TOWARDS CLIENTS

#### 1 — Quality of the professional relationship

**10** Members must, in the practice of their profession, identify themselves in accordance with the permit they hold. Members must, in particular, display that permit or their name and title at their workplace in full view of clients or, where unable to do so, members must wear a name tag indicating their name and title.

Members who practise another trade or profession must clearly indicate to their clients under which title they are providing their services.

**11** Members must seek to establish and maintain a relationship of trust with their clients.

**12** Members must not take advantage of a client’s vulnerability, in particular due to the client’s inexperience, personal condition or state of health.

**13** Members must respect the privacy of their clients, in particular by refraining from gathering information that is outside the scope required to provide professional services and by refraining from interfering in the personal affairs of their clients.

**14** Members must respect the personal values and convictions of their clients.

**15** During the professional relationship, members must not establish friendships likely to affect the quality of their professional services, or establish relations of an amorous or sexual nature with a client or someone close to the client. Members must not make remarks or gestures of a sexual nature to a client or someone close to the client.

The duration of the professional relationship is determined by taking into consideration, in particular, the nature of the issues and the duration of the professional services provided, the client’s vulnerability and the likelihood of having to provide professional services to the client again.

**16** Members must show reasonable diligence and availability in the practice of their profession.

**17** Members may not refuse or cease to provide professional services to a client without just and reasonable grounds. The following constitute, in particular, just and reasonable grounds:

(1) the inability to establish or maintain a relationship of trust with the client;

(2) a situation of real or apparent conflict of interest, or a circumstance whereby their professional independence could be called into question;

(3) an inducement by the client or someone close to the client to perform an act that is illegal or that contravenes the Professional Code (chapter C-26) or its regulations, including this code of ethics;

(4) non-compliance by the client with the conditions agreed upon for the provision of services;

(5) abusive behaviour by the client, which may manifest itself as harassment, threats, aggressive acts or remarks or acts of a sexual nature.

**18** Before ceasing to provide professional services to a client, members must provide reasonable notice to the client. Likewise, members must ensure that ceasing to provide services does not pose an imminent risk to the client’s health and is not unduly prejudicial to the client.

**19** Members must refrain from guaranteeing, directly or indirectly, recovery from an impairment or incapacity or the effectiveness of a treatment.

**20** Members must inform their clients as soon as possible of any complication or accident related to their professional services and immediately take the appropriate measures to limit any consequences thereof, where applicable.

**21** Members must at all times acknowledge a client’s right to consult another member, a member of another professional order or another competent person and, where applicable, facilitate their client’s efforts and collaborate with the person the client consults.

Members must at all times acknowledge the client’s right to obtain, from the supplier of his or her choice, any material, equipment or device.

**22** Members who act as an expert or assessor must:

(1) clearly, objectively and impartially inform the person who is the subject of the expert evaluation or assessment of the goals sought, the methods they intend to use to carry out the expert evaluation or assessment, the identity of the recipient of the expert or assessment report and the manner in which a copy of the report may be obtained;

(2) refrain from obtaining any information from that person or making any interpretations or comments not relevant to the expert evaluation or assessment;

(3) limit their report or recommendations and, if applicable, their deposition before the court to information relevant to the expert evaluation or assessment.

#### 2 — Consent

**23** Before providing a professional service, members must obtain the free and enlightened consent of their client or the client’s legal representative, where applicable.

Members must first provide the following information to the client and ensure that the client has understood this information:

(1) the objective, nature, relevance, terms and risks of the professional service that will be provided;

(2) the limitations and restrictions of the therapeutic procedures and treatment plan proposed, as well as their alternatives;

(3) the possibility of withdrawing consent at any time.

Where members who hold a physical therapist’s permit intend to proceed with cervical manipulation, they must, in addition to meeting the obligations referred to in the first and second paragraphs, obtain the written consent of the client.

**24** Members must ensure that the consent of the client remains free and enlightened throughout the time they are providing their professional services and recognize the client’s right to withdraw consent.

#### 3 — Quality of practice

**25** In the practice of their profession, members must take into account the conditions and restrictions specific to their category of permit, their knowledge, the extent of their proficiency and the means available to them.

**26** Members must practise their profession with competence. In the practice of their profession, members must base themselves on the latest developments in the practice of physiotherapy, good practices and standards that are generally recognized by science and physiotherapy practice.

**27** Members must develop their competencies and keep them up to date so that they are in step with developments in the profession.

**28** Before giving an opinion or advice, members must seek appropriate knowledge of the facts. They must refrain from expressing opinions or giving contradictory or incomplete advice.

**29** Before treating a client, members must perform an evaluation of the client or collect evaluative data, in accordance with their category of permit.

**30** Members must not provide disproportionate or inappropriate services in consideration of the needs of a client.

**31** Members must refrain from recommending that a client use, buy or lease any physiotherapy material, product, equipment or device whose use is not justified by the impairment, incapacity or treatment of the client.

**32** Where the interest of a client so requires, members must consult another member, a member of another professional order or another competent person or refer the client to one of those persons.

#### 4 — Independence and impartiality

**33** Members must subordinate their personal interests, those of an ally, those of the organization within which they carry on their professional activities or in which they have an interest and those of any other person carrying on activities within that organization, to those of their clients.

**34** Members must safeguard their professional independence at all times. They must, in particular, ignore any intervention or situation likely to affect their professional independence or that could influence the performance of their professional duties and obligations towards their clients.

**35** Members may not take advantage of their employer status or the office they hold to affect the professional independence of another member or a member of another professional order in their employ or under their authority.

**36** Except in emergencies or in cases which are manifestly not serious, members must refrain from treating themselves or from treating any person with whom there is a relationship that could prejudice the quality of their practice, in particular their spouse and children.

**37** Members must avoid placing themselves in a situation of conflict of interest, whether real or apparent. They must take appropriate steps to identify potential conflicts of interest and prevent any conflict of interest that may result from them.

Members have a conflict of interest in particular when they use the professional relationship for purposes other than those for which it is established or when there is a risk that their professional duties and obligations towards a client could be compromised by the member’s interests or those of another client, a former client or a third party.

When members carry on their professional activities within an organization, situations of conflict of interest must be assessed with respect to the organization and all its clients.

**38** As soon as members become aware that they are in a real or an apparent situation of conflict of interest, they must notify their client and agree, where applicable, on the appropriate measures to ensure that the situation does not cause prejudice to the client.

**39** Members who become aware of a real or an apparent situation of conflict of interest and who take appropriate measures must keep the following information and documents in the client’s record:

(1) the nature of the identified situation of conflict of interest;

(2) the appropriate measures that were taken and the reasons why those measures are expected to resolve the conflict of interest;

(3) the date and a description of the disclosure made to every client concerned and the document confirming the consent obtained, where applicable.

**40** Members who organize a training or information activity or who act as a resource person for that activity must declare to the participants and, where applicable, to any other person who collaborates in the preparation of the activity any direct or indirect interest in an organization involved in holding the activity.

**41** Members must refrain from obtaining an unjustified or illicit benefit for themselves or someone else.

In particular, members may not, out of complacency or for any other reason, falsify or destroy in whole or in part a receipt, a report, a document, a statement of fees or costs, or a file.

**42** Members must refrain from receiving, paying or agreeing to pay any benefit, rebate or commission in connection with the practice of their profession, except for the usual tokens of thanks or gifts of small value.

**43** Members must not urge anyone in a pressing or repeated manner to retain their professional services or those of another person.

#### 5 — Confidentiality and professional secrecy

**44** Members must preserve the secrecy of all confidential information that becomes known to them in the practice of their profession.

Members may be released from the obligation of professional secrecy only by the authorization of their client or where so ordered or expressly authorized by law.

For the purpose of obtaining such authorization, members must inform their clients of the possible implications of lifting professional secrecy.

**45** In order to preserve professional secrecy, members must, in particular,

(1) refrain from accessing or consulting information of a confidential nature without authorization or when it is not necessary for the exercise of their profession;

(2) refrain from making, in particular on social media, indiscreet remarks concerning a client or the services rendered to the client or from revealing that a person has requested their services;

(3) take reasonable means with respect to the persons with whom they collaborate to maintain professional secrecy regarding any confidential information;

(4) refrain from using confidential information to the detriment of a client, for a purpose other than that for which it was initially collected without the prior written consent of the client, or with a view to obtaining, directly or indirectly, a benefit for themselves or another person;

(5) take reasonable means to preserve the professional secrecy of all confidential information when they or the persons collaborating with them use information technologies;

(6) record in the client’s record any communication to a third party, with or without the client’s consent, of information protected by professional secrecy.

**46** Members providing professional services to a group of persons must inform the members of the group of the possibility that some aspect of their private life or that of another person may be disclosed and instruct them as to how such disclosures may be prevented and each person’s right to privacy may be protected.

**47** Where members ask a client to disclose confidential information, they must fully inform the client of the reason for such a request and the uses that could be made of the information thus disclosed.

**48** Members who, in accordance with the third paragraph of section 60.4 of the Professional Code (chapter C-26), communicate information that is protected by professional secrecy must:

(1) communicate only such information as necessary to achieve the purpose for which the information is communicated;

(2) communicate the information immediately using the most effective means in the circumstances;

(3) indicate during the communication that the information is protected by professional secrecy;

(4) enter in the record as soon as possible the subject of the communication, the reasons for communicating that information, the date and time the information was communicated, the name of the person to whom the information was communicated and the method of communication used.

#### 6 — Access to records and corrections

**49** Where members practise their profession in an environment subject to a law that provides clients with rights of access and correction, they must comply with the record access and correction rules set out in that law and facilitate their application.

In other cases, members must comply with the record access and correction rules set out in the Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector (chapter P-39.1) and facilitate their application. Those rules are completed by the special provisions of this Subdivision.

**50** Members must respond, with diligence and not later than 30 days after receipt thereof, to any written request from a client to examine or obtain a copy of a document concerning that client in any record established in respect of the client.

**51** Members who receive a correction request from a client must notify the client of the client’s right to prepare written comments and file them in the record.

**52** Upon receipt of a client’s comments to be filed in the client’s record, members must, with diligence and not later than 30 days after receipt thereof, file them in the record and transmit to the client written confirmation that they have done so.

**53** Members who refuse to grant a request for access or correction must enter the reasons for the refusal in the record of the client and file a copy of the decision sent to the person who made the request.

#### 7 — Fees

**54** Members must charge and accept fair and reasonable fees. Fees are considered fair and reasonable where they are warranted by the circumstances and proportionate to the professional services provided, having regard to, among other things,

(1) the member’s experience and specific expertise;

(2) the time required to provide the professional services, their specific nature and the difficulties encountered.

**55** Members may only charge fees for professional services provided.

**56** Members must refrain from demanding or receiving advance payment of their professional fees.

Members may however claim reasonable administrative fees for appointments that are missed or cancelled by a client according to predetermined conditions that are agreed upon in writing with the client.

**57** Members must inform their clients of the approximate and expected cost of their fees and other costs as well as the terms and conditions of payment. Members must inform clients immediately of any modifications to fees and costs.

**58** Members must provide their clients with a written statement of the fees and costs being charged. Members must provide the explanations necessary to understand the statement as well as the terms and conditions of payment.

**59** With regard to the collection of accounts, members must:

(1) refrain from charging interest on outstanding accounts unless they have a written agreement with a client to that effect, in which case, the rate charged must be reasonable;

(2) ensure that the person appointed to collect accounts proceeds with tact and moderation, and with respect for confidentiality and the debt collection practices authorized by law.

**60** Members must exhaust all other means at their disposal to obtain payment of their fees and other claimed costs before instituting legal proceedings.

**61** Members may share their fees only insofar as it corresponds to a distribution of the services provided and responsibilities assumed, and does not affect their professional independence.

### III — DUTIES AND OBLIGATIONS TOWARDS THE PUBLIC

#### 1 — Research

**62** Divisions I and II of Chapter II apply, with the necessary modifications, to members who participate, in any manner whatsoever, in research involving human beings.

Divisions I and II of Chapter II are completed by the provisions of this Subdivision.

**63** Before undertaking research, members must consider all foreseeable consequences that their research and work may have on participants and society. For that purpose, members must, in particular,

(1) consult the persons likely to help them in deciding whether to undertake the research or in taking measures intended to reduce the risks to the participants;

(2) ensure that the persons collaborating with them on the research respect the physical and psychological integrity of the participants.

**64** Members must ensure that the research is approved and monitored by a research ethics committee that complies with the standards in force, particularly in regard to the composition of the committee and its operating methods.

**65** Members must comply with generally accepted standards of ethics and scientific integrity.

**66** Members must ensure in particular that each participant:

(1) has been informed of the research’s objectives, benefits, risks and inconveniences, the members’ intention to use a technique or treatment that has not been sufficiently tested, the benefits that could be derived from standard treatments, if applicable, as well as the fact, if such is the case, that members will derive a benefit from the participant’s participation in the research;

(2) has given free and enlightened consent before beginning to participate in the research or when any change is made to the protocol;

(3) has been informed that their consent is revocable at any time;

(4) has been informed of the measures taken to preserve the confidentiality of the information collected as part of the research.

#### 2 — Advertising and public statements

**67** For the purposes of this Subdivision, **public statement** means any written or verbal communication intended for the public, in particular using signs, business cards or texts published during conferences or courses, or in the media, including social media.

**68** Any advertisement or public statement made by members or on their behalf must mention their name and their physical therapist or physiotherapy technologist title.

**69** Any advertisement or public statement made by members or on their behalf must be characterized by objectivity and moderation.

**70** Members must display professionalism and avoid tarnishing the image of the profession or imparting a mercantile character to it in any advertisements or public statements made by members or on their behalf.

**71** When an advertisement or a public statement made by members or on their behalf refers to activities that are reserved for members, it must not lead others to believe that those activities are carried out, directly or indirectly, by a person who is not authorized to engage in those activities.

**72** In advertisements or public statements made by members or on their behalf, members may not claim specific qualities or skills relating, in particular, to their level of competence or to the extent or efficiency of their services unless they can substantiate those claims.

**73** Members may not engage in making advertisements or public statements or allow others to do so on their behalf, by any means whatsoever, including on social media, which:

(1) are false, incomplete, deceptive or liable to mislead the public;

(2) are not based on standards that are generally recognized by science and physiotherapy practice;

(3) denigrate or discredit another person or disparage a service or product provided by that person;

(4) promote treatments or care whose efficacy or scientific value has not been recognized;

(5) recommend that the public buy, lease or use a product or service that is not related to physiotherapy;

(6) promote any discount, rebate or free products or services;

(7) are likely to influence persons who may be vulnerable, particularly due to their inexperience, their personal condition or their state of health.

**74** Members who advertise fees or a price must:

(1) specify the nature and extent of the services included in the fees or price;

(2) indicate if additional fees or services not included in the fees or price may be charged or required;

(3) keep the fees or the price in effect for a minimum period of 90 days after the advertisement was last broadcast or published.

Members may however agree with their clients on an amount lower than the one advertised.

#### 3 — Graphic symbol of the Order

**75** Members may use the graphic symbol of the Order in the following cases:

(1) in their communications;

(2) on a business card;

(3) on a website;

(4) on any document that must be given to clients in relation to physiotherapy services.

For that purpose, they must use the following wording: “member of the Ordre professionnel de la physiothérapie du Québec” or “OPPQ member”.

**76** Members who use the graphic symbol of the Order must ensure that its reproduction is true to the original held by the secretary of the Order and is not represented in such a manner as to imply that the advertisement or the documents come from the Order or are approved by the Order.

**77** Members must keep a complete copy of all advertisements and public statements, where applicable, for a period of 3 years after the advertisement or public statement was last broadcast or published. Such copies must be provided to a representative of the Order upon request.

### IV — DUTIES AND OBLIGATIONS TOWARDS THE PROFESSION

**78** Members must promote improvements in the quality of physiotherapy services and support measures likely to make those services more accessible.

**79** Members must participate, to the extent of their resources, qualifications and experience, in the development and quality of professional practice, in particular by assisting students or sharing their knowledge with other members or members of another professional order.

**80** Members must collaborate with other members, members of other professional orders and any other competent person.

When consulted by such persons, members must provide their opinions and recommendations as soon as possible or promptly notify those persons of their inability to do so if they cannot.

**81** Members must notify the Order when they have reason to believe that a person who is not a member is using the titles or abbreviations reserved for members of the Order or is illegally practising activities reserved for members.

Similarly, members must notify the Order when they have reason to believe that an applicant seeking admission to the Order does not meet the conditions for the issue of a permit or registration on the roll.

**82** Members must notify the syndic when they have reason to believe in the existence of a situation that is likely to cause prejudice to the competence or the integrity of another member.

Similarly, members must notify the syndic when they have reason to believe that the conduct of another member of the Order is contrary to the Professional Code (chapter C-26) or one of its regulations.

**83** Members must reply fully and truthfully to any verbal or written request made by an employee of the Order or a person exercising functions under the Professional Code (chapter C-26) or its regulations, within the time and using the means of communication determined by that employee or person.

**84** Members must make themselves available for any meeting required by a person exercising functions under the Professional Code (chapter C-26) or its regulations.

**85** Members must respect any commitment entered into with the board of directors, the executive committee, the secretary of the Order, a syndic or the professional inspection committee as well as with any committee to which the board of directors has delegated powers in accordance with the Professional Code (chapter C-26).

Members must comply with the decisions rendered by those persons or bodies.

**86** Members must not influence, intimidate, threaten or harass a person or carry out retaliatory measures against a person because the person has:

(1) reported or intends to report conduct contrary to the Professional Code (chapter C-26) or one of its regulations;

(2) collaborated or intends to collaborate in an inquiry or an inspection.

**87** Members who are notified of an inquiry or a complaint concerning their professional conduct may not discuss that inquiry or complaint, directly or indirectly, with the person who requested the inquiry or with any other person involved in the inquiry without obtaining written permission from the syndic or the syndic in charge of the inquiry.

## III — FINAL PROVISIONS
