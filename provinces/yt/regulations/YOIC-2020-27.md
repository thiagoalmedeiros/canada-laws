YOIC 2020/27

*Made under the Legal Profession Act, 2017*

### Remuneration of public representatives

1 The remuneration prescribed under subsection 5(3) of

the Act for each public representative is the honoraria,

and the amounts in respect of the payment and

reimbursement of expenses, that would be payable to

the public representative if the Executive were assigned

to Category D as described in Schedule A to Policy 1.8 of

the Government of Yukon’s General Administration

Manual.

### Regulated professions

2 The following regulated professions are prescribed for

the purposes of paragraph 31(1)(c) of the Act:

(a) chartered professional accountant;

(b) professional engineer.

### Indigenous court workers

3 (1) A person is prescribed under paragraph 32(1)(a) of

the Act to be an Indigenous court worker if a Yukon First

Nation employs the person in, or has appointed or

assigned the person to, a position in which they perform

one or more of the activities described in

paragraphs (2)(a) to (h).

(2) To the extent that any of the following activities

would, but for paragraph 31(1)(b) of the Act and this

subsection, be a legal service, it is a legal service that

Indigenous court workers may provide under

paragraph 32(1)(b) of the Act:

(a) helping individuals to understand

(i) the justice system and how it works,

(ii) if they are charged with an offence, the nature

of the charge,

(iii) court documents and forms, and

(iv) any directions or conditions that a court

imposes;

(b) assisting individuals with applications for legal

aid;

(c) supporting individuals in court;

(d) assisting with bail applications by providing to

the court information about the affected

individual and the affected community and any

other information the court requires;

(e) responding to requests for Gladue information;

(f) making referrals to other community support

services, such as counselling or alcohol and drug

treatment;

(g) helping justice system officials to understand

(i) the culture, values and customs of Indigenous

people, and

(ii) individuals’ social and economic situations;

(h) in relation to the justice system and its

interaction with Indigenous individuals and

communities

(i) improving communication,

(ii) creating community partnerships,

(iii) giving presentations and workshops, and

(iv) working on committees.
