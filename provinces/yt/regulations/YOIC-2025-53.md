## PART 1

## INTERPRETATION AND APPLICATION

### Definitions

1 In this Regulation

“ accessible campsite ” means a campsite designated as

an accessible campsite under paragraph 33(a);

« emplacement de camping accessible »

“ annual camping voucher ” means an annual camping

voucher issued under subsection 44(3); « coupon de

camping annuel »

“ backcountry campground ” means a campground that

is not accessible by road; « terrain de camping de

l’arrière -pays »

“ camp ” means to stay overnight in a park on a short

term, temporary basis; « camper »

“ campground ” means an area within a park that is

developed or maintained by the Minister as a place for

camping by the public; « terrain de camping »

“ camping accommodation unit ” means a vehicle or

moveable structure that may be used as temporary

accommodation or shelter, including, without limitation,

the following:

(a) a tent,

(b) a tent-trailer,

(c) a motor home,

(d) a camping trailer,

(e) a truck camper,

(f) a camper van,

(g) any vehicle when used for accommodation;

« unité d’hébergement de camping »

“ campsite ” means an area within a campground that is

designated by the Minister as a place where one or more

camping accommodation units may be parked or placed;

« emplacement de camping »

“ campsite permit ” means a park permit established by

paragraph 39(1)(b); « permis de camping »

“ campsite post ” means a post at a campsite provided

for the display of a campsite permit; « piquet

d’affichage »

“ commercial or large group recreation permit ” means

a park permit established by paragraph 39(1)(d);

« permis de loisirs commerciaux ou en grands

groupes »

“ day use area ” means

(a) a park established for the purpose of providing

a recreation site for day use activities,

(b) a designated beach,

(c) a designated swimming area, and

(d) the following park facilities:

(i) a boat launch,

(ii) a dock,

(iii) a cook shelter,

(iv) a playground,

but does not include a campsite; « aire de fréquentation

diurne »

“ designated ” except where a contrary intention

appears, means, with respect to all or part of a park, or

an area, place, thing, time, period of time or manner,

designated

(a) by a sign posted under section 5,

(b) by the management plan for a park, or

(c) by the interim guidelines for a park; « désigné »

“ domestic animal ” means an animal of a species of

vertebrates that has been domesticated by humans so

as to live and breed in a tame condition and depend on

humans for survival, but does not include

(a) livestock, or

(b) an animal of a species or type that is wild by

nature but has been domesticated; « animal

domestique »

“ drone ” means an aircraft that

(a) is not designed to convey a person, and

(b) is operated by any means including one or more

of the following:

(i) remote control,

(ii) pre-programmed software,

(iii) onboard computer autonomy or artificial

intelligence; « drone »

“ firearm ” includes any device capable of propelling a

projectile by means of an explosion, compressed gas,

springs or strings, including, without limitation, a rifle,

shotgun, handgun, spring gun, air rifle, BB-gun,

longbow, crossbow and slingshot; « arme à feu »

“ game bird ” has the same meaning as in the Wildlife

Act ; « gibier à plumes »

“ general permit ” means a park permit established by

paragraph 39(1)(e); « permis général »

“ group campsite ” means a campsite designated as a

group campsite under paragraph 33(b); « emplacement

de camping de groupe »

“ interim guidelines ” means interim guidelines

established for a park under subsection 20(2) of the Act

that are in effect; « lignes directrices provisoires »

“ livestock ” has the same meaning as in the Animal

Protection and Control Act ; « bétail »

“ management plan ” means

(a) a management plan for a park approved and in

force under section 23 of the Act, as amended

from time to time, and

(b) a management plan for a park approved and in

effect under a settlement agreement, as

amended from time to time; « plan directeur »

“ motor vehicle ” has the same meaning as in the Motor

Vehicles Act ; « véhicule automobile »

“ multiple use recreation land use zone ” means a land

use zone prescribed by paragraph 10(1)(a); « zone

d’utilisation — loisirs multiples »

“ natural land use zone ” means a land use zone

prescribed by paragraph 10(1)(b); « zone d’utilisation

du sol — milieu naturel »

“ nightly camping voucher ” means a nightly camping

voucher issued under subsection 44(3); « coupon de

camping à la nuitée »

“ occupant ” means, in respect of a campsite,

(a) the holder of the campsite permit for the

campsite, and

(b) a person who is camping at the campsite under

the authority of a campsite permit;

« occupant »

“ off-road vehicle ” has the same meaning as in the

Territorial Lands (Yukon) Act ; « véhicule hors route »

“ organized recreational activity ” means a recreational

activity organized by or on behalf of

(a) one or more individuals for, or with an

expectation of, gain or profit, or

(b) an organization whether or not for, or with an

expectation of expectation of, gain or profit,

but does not include a special event; « activité de loisirs

organisée »

“ park facility ” means any of the following:

(a) a structure,

(b) a building,

(c) infrastructure,

(d) an improvement,

(e) an installation,

(f) a designated trail that is maintained by the

Government of Yukon; « installation de parc »

“ personal recreational activity ” means a recreational

activity undertaken by an individual or a group of

individuals, including, without limitation, groups of

friends, family members or acquaintances, but does not

include

(a) an organized recreational activity, or

(b) a special event; « activité de loisirs

personnelle »

“ quiet camping area ” means an area in a campground

designated as a quiet camping area under section 36;

« aire de tranquillité »

“ quiet hours ” means, in respect of a campground, the

period beginning at 11:00 p.m. on a day and ending at

7:00 a.m. on the following day; « heures de

tranquillité »

“ reservable campsite ” means a campsite designated as

a reservable campsite under paragraph 33(d);

« emplacement de camping réservable »

“ resident ” means a person who, in the settled routine of

their life, makes their home, resides and is ordinarily

present in Yukon; « résident »

“ small game animal ” has the same meaning as in the

Wildlife Act ; « petit gibier »

“ snowmobile ” has the same meaning as in the Motor

Vehicles Act ; « motoneige »

“ special event ” means an event or activity that family

members or members of a club or other organization

may attend, or that the public is invited to attend

through advertisements or other forms of public notice,

that

(a) may impact or interfere with the normal

operation of a park or the public’s normal use

and enjoyment of a park because of

(i) the nature of the event or activity,

(ii) the number of people attending it, or

(iii) its location in the park,

(b) requires the exclusive use of an area of a park,

or

(c) involves conduct that does not normally take

place in the area of a park or in the park facility

where the event or activity will occur;

« événement spécial »

“ special feature land use zone ” means a land use zone

prescribed by paragraph 10(1)(c); « zone d’utilisation —

attributs spéciaux »

“ specified camping accommodation unit

campsite ” means a campsite designated under

paragraph 33(c); « emplacement de camping pour unité

d’hébergement de camping spécifiée »

“ stop ” means, in respect of a vehicle,

(a) when required, a complete cessation of vehicular

movement, and

(b) when prohibited, any halting, even momentarily,

of the vehicle, whether occupied or not, except

when necessary

(i) to avoid conflict with other traffic, or

(ii) to comply with the directions of a park officer

or other peace officer, a sign posted under

section 5 or a traffic control device within the

meaning of the Motor Vehicles Act ;

« arrêter »

“ subsisting mineral interests land use zone ” means a

land use zone prescribed by paragraph 10(1)(e); « zone

d’utilisation — intérêts miniers subsistants »

“ unoccupied ” means, in respect of a campsite, that no

occupant is camping at the campsite; « inoccupé »

“ vehicle ” means a device in, on, or by which a person or

thing may be transported or drawn on a highway within

the meaning of the Motor Vehicles Act ; « véhicule »

“ wildland land use zone ” means a land use zone

prescribed by paragraph 10(1)(d); « zone d’utilisation —

milieu sauvage »

“ wildlife ” has the same meaning as in the Wildlife Act ,

except that it does not include livestock; « animal

sauvage »

“ wildlife attractant ” means anything that may attract

wildlife including, without limitation, the following

things:

(a) domestic animals and livestock,

(b) animal excrement,

(c) offal,

(d) food, including pet food,

(e) dishes, including pet dishes,

(f) any items associated with food preparation or

dishwashing, including pots, pans and cooking

devices,

(g) bottles and cans,

(h) coolers,

(i) garbage, wrappings, food waste and compost,

(j) chemical attractants,

(k) containers of any items listed in paragraphs (b)

to (j). « attractif d’animal sauvage »

### Prescribed public nuisances

2 For the purpose of section 59 of the Act, the following

things are prescribed as public nuisances:

(a) intimidating, threatening or assaulting a person

in a park, including a park officer;

(b) unreasonably interfering with a person’s use and

enjoyment of a park or a park facility.

### Application

3 (1) This Regulation applies to every park except as

otherwise expressly provided in

(a) the order establishing a park; or

(b) the interim guidelines for a park.

(2) If a provision of this Regulation is inconsistent with

or in conflict with a provision of the Herschel Island Park

Regulations , the provision of the Herschel Island Park

Regulations prevails to the extent of the conflict or

inconsistency.

### Recognition of existing aboriginal and treaty rights

4 The existing aboriginal and treaty rights of indigenous

peoples in areas established as parks are recognized.

## PART 2

## GENERAL

### Regulation and management in accordance with signs, management plans and interim guidelines

5 (1) The Minister may post signs in a park

(a) regulating or prohibiting developments, uses or

activities in a park or an area of a park or the

occupation of land within a park or area of a

park;

(b) designating all or part of a park, or an area,

place, thing, time, period of time or manner, for

the purposes of a provision of this Regulation;

(c) giving effect to any provision of this Regulation

that refers to the posting of a sign; or

(d) setting out instructions, directions, conditions,

restrictions or requirements with respect to any

matter referred to in paragraphs (a) to (c).

(2) A sign referred to in subsection (1) must be posted

at the place or places in the park that the Minister

considers will bring the content of the sign to the notice

of the public or the persons likely to be affected by it.

(3) The existence of a sign referred to in subsection (1)

is proof, in the absence of evidence to the contrary, that

it was properly posted under this Regulation.

(4) A person must comply with a regulation, prohibition,

instruction, direction, condition, restriction or

requirement set out on a sign posted under

subsection (1).

(5) Despite any other provision of this Regulation, if the

management plan or interim guidelines for a park

provide that a development, use or activity is not

permitted in a land use zone in the park, a person is not

authorized to undertake the development, use or activity

in that land use zone.

(6) Despite any other provision of this Regulation, if the

management plan or interim guidelines for a park

provide that a park permit is required to undertake a

development, use or activity in a land use zone in the

park, a park permit is required to undertake the

development, use or activity in that land use zone.

(7) Despite any other provision of this Regulation, if the

management plan or interim guidelines for a park

establish conditions that are applicable to the

undertaking of a development, use or activity in a land

use zone in the park, a person must comply with the

conditions in undertaking the development, use or

activity in that land use zone.

(8) If the management plan or interim guidelines for a

park establish an exception referred to in

subsection 8(1), 9(1), 15(2), 20(6), 21(5) or 31(2),

subparagraph 32(2)(b)(i) or subsection 32(3) a person

must, in acting under or relying on the exception,

comply with any applicable regulation, prohibition,

instruction, direction, condition, restriction or

requirement set out in the management plan or interim

guidelines.

(9) If the management plan or interim guidelines for a

park designate an area where camping is authorized as

referred to in subparagraph 32(2)(b)(ii), a person must

comply with any regulation, prohibition, instruction,

direction, condition, restriction or requirement set out in

the management plan or interim guidelines in respect of

camping in that area.

### Open days and hours

6 (1) The Minister may, by posting a sign under section 5,

fix the days and hours of the day during which a park,

park facility, day use area or other area of the park is

open to the public.

(2) Except as otherwise provided by a sign posted under

subsection (1), a day use area is not open to the public

during the period beginning at 11:00 p.m. each day and

ending at 7:00 a.m. on the following day.

(3) A person must not enter or remain in a park, park

facility, day use area or other area within a park during

any period when the park, park facility, day use area or

other area of the park is not open to the public.

### Requirement to register

7 (1) The Minister may require visitors to register before

entering a park or part of a park.

(2) If the management plan or interim guidelines for a

park provides that visitors are to register before

entering the park or a part of the park, the Minister must

require visitors to register in accordance with the

management plan or interim guidelines.

(3) A person must comply with a requirement to

register referred to in this section.

### Authorization to undertake certain activities without park permit

8 (1) Except as otherwise provided by this Regulation, a

sign posted under section 5, or the management plan or

interim guidelines for a park, a person may undertake

the following activities in a park without a park permit:

(a) a personal recreational activity if the activity is

undertaken by an individual alone or as part of

a group consisting of 12 or fewer individuals;

(b) an organized recreational activity if all the

following conditions are met:

(i) the activity is undertaken by an individual

alone or as part of a group consisting of 12 or

fewer individuals,

(ii) the activity is undertaken in a recreation park,

a campground other than a backcountry

campground, or a day use area,

(iii) the activity is undertaken between the hours

of 7:00 a.m. and 9:00 p.m.,

(iv) the activity is not more than four hours in

duration,

(v) the activity is not undertaken on more than

seven consecutive days,

(vi) the activity is not undertaken on more than

14 days within any 28-day period;

(c) travelling through a park;

(d) harvesting wild fruits and berries for personal

consumption;

(e) pulling and removing invasive plant species;

(f) taking photographs or videos without an

expectation of gain or profit, where the taking of

the photographs or videos does not involve the

use of a camera that is triggered remotely;

(g) collecting deadfall to make a small warming or

cooking fire in a place other than a recreation

park, special feature land use zone, campground

or day use area.

(2) Nothing in this section affects a requirement to

comply with any law relating to undertaking an activity

for which a park permit is not required.

### Property brought into a park

9 (1) Except as authorized by this Regulation, a sign

posted under section 5, the management plan or interim

guidelines for a park or a general permit or other park

permit,

(a) a person must not leave property unattended in

a park for more than 24 hours, except a motor

vehicle, its contents and any trailer attached to

the motor vehicle, if the motor vehicle is parked

in a designated parking area where parking is

permitted for more than 24 hours;

(b) a person must not leave property, other than a

motor vehicle within the meaning of the Motor

Vehicles Act , its contents and any trailer

attached to the motor vehicle, in a designated

parking area within a park;

(c) a person must not park a motor vehicle in a

designated parking area within a park for more

than 24 consecutive hours unless they are in the

park; and

(d) a person must take all the property that they

brought into a park with them when they leave

the park.

(2) If a person leaves, parks or stores a vehicle,

camping accommodation unit or other personal

property, including a wildlife attractant, in a park

contrary to this Regulation, a park officer may take

reasonable steps to have the vehicle, camping

accommodation unit or other personal property,

including a wildlife attractant, removed to be stored at

a place that the officer considers suitable.

(3) The owner of a vehicle, camping accommodation

unit or other property, including a wildlife attractant,

removed and stored under subsection (2) is liable for the

reasonable costs incurred by the Government of Yukon

in removing and storing the vehicle, camping

accommodation unit or other property, and the costs are

a debt due to the Government of Yukon by the owner

and may be recovered, with costs, by action of the

Government of Yukon in a court of competent

jurisdiction.

## PART 3

## LAND USE ZONES

### Land use zones prescribed

10 (1) The following land use zones are prescribed:

(a) multiple use recreation land use zone;

(b) natural land use zone;

(c) special feature land use zone;

(d) wildland land use zone;

(e) subsisting mineral interests land use zone.

(2) The purpose of the multiple use recreation land use

zone is to allow for a variety of recreational

developments, uses and activities that may

accommodate a high number of users.

(3) The purpose of the natural land use zone is to

conserve a natural landscape in which people can

connect to nature by engaging in recreational activities

in an environment that is otherwise largely undeveloped.

(4) The purpose of the special feature land use zone is

to preserve and protect important natural, historical or

cultural values, resources, features or processes.

(5) The purpose of the wildland land use zone is to

protect a largely undisturbed natural landscape to

(a) protect its biodiversity and ecological integrity;

(b) provide for continuity of indigenous peoples’

ways of life; and

(c) allow for people to engage in recreational

activities in an undeveloped natural landscape.

(6) The purpose of the subsisting mineral interests land

use zone is to allow for developments, uses and

activities, and the occupation of land considered

necessary for a person’s continued exercise of rights

under the Placer Mining Act or the Quartz Mining Act , in

respect of a mineral claim that is a valid and subsisting

interest in land referred to in section 16 of the Act.

### Land use zones in parks

11 (1) The management plan or interim guidelines for an

ecological reserve may provide for one or more areas

within the park to be designated as any of the following:

(a) a special feature land use zone;

(b) a wildland land use zone.

(2) The management plan or interim guidelines for a

natural environment park may provide for one or more

areas within the park to be designated as any of the

following:

(a) a multiple use recreation land use zone;

(b) a natural land use zone;

(c) a special feature land use zone;

(d) a wildland land use zone;

(e) subject to subsection (5), a subsisting mineral

interests land use zone.

(3) The management plan or interim guidelines for a

wilderness preserve may provide for one or more areas

within the park to be designated as any of the following:

(a) a special feature land use zone;

(b) a wildland land use zone.

(4) The management plan or interim guidelines for a

recreation park may provide for one or more areas

within the park to be designated as any of the following:

(a) a multiple use recreation land use zone;

(b) a natural land use zone;

(c) a special feature land use zone.

(5) The boundaries of an area designated as a

subsisting mineral interests land use zone must exactly

match the boundaries of the mineral claim or, in the case

of mineral claims that are adjoining, the mineral claims

that constitute the valid and subsisting interest in land

referred to in section 16 of the Act.

(6) Except as otherwise provided by the management

plan or interim guidelines for a park

(a) all areas within an ecological reserve are

designated as a special feature land use zone;

(b) subject to subsection (7), all areas within a

natural environment park are designated as a

natural land use zone;

(c) all areas within a recreation park are designated

as a multiple use recreation land use zone; and

(d) all areas within a wilderness preserve are

designated as a wildland land use zone.

(7) If lands in which a mineral claim that constitutes a

valid and subsisting interest in land referred to in

section 16 of the Act are included within a natural

environment park, the area of the park that is within the

boundaries of the mineral claim or, in the case of

mineral claims that are adjoining, the mineral claims, is

designated as a subsisting mineral interests land use

zone.

### Developments, uses and activities in land use zones

12 (1) Subject to subsection (2), the management plan or

interim guidelines establishing a land use zone for a park

may set out

(a) the developments, uses and activities that may

be permitted in that land use zone; and

(b) the developments, uses and activities that may

not be permitted in that land use zone.

(2) The developments, uses and activities that may be

permitted or may not be permitted in a land use zone as

set out in the management plan or interim guidelines for

a park must be consistent with the purpose of the land

use zone.

(3) The management plan or interim guidelines

establishing a land use zone for a park may

(a) establish whether a park permit is required to

undertake a development, use or activity that

may be permitted in the land use zone; and

(b) establish conditions applicable to the

undertaking of a development, use or activity

that may be permitted in the land use zone,

whether or not a park permit is required to

undertake the development, use or activity.

## PART 4

## CARE AND PRESERVATION OF PARKS

### Duty to restore park

13 (1) A person who does not hold a park permit, must,

before leaving a park, restore the areas of the park and

park facilities used by the person as nearly as possible

to the same condition that they were in before the

person used them.

(2) The holder of a park permit must carry out the

mitigation or restoration required by subsection 45(1) of

the Act before leaving the park unless otherwise

authorized by a park permit.

### Prohibitions regarding park facilities

14 Except as authorized by a park permit, a person must

not

(a) move, remove, alter, damage, destroy or affix

anything to a park facility; or

(b) use a park facility for a purpose other than its

intended purpose.

### Signs, notices, plaques and monuments

15 (1) A person must not, without lawful authority,

remove, alter, damage, destroy or deface a sign,

signboard, notice, plaque or monument in a park.

(2) Except as authorized by this Regulation, the

management plan or interim guidelines for a park or a

park permit, a person must not display or post a sign,

signboard, notice or advertisement in a park.

### Firewood

16 (1) All wood located within a park, other than firewood

brought into a park by a person, is the property of the

Government of Yukon.

(2) Except as provided by paragraph 8(1)(g), a person

must not use any wood located within a park for a fire

except

(a) firewood provided by the Government of Yukon

for that purpose; or

(b) firewood brought into the park for use as

firewood.

(3) A person must not remove firewood provided by the

Government of Yukon from a park.

(4) A person must not use firewood provided by the

Government of Yukon in a campground or day use area

except within that campground or day use area.

(5) A person must not accumulate more than 0.113 m 3

of firewood provided by the Government of Yukon.

### Release of flora, fauna or fungi

17 (1) Except as authorized by a park permit, a person

must not release, or permit to be released, in a park

(a) any species of flora, fauna or fungi, whether or

not it is native to the park; or

(b) any reproductive propagules or larva of an

organism referred to in paragraph (a).

(2) Except as authorized by a park permit, a person

must not bring anything into a park that may carry any

species of flora, fauna or fungi that is not native to the

park or that are invasive species.

### Protection of water

18 (1) Except as authorized under an Act, a person must

not obstruct, divert or otherwise interfere with a body of

water or waterway within a park.

(2) A person must not drain or empty or otherwise

introduce water, other than potable water, into a body

of water or waterway within a park.

(3) A person must not clean a vehicle, off-road vehicle,

snowmobile, machinery, motorized equipment or a tool

in a park.

(4) Except in a campsite, a person must not bathe or

wash clothing or cooking or other kitchen items or clean

fish within 30 m of a well, water holding tank or water

pump.

### Litter and waste

19 (1) A person must not bring litter within the meaning of

section 61 of the Act or other waste into a park for

disposal.

(2) For the purposes of section 61(1)(c) of the Act, the

manner in which litter or a type of litter may be

abandoned or discarded in a park or an area of a park

may be designated.

(3) A person must not empty sewage or other waste

from any holding tank, including a holding tank in a

camping accommodation unit, vehicle, boat or other

conveyance, at any place in a park, including in any

toilet, litter receptacle or other park facility.

(4) A person must not dispose of human waste in a

campground or day use area except in a toilet provided

for that purpose by the Government of Yukon.

## PART 5

## ANIMALS, FIRES AND PUBLIC HEALTH AND

## SAFETY

### Control of animals

20 (1) A person must not bring a domestic animal or

livestock into a park or area of a park that is designated

as a park or area in which a person is not permitted to

bring a domestic animal or livestock.

(2) A person must not bring a type of animal into a park

or area of a park that is designated as a park or area in

which a person is not permitted to bring that type of

animal.

(3) A person must not leave a domestic animal or

livestock that is in their possession or control unattended

in a park.

(4) Except as authorized by a general permit, a person

must not bring livestock into any of the following areas:

(a) a campground;

(b) a day use area;

(c) any area within a recreation park.

(5) Subject to subsection (6), a person must keep

domestic animals and livestock that are in their

possession or control physically controlled in the

following areas:

(a) a campground;

(b) a day use area;

(c) any area within a recreation park;

(d) on or within 50 m of a trail;

(e) an area designated as an area where domestic

animals and livestock must be physically

controlled.

(6) Except as otherwise provided by a sign posted under

section 5 or the management plan or interim guidelines

for a park, a person who has possession or control of a

domestic animal in an area of a park, other than an area

referred to in paragraphs (5)(a) to (e), must ensure that

the domestic animal is, at all times, either

(a) physically controlled; or

(b) under the person’s active control.

(7) For the purposes of subsections (5) and (6), an

animal is not physically controlled unless the animal is

(a) on a leash held by the person;

(b) securely tethered to an immovable stationary

object; or

(c) confined within a camping accommodation unit,

crate, vehicle or other thing capable of keeping

the animal confined.

(8) For the purposes of subsection (6), an animal is not

under a person’s active control unless

(a) the animal can be seen by the person; and

(b) the animal promptly complies with the person’s

commands.

(9) A person who has a domestic animal or livestock in

their possession or control must ensure that the

domestic animal or livestock does not

(a) chase, harass, bite or injure any person, another

domestic animal, other livestock or wildlife;

(b) become a nuisance to or cause unreasonable

disturbance to any person, another domestic

animal, other livestock or wildlife;

(c) gain access to an area where a person is not

permitted to bring the domestic animal or

livestock; or

(d) damage a park facility or natural resources.

(10) A person who has possession or control of a

domestic animal or livestock in a park must clean up the

animal’s excrement and dispose of it

(a) in the manner that is designated; or

(b) if paragraph (a) does not apply

(i) in a litter receptacle provided by the

Government of Yukon in the park,

(ii) outside the park in accordance with any

applicable laws, if the park is a recreation

park and no litter receptacle is provided by

the Government of Yukon in the park, or

(iii) in a park other than a recreation park, at least

three metres away from all park facilities if no

litter receptacle is provided by the

Government of Yukon.

(11) Nothing in this section prohibits or restricts an

individual with a disability from having a service animal

with them for the purpose of assisting them with their

disability.

### Wildlife attractants

21 (1) A person must not feed wildlife in a park.

(2) A person must ensure that any wildlife attractant

that the person has brought into a park

(a) when left unattended, is stored or disposed of in

a manner so that it

(i) does not attract wildlife, and

(ii) is not accessible by wildlife; and

(b) is removed from the park or disposed of in the

manner set out in paragraph (a) when the

person leaves the park.

(3) A person must not clean, field dress or butcher an

animal, other than fish, a game bird or a small game

animal, in a recreation park, campground or day use

area.

(4) A person who cleans fish or field dresses or butchers

a game bird or small game animal in a recreation park,

campground or day use area must dispose of the offal

(a) in the manner required by a sign posted under

section 5;

(b) in the case of fish, in a body of water or

waterway at a place other than a designated

boat launch, designated beach or designated

swimming area; or

(c) if no sign is posted and paragraph (b) does not

apply

(i) in a litter receptacle in the park provided by

the Government of Yukon, or

(ii) if no litter receptacle has been provided by the

Government of Yukon in the park, outside of

the park in accordance with any applicable

laws.

(5) Except as otherwise provided by a sign posted under

section 5 or the management plan or interim guidelines

for a park, a person must not hang an animal carcass,

including fish, in any of the following places:

(a) any area of a campground except a campsite;

(b) a day use area;

(c) a park facility.

### Fires

22 (1) A person must not set, build, light or maintain a fire

in a recreation park, special feature land use zone,

campground or day use area except

(a) in a fire ring, stove or other facility provided for

that purpose by the Government of Yukon; or

(b) subject to subsection (2), in a barbecue, stove or

other cooking device.

(2) A person must not use a barbecue, stove or other

cooking device that has a cooking surface that is larger

than 0.426 m 2 in a park unless they are authorized to

use a larger barbecue, stove or other cooking device by

a general permit.

(3) A person must not set, build, light or maintain a fire

on the top of the grate of a fire ring provided by the

Government of Yukon.

(4) A person must not stack wood in a fire ring so that

any part of the wood extends above the top of the grate

of the fire ring.

(5) A person must not leave a fire unattended in a park

or allow a fire to spread in a park.

(6) A person must fully extinguish a fire, including any

hot coals or smouldering materials, before leaving it

unattended.

(7) Despite subsections (5) and (6), a person may leave

a fire unattended if the fire is in a wood stove or other

device suitably designed and capable of confining it

(a) that is provided by the Government of Yukon and

that is located in a cook shelter; or

(b) that is located in a camping accommodation

unit.

(8) If a park officer considers it necessary for public

health and safety or the care, preservation, control or

management of a park or of park facilities, equipment

or other property in a park, the park officer may order

a person to extinguish a fire or to reduce the size of a

fire.

(9) If the Minister considers it necessary for public

health and safety or the care, preservation, control or

management of a park or of park facilities, equipment

or other property in a park, the Minister may, by posting

signs or by other means the Minister considers suitable,

restrict or prohibit the setting, building, lighting or

maintaining of fires in all or part of a park.

(10) A person must not set, build, light or maintain a

fire contrary to a restriction or prohibition established

under subsection (9).

### Firearms

23 A person must not

(a) discharge a firearm in a campground; or

(b) cause a projectile from a firearm to pass into a

campground.

### Smoking and vaping

24 A person must not smoke or vape in any of the following

areas within a park:

(a) a designated beach;

(b) a designated swimming area;

(c) a change house;

(d) a cook shelter;

(e) an outhouse;

(f) a playground;

(g) any other area designated as a place where

smoking or vaping is prohibited.

### Liquor

25 (1) Subject to the Liquor Act and unless otherwise

prohibited under subsection (2), for the purposes of

paragraph 55(1)(d) of the Act, a person may consume

liquor or have open a container of liquor at a campsite

occupied or used under a campsite permit.

(2) The Minister may, by posting signs under section 5,

prohibit persons from consuming liquor or having open

a container of liquor at a campsite occupied or used

under a campsite permit.

(3) A person must not consume liquor or have open a

container of liquor at a campsite occupied or used under

a campsite permit contrary to a prohibition imposed

under subsection (2).

## PART 6

## TRAVEL AND TRAFFIC, WATERCRAFT AND

## AIRCRAFT

### Rules governing travel, traffic and roads and trails

26 (1) Subject to the Highways Act and the Motor Vehicles

Act , the Minister may, by posting signs under section 5,

and the management plan or interim guidelines for a

park may, establish rules governing the operation of

vehicles, off-road vehicles and snowmobiles in a park,

travel in a park and the use of roads or trails within a

park, including, without limitation, rules

(a) governing the types of vehicles, off-road vehicles

and snowmobiles that a person is authorized to

operate and where they may be operated;

(b) establishing routes of travel and otherwise

controlling and prohibiting the movement of

vehicles, off-road vehicles, snowmobiles and

pedestrian and other forms of travel and traffic;

(c) governing the speed at which vehicles, off-road

vehicles and snowmobiles may travel;

(d) governing the purpose for which a road or trail

may be used; or

(e) governing the times of day or the times of year

during which a person is authorized

(i) to operate a type of vehicle, off-road vehicle

or snowmobile, or

(ii) to use a road or trail.

(2) In addition to complying with the Highways Act and

the Motor Vehicles Act , a person must comply with the

rules established under subsection (1).

### Off-road vehicles

27 (1) A person must not operate an off-road vehicle in a

park except as authorized by this section.

(2) Subject to the Highways Act , the Motor Vehicles Act ,

the Territorial Lands (Yukon) Act and the Off-Road

Vehicle Management Area Regulation , and to the rules

referred to in section 26, a person is authorized to

operate an off-road vehicle in a park

(a) on a maintained highway, and on a maintained

roadway, within the meaning of section 217.01

of the Motor Vehicles Act ;

(b) on a park access road;

(c) in an off-road vehicle management area within

the meaning of the Off-Road Vehicle

Management Area Regulation ;

(d) on a designated road, or a designated trail, on

which a person is authorized to operate an off-

road vehicle by a rule referred to in section 26;

(e) in places as authorized by a park permit; and

(f) in designated areas.

### Snowmobiles

28 (1) A person must not operate a snowmobile in a park

except as authorized by this section.

(2) Subject to the Highways Act and the Motor Vehicles

Act , the rules referred to in section 26 and subsection (3)

of this section, a person is authorized to operate a

snowmobile in a park during any time that the

conditions are suitable for the operation of a

snowmobile.

(3) A person must not operate a snowmobile in a

recreation park, campground or day use area except

(a) on a maintained highway, or on a maintained

roadway, within the meaning of section 217.01

of the Motor Vehicles Act ;

(b) on a park access road;

(c) on a designated road, or a designated trail, on

which a person is authorized to operate a

snowmobile by a rule referred to in section 26;

or

(d) on a frozen body of water or frozen waterway.

### Operation of vehicles and parking

29 Except as otherwise ordered by a park officer, a person

must not do any of the following in a park:

(a) stop or park a vehicle, off-road vehicle or

snowmobile on the travelled portion of a road or

trail;

(b) stop or park a vehicle, off-road vehicle or

snowmobile in a manner that

(i) impedes the proper use of a road or trail, or

(ii) damages vegetation;

(c) operate, stop or park a vehicle, off-road vehicle

or snowmobile in a manner that blocks access to

park facilities;

(d) park a vehicle, off-road vehicle or snowmobile in

an area designated as an area where the parking

of vehicles, off-road vehicles or snowmobiles is

prohibited;

(e) park a trailer, within the meaning of the Motor

Vehicles Act , whether attached to a vehicle, off-

road vehicle or snowmobile or not, in an area

designated as an area where the parking of

trailers is prohibited;

(f) unless authorized by a general permit, park a

vehicle, off-road vehicle or snowmobile in any

area within a campground except

(i) in a designated parking area, or

(ii) on the campsite the person is authorized to

occupy or use by a campsite permit or a

general permit.

### Watercraft

30 (1) A person must not operate or use, or cause to be

operated or used, on a body of water within a park

(a) any type of motorized watercraft that is

designated as a type of motorized watercraft

that is prohibited to be operated on that body of

water; or

(b) a motorized watercraft with a motor with a

greater horsepower than the designated

maximum horsepower motor for that body of

water.

(2) A person must not moor, anchor or beach a

watercraft at a dock, boat launch, designated beach or

designated swimming area except to allow people to get

on or off the watercraft before getting underway.

### Aircraft

31 (1) Subject to subsection (2), except as authorized by a

park permit, a person must not take off or land an

aircraft in a park.

(2) Except as otherwise provided by a sign posted under

section 5 or the management plan or interim guidelines

for a park, and subject to subsections (3) and (4), a

person is authorized to fly a drone in a park without a

park permit.

(3) Except as authorized by a park permit or a sign

posted under section 5, a person must not fly a drone

within 50 m, measured horizontally and at any altitude,

of any of the following:

(a) a campground;

(b) a designated beach;

(c) a designated swimming area;

(d) a staff accommodation area;

(e) a designated aircraft landing area;

(f) an area designated as an area where a drone

must not be flown;

(g) another person, except a person involved in the

operation of the drone.

(4) A person operating a drone must land the drone

without delay if the person sees or hears an aircraft,

other than a drone, that is approaching to land.

## PART 7

## CAMPING

### Authorization for camping

32 (1) A person must not camp in a park except as

authorized by subsection (2).

(2) A person is authorized to camp in a park as follows:

(a) in a recreation park or a campground

(i) at a campsite other than a group campsite, if

authorized under a campsite permit issued in

respect of the campsite, or

(ii) at a group campsite, if authorized under a

campsite permit issued in respect of the

group campsite;

(b) in a park other than a recreation park, unless

otherwise directed by the Minister,

(i) except as otherwise provided by a sign posted

under section 5 or the management plan or

interim guidelines for the park

(A) in any natural land use zone, wildland land

use zone or subsisting mineral interests

land use zone in the park, and

(B) in an area that is more than 500 m from

any road within or adjacent to the park,

and

(ii) in an area designated as an area where a

person may camp by the management plan

or interim guidelines for the park.

(3) Except as otherwise provided by the management

plan or interim guidelines for a park, a park permit is

not required to camp at a place where camping is

authorized under paragraph (2)(b).

### Designation of types of campsites

33 The Minister may, by posting signs under section 5,

(a) designate campsites as accessible campsites;

(b) designate campsites as group campsites;

(c) designate campsites as specified camping

accommodation unit campsites, and specify the

types of accommodation units that may be

placed on those campsites; or

(d) designate campsites as reservable campsites.

### Interference with campsites

34 (1) A person must not do any of the following in respect

of a campsite

(a) in respect of a campsite permit held by another

person;

(i) remove the campsite permit from the

campsite post if the permit is not expired, or

(ii) place a campsite permit on the campsite post

over or under the permit;

(b) remove a reserved sign posted on a campsite

post for a reservable campsite;

(c) camp at a reservable campsite reserved for

another person or for which another person

holds a campsite permit, except as authorized

by the reservation or holder of the permit and in

accordance with these Regulations;

(d) camp at or use an accessible campsite unless

(i) a person who is camping at or using the

campsite under the authority of the permit

has a disability that results in them requiring,

in carrying out their activities of daily living,

the use of a mobility or visual aid or the

assistance of another person or service

animal, or

(ii) the following conditions are met:

(A) the campsite permit is registered, and the

campsite is occupied or used after the time

specified on a sign posted at the campsite,

and

(B) the period of stay under the campsite

permit is one night, unless a longer period

of stay is authorized by a general permit;

(e) place a camping accommodation unit on a

specified camping accommodation unit

campsite unless it is of a type specified for that

campsite; or

(f) enclose a campsite.

(2) A campsite permit for a campsite is not valid if the

holder of the permit or a person camping at or using the

campsite under the authority of the campsite permit has

contravened any of paragraphs (1)(a) to (f).

### General provisions respecting campgrounds, campsites and campsite permits

35 (1) A person must not register a campsite permit for

more than one campsite for the same period of time.

(2) A person must not camp at a campsite or otherwise

use a campsite unless the holder of the campsite permit

is camping at or using the campsite at the same time.

(3) A person must not be an occupant of one or more

campsites within the same campground for a total of

more than 14 days in any 28-day period.

(4) A campsite permit issued to a person for a campsite

in a campground is not valid if the period of stay under

the permit, considered alone or in conjunction with the

periods of stay under any other campsite permit issued

to the person, would result in the person being

authorized to camp at one or more campsites within that

campground for a total of more than 14 days in any 28-

day period.

(5) Except as authorized by a general permit, a person

must not place a camping accommodation unit that is

being used for camping purposes in a recreation park or

campground except on a campsite.

(6) Except as authorized by a general permit, the

maximum number of individuals who may be present at

the same time during the hours beginning at 7:00 a.m.

and ending at 11:00 p.m.

(a) at a specified camping accommodation unit

campsite for tents is five; and

(b) at any other campsite is 12.

(7) Except as authorized by a general permit, the

maximum number of occupants or users

(a) of a specified camping accommodation unit

campsite for tents is five;

(b) of a group campsite is 12; and

(c) of any other campsite is eight.

(8) The holder of a campsite permit must ensure

(a) that the number of individuals at the campsite

does not exceed the number allowed under

subsection (6); and

(b) that the number of occupants or users of the

campsite does not exceed the number allowed

under subsection (7).

(9) During the period beginning on May 1 and ending

on September 30 of a year

(a) a person, including the holder of a campsite

permit for a campsite, must not use the

campsite for a purpose other than camping and

related purposes;

(b) the holder of a campsite permit for a campsite

must ensure that the campsite

(i) is not left unoccupied during the first night of

the period for which the campsite permit is in

effect, and

(ii) after the first night of the period for which the

campsite permit is in effect, is not left

unoccupied for a period of more than

24 hours except as authorized by a general

permit.

(10) If a campsite is left unoccupied contrary to

subsection (9) and a park officer cancels the campsite

permit under paragraph 73(a) of the Act, the park officer

may remove any property that is unattended from the

campsite and subsections 9(2) and (3) apply in respect

of the removal and storage of the property.

### Noise

36 (1) The Minister may designate an area of a

campground as a quiet camping area by posting signs

under section 5.

(2) A person must not do any of the following during

quiet hours or in a quiet camping area of a campground:

(a) make noise that could reasonably be expected to

interfere with another person’s quiet enjoyment

of the campground;

(b) operate equipment that makes noise that could

reasonably be expected to interfere with another

person’s quiet enjoyment of the campground

including, without limitation,

(i) a motor vehicle or off-road vehicle, except

when entering or exiting the campground,

(ii) a fuel powered generator, and

(iii) audio equipment.

(3) In addition to the restriction on the operation of a

fuel powered generator under paragraph (2)(b), a

person must not operate a fuel powered generator in a

campground except during the hours from 9:00 a.m. to

11:00 a.m. and from 5:00 p.m. to 8:00 p.m. unless they

are authorized to do so by a general permit.

(4) A person must not leave a fuel powered generator

unattended in a campground while it is in operation.

### Belongings

37 (1) Except as authorized by a sign posted under

section 5 or by a general permit, a person must not leave

their belongings in a campground at a place other than

on the campsite that the person occupies or uses under

a campsite permit.

(2) The holder of a campsite permit for a campsite must

ensure that their belongings and the belongings of the

other occupants or users, including the camping

accommodation unit or units on the campsite, are

removed from the campsite

(a) by the time the permit expires; or

(b) without delay if the permit is suspended or

cancelled before it expires.

## PART 8

## PARK PERMITS

### Age of eligibility for park permit

38 A person who is under 16 years of age is not eligible for

a park permit.

### Park permits

39 (1) The following types of park permits are established:

(a) aircraft permit, which may authorize

(i) the taking-off or landing of an aircraft, other

than a drone, or

(ii) the flying of a drone;

(b) campsite permit, to authorize the occupation or

use of a campsite;

(c) commercial film or photography permit, which

may authorize filming or photography for, or

with an expectation of, gain or profit;

(d) commercial or large group recreation permit,

which may authorize one or more personal

recreational activities or organized recreational

activities that are not excluded from the

requirement for a park permit under

subsection 8(1);

(e) general permit, which may authorize

developments, uses, activities or occupations of

land referred to in

(i) paragraphs 8(1)(c) to (g),

(ii) subsection 9(1),

(iii) subsection 20(4),

(iv) subsection 22(2),

(v) paragraph 29(f),

(vi) subsection 32(3),

(vii) clause 34(1)(d)(ii)(B),

(viii) subsections 35(5) to (7) and

subparagraph 35(9)(b)(ii),

(ix) subsection 36(3),

(x) subsection 37(1);

(f) land use permit, which may authorize the use of

land, and any use, activity or infrastructure

associated with that use of land, including,

without limitation, the following:

(i) the occupation of land,

(ii) the development of land,

(iii) industrial development,

(iv) exploration for natural resources,

(v) the commercial harvesting of natural

resources;

(g) park-related development permit which may

authorize a park-related development as

referred to in subsection 36(1) of the Act;

(h) research or educational permit, which may

authorize a research activity or an educational

activity, including, without limitation, the

following:

(i) the collection of samples,

(ii) the creation of research plots,

(iii) monitoring,

(iv) surveying,

(v) inventorying,

(vi) field trips,

(vii) field courses,

(viii) workshops,

(ix) public education events;

(i) special event permit, which may authorize the

holding of a special event;

(j) specified development, use, activity or

occupation of land permit which may authorize

the development, use, activity or occupation of

land that is specified in the permit where

another type of park permit does not apply.

(2) The Minister may establish terms or conditions that

apply to all park permits of a particular type.

### Fees

40 (1) The fees payable in respect of park permits are as

follows:

(a) subject to subsection (2), for a campsite permit

(i) obtained under an annual camping voucher,

nightly camping voucher or campsite

reservation $0,

(ii) not obtained under an annual camping

voucher, a nightly camping voucher or

campsite reservation, $20 per night;

(b) for a general permit, $0;

(c) for any other type of park permit, $0;

(d) to apply for a park permit, other than a campsite

permit or a general permit, $40.

(2) The fee payable for a campsite permit during the

period beginning on October 1 of a year and ending on

April 30 of the following year is $0.

(3) The fee to apply for a commercial or large group

recreation permit, or a special event permit, where the

application is made by an organization that the Minister

is satisfied is primarily operated for educational,

benevolent or charitable purposes, or without an

expectation of gain or profit, is $0.

(4) The fee payable for

(a) an annual camping voucher is $200;

(b) a nightly camping voucher per night is $18; and

(c) a campsite reservation per night is $18.

(5) The service fee to make a campsite reservation

through the online service for the reservation of

reservable campsites is $10.

(6) The fee payable for a campsite permit, campsite

reservation, annual camping voucher or nightly camping

voucher by a resident of Yukon who is 65 years of age

or older is 50% of the fee otherwise payable under this

section.

(7) If tax, including the goods and services tax under

the Excise Tax Act (Canada), is payable in respect of a

fee under this section, the tax is included in the fee

amount.

### Application for park permit

41 A person may apply for a park permit in the form

determined by the Minister under section 37 of the Act

and must include the information required to be

provided by the form.

### Registration of campsite permits required

42 A campsite permit is not valid until it is registered in

accordance with the instructions on a sign posted under

section 5 and the fee for the campsite permit has been

paid.

### Online campsite reservations

43 (1) The Minister may establish and operate an online

service for the reservation of reservable campsites.

(2) A campsite reservation applies only to the reservable

campsite set out in the reservation.

### Advance payment for campsite permits

44 (1) The Minister may establish and operate an online

payment service for advance payment for campsite

permits, through the issuance of annual camping

vouchers and nightly camping vouchers.

(2) The Minister may establish limits and terms and

conditions with respect to the use of the online payment

service and with respect to annual camping vouchers

and nightly camping vouchers, including, without

limitation,

(a) providing that the service is available only to

residents of Yukon;

(b) setting the number of days for which an annual

camping voucher holder may obtain campsite

permits under the voucher; and

(c) the period during which a type of voucher is

valid.

(3) The Minister may issue an annual camping voucher

or a nightly camping voucher to a person who pays the

fee for the voucher set out in paragraph 40(4)(a) or (b).

(4) A person to whom an annual camping voucher or a

nightly camping voucher is issued may use the voucher

to obtain campsite permits as determined by the

Minister and specified in the online payment service

without paying an additional fee during the period for

which the voucher is valid.

(5) A person to whom an annual camping voucher or a

nightly camping voucher is issued under subsection (3)

must not

(a) share the voucher with any other person;

(b) use the voucher to obtain a campsite permit for

more than one campsite for the same night; or

(c) use the voucher to obtain a campsite permit for

a campsite in a backcountry campground or for

a group campsite.

(6) A person must not use an annual camping voucher

or a nightly camping voucher issued to another person

to obtain a campsite permit.

### Authority under park permit

45 Subject to subsections 35(2), (6) and (7), a campsite

permit authorizes, in addition to the holder of the

campsite permit, an individual camping or using a

campsite with the holder of the campsite permit to

occupy or use the campsite.

### Duties of persons acting under park permits

46 (1) A person who undertakes a development, use or

activity in a park, or who occupies land within a park, or

who is an occupant or user of a campsite, under the

authority of a park permit held by another person must

comply with the terms and conditions of the park permit.

(2) In addition to the duty of the holder of a park permit

under section 52 of the Act, it is a term of every park

permit that the holder of the permit has a duty to take

reasonable care to ensure that every person who is

acting under the authority of the permit complies with

(a) the provisions of the Act, this Regulation and any

other regulation under the Act respecting the

undertaking of the development, use or activity

or the occupation of land; and

(b) the terms and conditions of the park permit.

## PART 9

## TRANSITIONAL PROVISION, REPEAL AND COMING

## INTO FORCE

### Transitional provision

47 (1) A park permit issued under the Act, other than a

campground permit, that is in force immediately before

the coming into force of this Regulation and that would

continue in force according to its terms after the coming

into force of this Regulation is, on the coming into force

of this Regulation, continued as a park permit of the type

determined by the Minister and remains subject to the

same terms and conditions.

(2) A campground permit within the meaning of the

Yukon Campground Regulations that is in force

immediately before the coming into force of this

Regulation and that would continue in force according to

its terms after the coming into force of this Regulation

is, on the coming into force of this Regulation, continued

as a campsite permit and remains subject to the same

terms and conditions.

### Repeals

48 The following regulations are repealed:

(a) Yukon Campground Regulations ;

(b) Park Permit Fee Regulation .

### Coming into force

49 This Regulation comes into force on the later of the

following days:

(a) April 1, 2025;

(b) the day it is filed with the registrar of regulations

under the Regulations Act .
