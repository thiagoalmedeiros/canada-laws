## PART 1

## INTERPRETATION AND DESIGNATION OF

### Definitions

1 In this Regulation

“ approved degree program ” means a program

(a) that meets the academic requirements

established or approved by the Registrar of

Psychologists, or that is approved or accredited

by a body approved by the Registrar, and

(b) that leads to a master’s degree or doctorate in

psychology; « programme agréé menant à un

grade »

“ certificate of standing and professional

conduct ” means a document that is issued by a

regulatory authority of any jurisdiction in respect of an

individual and that includes information about the

individual's registration and licensing history as those

relate to their conduct or character or their competence

to practise psychology, including

(a) any practice limitations, restrictions or

conditions imposed on their registration, licence

or other authorization to practise psychology,

and

(b) any current or past disciplinary actions or

agreements respecting their conduct or

character or their competence to practise

psychology; « certificat de compétence et de

conduite professionnelle »

“ continuing competency activity ” means a certification,

quality assurance requirement or other activity specified

by the Registrar of Psychologists in the standards of

practice for assuring continuing quality of practice and

ability to practise; « activité de compétence continue »

“ courtesy registrant ” means an individual registered in

the courtesy class of registrant; « membre invité »

“ criminal record check ” means a record check to obtain

information from a law enforcement agency in Canada

as to whether an individual has, under any enactment of

Canada or a province, any

(a) conviction or finding of guilt in relation to which

no pardon or record suspension is in effect, or

(b) outstanding charge awaiting court disposition;

« vérification du casier judiciaire »

“ entry-to-practice examination ” means the

Examination for Professional Practice in Psychology set

by the Association of State and Provincial Psychology

Boards, or an examination approved as equivalent by the

Registrar of Psychologists; « examen d’aptitude pour

l’accès à la profession »

“ full registrant ” means an individual registered in the

full class of registrant; « membre de plein droit »

“ inactive registrant ” means an individual registered in

the inactive class of registrant; « membre inactif »

“ international criminal record check ” means a record

check to obtain information from an agency recognized

by the Registrar of Psychologists in a jurisdiction outside

Canada as to whether an individual has any conviction

or finding of guilt, or outstanding charge awaiting court

disposition, under a law of that jurisdiction;

« vérification internationale du casier judiciaire »

“ provisional registrant ” means an individual registered

in the provisional class of registrant; « membre

temporaire »

“ Psychologists Advisory Committee ” means the

Committee appointed under section 22; « comité

consultatif sur la psychologie »

“ psychology ” means the health profession in which a

person performs one or more of the following services:

(a) the prevention or treatment of mental conditions

or disorders,

(b) the assessment, diagnosis, treatment, guidance

or support of individuals or groups of individuals

in order to enhance their developmental,

behavioural, cognitive, emotional, intellectual or

interpersonal functioning, primarily by using

psychological assessment and intervention

strategies such as psychometric testing and

psychotherapy,

(c) teaching, supervising or consulting on the

practice of psychology,

(d) managing or conducting research in the science,

techniques and practice of psychology;

« psychologie »

“ registrant ” means an individual who is registered as a

member of the health profession of psychology;

« membre »

“ Registrar of Psychologists ” means the registrar;

« registraire des psychologues »

“ registration year ” means the period beginning on

February 1 and ending on the following January 31;

« année d’inscription »

“ standards of practice ” means the standards of practice

specified under paragraph 5(1)(a); « normes

d’exercice »

“ standards of professional ethics ” means the standards

of professional ethics specified under paragraph 5(1)(b);

« normes de déontologie »

“ unprofessional conduct ” of a registrant means any of

the following acts or omissions:

(a) displaying a lack of knowledge, skill or judgment

in the performance of services within the

practice of psychology,

(b) misrepresenting their status as a registrant or

the practice limitations, restrictions or

conditions imposed on their registration,

(c) failing or refusing to cooperate with the

Registrar of Psychologists or an inspector,

(d) contravening any of the following:

(i) a provision of the Act or this Regulation,

(ii) an order made under the Act,

(iii) a practice limitation, restriction or condition

imposed on their registration,

(iv) a provision of any other enactment of Canada

or a province that applies to the profession of

psychology,

(v) the standards of practice,

(vi) the standards of professional ethics,

(e) acting in a way that is detrimental to the best

interests of the public,

(f) making a false representation in an application

under this Regulation,

(g) consenting, whether expressly or impliedly, to

unprofessional conduct committed by a person

who is employed, managed or supervised by, or

who is an agent of, the registrant,

(h) harming or risking harm to the integrity of the

profession of psychology,

(i) being convicted of an offence related to any

matter described in paragraphs (a) to (f).

« conduite non professionnelle »

### Designation of health profession of psychology

2 The health profession of psychology is designated for the

purposes of the Act.

## PART 2

## PRACTICE OF PSYCHOLOGY

### Restricted titles and abbreviations

3 (1) The following titles and abbreviations may be used

only by registrants:

(a) psychologist;

(b) registered psychologist and its abbreviation

R.Psych.;

(c) registered doctoral psychologist and its

abbreviation R.D.Psych.;

(d) provisional psychologist;

(e) registered provisional psychologist;

(f) inactive psychologist;

(g) registered inactive psychologist.

(2) Full registrants and courtesy registrants are

authorized to use the following titles and abbreviations:

(a) psychologist;

(b) registered psychologist and its abbreviation

R.Psych.

(3) Full registrants and courtesy registrants who

graduated from an approved degree program with a

doctorate in psychology are authorized to use the

following titles and abbreviations:

(a) registered doctoral psychologist and its

abbreviation R.D.Psych.;

(b) a title or abbreviation set out in subsection (2).

(4) Provisional registrants are authorized to use the

following titles:

(a) provisional psychologist;

(b) registered provisional psychologist.

(5) Inactive registrants are authorized to use the

following titles:

(a) inactive psychologist;

(b) registered inactive psychologist.

### Scope of practice

4 (1) Subject to subsection (4), full registrants,

provisional registrants and courtesy registrants may

practise psychology.

(2) Subject to subsection (4), a provisional registrant

must not practise psychology except under the

supervision of a full registrant whose registration is not

subject to limitations, restrictions or conditions.

(3) An inactive registrant must not practise psychology.

(4) In practising psychology, a registrant must restrict

themselves to the services that they are competent to

perform and to those that are appropriate to their area

of practice.

### Standards of practice and ethics

5 (1) The Registrar of Psychologists must specify

(a) standards of practice for registrants; and

(b) standards of professional ethics for registrants.

(2) A registrant must practise psychology in accordance

with the standards of practice and the standards of

professional ethics specified by the Registrar.

### Continuing competence

6 (1) Full registrants and provisional registrants must

complete 75 hours of continuing competency activities

over the course of every period of three registration

years.

(2) If a registrant is registered for only a part of a

period referred to in subsection (1), the Registrar of

Psychologists may reduce the number of hours of

continuing competency activities that the registrant

must complete for that period.

### Prohibited marketing

7 (1) In this section

“ marketing ” is a type of advertising and includes

(a) an advertisement,

(b) a promotional activity or material, a listing in a

directory, a public appearance, a publication or

a communication in any medium to a client,

prospective client or the public in the nature of

an advertisement, or any other means by which

a registrant’s psychology services are promoted,

and

(c) contact with a prospective client that is initiated

by or under the direction of a registrant.

« marketing »

(2) A registrant must not undertake or authorize any

person to undertake any marketing in respect of

psychology services that

(a) is false, inaccurate or reasonably capable of

misleading the recipient or intended recipient;

(b) is unverifiable; or

(c) harms or risks harm to the integrity of the

profession of psychology.

### Professional liability insurance

8 (1) A registrant, other than an inactive registrant, must

at all times maintain professional liability insurance in

an amount of at least $3,000,000 against liability for

claims against them in respect of their own conduct in

their practice and in respect of the conduct of other

persons in connection with their practice for which they

are liable.

(2) A registrant must provide proof of professional

liability insurance on applying for

(a) registration; and

(b) renewal of registration.

(3) If a registrant fails to provide proof of professional

liability insurance, the Registrar of Psychologists must

cancel the registrant’s registration, unless the Registrar

otherwise directs.

## PART 3

## REGISTRATION AND THE REGISTER

### Classes of registrants

9 The following classes of registrant are established:

(a) full registrant;

(b) provisional registrant;

(c) courtesy registrant;

(d) inactive registrant.

### Full registrants

10 (1) An individual who has met the application

requirements under section 14 or 15, as the case may

be, and who is registered or licensed and in good

standing in the full class of registrant or in the equivalent

of the full class of registrant in a province other than

Yukon is eligible for registration as a full registrant if the

individual

(a) has successfully completed the jurisprudence

and ethics for the practice of psychology

examination set by the Registrar of

Psychologists;

(b) has completed cultural safety and humility

training set by the Registrar; and

(c) holds the professional liability insurance

required by section 8.

(2) An individual who has met the application

requirements under section 14 or 15, as the case may

be, who is not registered or licensed in the full class of

registrant or the equivalent of the full class of registrant

in a province other than Yukon is eligible for registration

as a full registrant if the individual

(a) holds a master’s degree or doctorate in

psychology from an approved degree program;

(b) passed the entry-to-practice examination with

at least the minimum score set by the Registrar;

(c) has successfully completed the jurisprudence

and ethics for the practice of psychology

examination set by the Registrar;

(d) has completed cultural safety and humility

training set by the Registrar;

(e) has met the requirements of

paragraph 11(2)(a); and

(f) holds the professional liability insurance

required by section 8.

### Provisional registrants

11 (1) An individual who has met the application

requirements under section 14 or 15, as the case may

be, is eligible for registration as a provisional registrant

if

(a) the individual holds a master ’ s degree or

doctorate in psychology from an approved

degree program;

(b) the individual has submitted a supervision plan

for completing, within three years after initially

being registered as a provisional registrant or

reregistered as a provisional registrant on

application as referred to in subsection (4),

1600 hours of supervised practice in Yukon in

accordance with the standards of practice;

(c) one or more full registrants have certified that

they will supervise the individual’s practice

under the supervision plan; and

(d) the individual holds the professional liability

insurance required by section 8.

(2) A provisional registrant must, subject to

subsection (3),

(a) complete, within three years after initially being

registered as a provisional registrant or

reregistered as a provisional registrant on

application as referred to in subsection (4),

1600 hours of supervised practice in Yukon in

accordance with their supervision plan; and

(b) pass, within four years after initially being

registered as a provisional registrant or

reregistered as a provisional registrant on

application as referred to in subsection (4), the

entry-to-practice examination with at least the

minimum score set by the Registrar of

Psychologists.

(3) On application of a provisional registrant, the

Registrar may extend one or both of the timelines

referred to in subsection (2) for not more than one year.

(4) If a provisional registrant does not complete the

requirements set out in subsection (2) within the time

required, the Registrar must cancel the individual ’s

registration, but the individual may apply to be

reregistered as a provisional registrant.

### Courtesy registrants

12 (1) An individual who has met the application

requirements under section 14 is eligible for registration

as a courtesy registrant if

(a) the individual is seeking registration in Yukon

for a specified purpose that is approved by the

Registrar of Psychologists;

(b) the Registrar is satisfied that the individual is

competent to provide the service;

(c) the individual is registered or licensed and in

good standing in the equivalent of the full class

of registrant in a province other than Yukon; and

(d) the individual holds the professional liability

insurance required by section 8.

(2) A courtesy registrant must remain registered and in

good standing in the province referred to in

paragraph (1)(c) and their registration is cancelled if

their registration in that province is cancelled or they are

no longer in good standing in that province.

(3) A courtesy registrant’s registration is in effect for a

period determined by the Registrar, which must not

exceed three months.

(4) An individual must not be registered as a courtesy

registrant more than twice within a registration year.

### Inactive registrants

13 An individual who has met the application requirements

under section 15 is eligible for registration as an inactive

registrant if

(a) they are registered as a full registrant or an

inactive registrant; and

(b) they are not engaged in the practice of

psychology.

### Application for registration

14 An applicant for registration must submit to the

Registrar of Psychologists

(a) a completed application, in a form acceptable to

the Registrar, that includes the information

required by the form;

(b) proof that the applicant holds a master’s degree

or doctorate in psychology from an approved

degree program;

(c) a certificate of standing and professional

conduct, satisfactory to the Registrar, from each

jurisdiction other than Yukon where the

applicant has ever been registered, licensed or

otherwise authorized to practise psychology;

(d) results, satisfactory to the Registrar, of a

criminal record check that was carried out

within the three-month period before the date

the application is submitted;

(e) if the applicant resided or was registered,

licensed or otherwise authorized to practise

psychology in a jurisdiction outside Canada at

any time during the six-month period that ends

on the date the application is submitted, results,

satisfactory to the Registrar of an international

criminal record check from that jurisdiction that

was carried out within the three-month period

before the date the application is submitted;

(f) evidence of good character satisfactory to the

Registrar;

(g) proof satisfactory to the Registrar that the

applicant holds professional liability insurance as

required by section 8;

(h) any other information the Registrar requires to

determine the applicant’s eligibility for

registration;

(i) the fees required by subsection 26(1).

### Registration renewals and changes in class

15 A full registrant, provisional registrant or inactive

registrant may apply to renew their registration or to be

registered in another class of registrant by submitting

the following to the Registrar of Psychologists on or

before the December 31 immediately preceding the date

on which their registration expires under

subsection 19(3):

(a) a completed application, in a form acceptable to

the Registrar, that includes the information

required by the form;

(b) except in the case of an applicant for registration

as an inactive registrant, proof satisfactory to

the Registrar that the applicant holds any

current certifications for continuing competency

activities specified by the Registrar in the

standards of practice;

(c) a certificate of standing and professional

conduct, satisfactory to the Registrar, from each

jurisdiction in which the applicant has been

registered, licensed or otherwise authorized to

practise psychology during the previous

registration year;

(d) a declaration that the applicant has provided the

notices required to be provided under

section 20, if any;

(e) except in the case of an applicant for registration

as an inactive registrant, proof satisfactory to

the Registrar that the applicant holds

professional liability insurance as required by

section 8;

(f) any other information the Registrar requires to

determine the applicant’s eligibility for

registration;

(g) the fees required by subsection 26(1).

### Referral of registration matter to Psychologists Advisory Committee

16 (1) The Registrar may refer a registration matter to the

Psychologists Advisory Committee

(a) if the Registrar is uncertain whether an

applicant has met the applicable requirements

for registration; or

(b) the Registrar considers it advisable to confer

with the Psychologists Advisory Committee

before deciding whether to

(i) refuse to register an applicant under

subsection 17(1) or (2), or

(ii) impose limitations, restrictions or conditions

on the registration of an applicant under

section 18.

(2) If a matter has been referred to the Psychologists

Advisory Committee under subsection (1), the

Committee must review the matter and make the

recommendations to the Registrar that the Committee

considers appropriate.

### Refusal to register

17 (1) The Registrar of Psychologists may refuse to register

an applicant if

(a) the applicant's registration, licence or other

authorization to practise psychology has been

cancelled or suspended or limited in any

jurisdiction at any time;

(b) at the time of the application, an investigation,

review or proceeding is taking place in any

jurisdiction that could result in the suspension,

cancellation or limitation of the applicant's

registration, licence or other authorization to

practise psychology;

(c) the applicant has contravened the Act or this

Regulation;

(d) the applicant has been convicted in Canada or

elsewhere of an offence that relates to a matter

described in the definition “unprofessional

conduct” in section 1; or

(e) the Registrar determines that the applicant has

not met the applicable requirements for

registration.

(2) The Registrar must refuse to register an applicant if

the Registrar considers that

(a) it is necessary to do so to protect the public

because of a complaint or disciplinary, civil or

criminal proceedings in any jurisdiction with

respect to the applicant’s conduct or character

or their competence to practise psychology; and

(b) it is not reasonably practicable for the Registrar

to impose a limitation, restriction or condition

on the practice of psychology by the applicant to

protect the public.

(3) If the Registrar refuses to register an applicant, the

Registrar must notify the applicant in writing of the

Registrar’s decision, of the reasons for the decision and

of the applicant’s right under subsection 9(3) of the Act

to appeal that decision to the Supreme Court.

### Imposition of limitations, restrictions or conditions

18 (1) The Registrar of Psychologists may impose

limitations, restrictions or conditions on the registration,

reregistration or renewal of registration of an applicant

if the Registrar considers it necessary to protect the

public because of a complaint or disciplinary, civil or

criminal proceedings in any jurisdiction with respect to

the applicant’s conduct or character or their competence

to practise psychology.

(2) If an applicant for registration or renewal of

registration is registered or licensed to practise

psychology in a province other than Yukon and their

registration or licence in that province is subject to

limitations, restrictions or conditions, the Registrar may

impose the limitations, restrictions or conditions on the

registration of the applicant that the Registrar considers

appropriate to protect the public.

### Registration

19 (1) If the Registrar of Psychologists registers an

applicant, the Registrar must issue a certificate of

registration to the applicant.

(2) The Registrar must include the following

information on a certificate of registration:

(a) the registrant’s name and class of registrant;

(b) a notation of any limitations, restrictions or

conditions on the registrant’s registration or

limits or conditions on the registrant’s practice

of psychology imposed under the Act or this

Regulation;

(c) the effective date and expiry date of the

registration.

(3) Subject to any suspension or cancellation of the

registration of the registrant, the certificate of

registration

(a) of a full registrant, provisional registrant or

inactive registrant is valid for a period beginning

on the date it is issued and ending on the last

day of the registration year;

(b) of a courtesy registrant is valid for a period

beginning on the date it is issued and ending on

the date specified in the certificate.

### Duty to notify the Registrar of Psychologists

20 A registrant must notify the Registrar of Psychologists in

writing without delay after any of the following occurs:

(a) there is a change in their name or contact

information, including their address and any

other contact information that is required by the

Registrar;

(b) they are refused registration, a licence or other

authorization to practise by a regulatory

authority that regulates a profession in any

jurisdiction;

(c) a practice limitation, restriction or condition is

imposed on their registration, licence or other

authorization by a regulatory authority that

regulates a profession in any jurisdiction;

(d) they become the subject of a complaint,

investigation, reprimand, hearing, finding or

appeal respecting their professional conduct or

their competence to practise any profession in

any jurisdiction;

(e) they become a party to a process for mediation,

conciliation, negotiation or any other means of

facilitating the resolution, without a hearing, of

a complaint respecting their professional

conduct or their competence to practise any

profession in any jurisdiction;

(f) they become the subject of a professional

liability claim in any jurisdiction;

(g) they cease to hold professional liability insurance

as required by section 8;

(h) they are charged

(i) in any jurisdiction with an offence

(A) that relates to a matter described in the

definition “unprofessional conduct” in

section 1, or

(B) that relates to the practice of any other

profession or occupation, or

(ii) with an offence under any other enactment of

Canada or a province;

(i) they are found guilty

(i) in any jurisdiction of an offence

(A) that relates to a matter described in the

definition “unprofessional conduct” in

section 1, or

(B) that relates to the practice of any other

profession or occupation, or

(ii) of an offence under any other enactment of

Canada or a province;

(j) their registration, licence or other authorization

to practise in another jurisdiction is suspended

or cancelled by the regulatory authority of that

jurisdiction.

### Register

21 The following types of information are prescribed for the

purpose of subsection 9(2) of the Act as information that

the Registrar of Psychologists must record in the register

in respect of every registrant and former registrant:

(a) a notation of their class of registrant;

(b) work contact information;

(c) a notation of any limitations, restrictions or

conditions on their registration or limits or

conditions on their practice of psychology

imposed under the Act or this Regulation;

(d) a notation of the date on which their registration

expired or is to expire;

(e) information regarding every complaint,

investigation or disciplinary proceeding of which

the registrant or former registrant is the

subject;

(f) a record of every undertaking they have made

with a regulatory authority in any jurisdiction to

carry out voluntary remedial measures;

(g) a notation of a change in their class of registrant

and the effective date of the change;

(h) a notation of any expiry of their registration or

suspension or cancellation of their registration

and the reason for any suspension or

cancellation.

## PART 4

## GENERAL

### Psychologists Advisory Committee

22 (1) The Minister may appoint a Psychologists Advisory

Committee, to be composed of at least three members,

one of whom must be appointed by the Minister to be

the chair of the Committee.

(2) At least three members of the Psychologists

Advisory Committee must

(a) be full registrants or inactive registrants; or

(b) be registered or licensed in the equivalent of the

full class of registrant in a province other than

Yukon.

(3) A member may be appointed to the Psychologists

Advisory Committee for a term of up to three years and

may be reappointed for additional terms.

(4) Three members of the Psychologists Advisory

Committee constitute a quorum, but a vacancy in the

membership does not impair the right of the remaining

members to act.

(5) The Psychologists Advisory Committee

(a) on the request of the Minister or the Registrar of

Psychologists, may advise the Minister and the

Registrar on matters pertaining to the practice

of psychology including the following:

(i) the standards of practice,

(ii) the standards of professional ethics,

(iii) requirements for registration,

(iv) information to be provided to the public about

the functions of registrants and the areas of

knowledge, skill and judgment of registrants,

and about the standards of practice and the

standards of professional ethics,

(v) any other matters that relate to governing the

practice of psychology by registrants;

(b) on the request of the Registrar, may review

applications for registration and for the renewal

of registration and provide advice about them;

and

(c) on the request of the Registrar, may participate

in national initiatives related to the practice of

psychology and provide the Registrar with

feedback on those initiatives.

### Membership of the discipline committee

23 (1) If the Minister appoints a discipline committee of

only one person, the Minister must ensure that the

person who is appointed

(a) is a full registrant or an inactive registrant; or

(b) is registered or licensed in the equivalent of the

full class of registrant in a province other than

Yukon.

(2) If the Minister appoints a discipline committee of

more than one person, the Minister must ensure that

(a) at least one person who is appointed as a

member

(i) is a full registrant or an inactive registrant, or

(ii) is registered or licensed in the equivalent of

the full class of registrant in a province other

than Yukon; and

(b) at least one person who is appointed as a

member is not a registrant.

### Procedures to be followed by the discipline committee

24 (1) A discipline committee must keep a record of

(a) its proceedings; and

(b) all the evidence that it receives in relation to a

hearing.

(2) The record of evidence referred to in

paragraph (1)(b) must be maintained until the later of

(a) the day that is one year after the day on which

the discipline committee delivers its order to the

respondent and the Registrar of Psychologists

under subsection 27(3) of the Act; and

(b) the day on which all appeal proceedings and

judicial review proceedings, if any, in respect of

the order are concluded.

(3) A person who requests a transcript of the oral

evidence that is received by a discipline committee must

pay for the preparation of the transcript.

### Maximum fine and maximum amount of costs

25 (1) For the purpose of paragraph 27(1)(f) of the Act, the

maximum fine is $25,000.

(2) For the purpose of paragraph 27(1)(g) of the Act, the

maximum amount of costs is $50,000.

### Fees

26 (1) The following fees are payable to the Registrar of

Psychologists in respect of registrations:

(a) application fee for registration or, subject to

paragraph (d), renewal of registration, $50;

(b) registration fee

(i) for full registrants and provisional

registrants, $250,

(ii) for registration of a registrant in a different

class of registrant, $250,

(iii) for courtesy registrants, $0,

(iv) for renewal of registration, $0;

(c) fee to issue the following certificates for all or

any part of a registration year:

(i) certificate of registration — full registrant,

$200,

(ii) certificate of registration — provisional

registrant, $100,

(iii) certificate of registration — courtesy

registrant, $100,

(iv) certificate of registration — inactive

registrant, $100;

(d) late application fee to renew a registration after

the date set by section 15, $200.

(2) The fee for a certificate of standing and professional

conduct is $50.

### Transitional

28 (1) Before the coming into force of this Regulation, a

person may apply to be registered as a full registrant,

provisional registrant or courtesy registrant, and the

Registrar of Psychologists may register the person, but

the registration of the person is not effective until the

day on which this section comes into force.

(2) Until March 1, 2026, the Registrar may, in the

Registrar’s discretion, register in the full class of

registrant an applicant who has not met the

requirements set out in paragraphs 10(1)(a) or (b) or

10(2)(b), (c), (d) or (e).

(3) It is a condition of the registration of a registrant

who is registered as provided by subsection (2) that the

registrant must meet the applicable requirements

referred to in subsection (2) by July 31, 2026.

(4) If a registrant does not meet the applicable

requirements referred to in subsection (2) by

July 31, 2026, their registration is suspended effective

August 1, 2026, until they meet the requirements or

their registration expires.

(5) The Registrar must not renew the registration of a

registrant who has not met the requirements referred to

in subsection (2), but the individual may apply to

reregister after meeting the requirements.

### Coming into force

29 This Regulation comes into force on the later of the

following days:

(a) February 1, 2025;

(b) the day it is filed with the registrar of regulations

under the Regulations Act .
