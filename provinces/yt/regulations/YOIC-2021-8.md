## PART 1

## GENERAL

### Definitions

1 In this Regulation

“ Advisory Committee ” means the Midwifery Advisory

Committee appointed under section 47; « comité

consultatif »

“ BC Drug Schedules Regulation ” means the Drug

Schedules Regulation (British Columbia), as amended

from time to time; « règlement de la C.-B. sur les

annexes des médicaments »

“ BC Midwives Regulation ” means the Midwives

Regulation (British Columbia), as amended from time

to time; « règlement sur les sages-femmes de la C.-

B. »

“ Canadian Free Trade Agreement ” means the

Canadian Free Trade Agreement signed in 2017 by the

governments of Canada and the provinces, as amended

from time to time; « Accord de libre-échange

canadien »

“ certificate of standing and professional conduct ”

means a document that is issued by a regulatory

authority of any jurisdiction in respect of an individual

and that includes information about the individual's

licensing and registration history as those relate to

their conduct or character or their competence to

practise midwifery, including

(a) any practice limitations, restrictions or

conditions imposed on their licence,

registration or other authorization to practise

midwifery, and

(b) any current or past disciplinary actions or

agreements respecting their conduct or

character or their competence to practise

midwifery; « certificat de compétence et de

déontologie professionnelle »

“ client ” means an individual who is receiving care from

a registered midwife in relation to their pregnancy or

potential pregnancy, including the post-partum period;

« client »

“ code of ethics ” means the code of ethics that is

specified by the Registrar of Midwives under

section 15, as amended from time to time; « code de

déontologie »

“ criminal record check ” means a record check to

obtain information from a law enforcement agency in

Canada as to whether an individual has, under any

enactment of Canada or a province, any

(a) conviction or finding of guilt in relation to

which no pardon or record suspension is in

effect, or

(b) outstanding charge awaiting court disposition;

« vérification du casier judiciaire »

“ equivalent licence ” means a licence, registration or

other authorization to practise midwifery

(a) that is issued by the regulatory authority of

another Canadian jurisdiction that is a party to

the Canadian Free Trade Agreement,

(b) that is equivalent to a full licence in Yukon,

(c) that is not suspended or cancelled or that has

not expired, and

(d) the holder of which is not in arrears of any

amount due to the regulatory authority that

issued it; « licence équivalente »

“ international criminal record check ” means a record

check to obtain information about an individual from

an agency recognized by the Registrar of Midwives in a

jurisdiction outside Canada as to whether the individual

has any conviction or finding of guilt, or outstanding

charge awaiting court disposition, under a law of that

jurisdiction; « vérification internationale du casier

judiciaire »

“ licensing year ” means the period that begins on

April 1 of a year and ends on March 31 of the following

year; « année visée par une licence »

“ midwifery ” means the health profession in which a

person performs services listed or referred to in

sections 13 and 14 in relation to pregnancy, labour,

delivery and the post-partum period; « profession de

sage-femme »

“ registered midwife ” means an individual who is a

registrant and holds a valid licence of a class

established in section 21; « sage-femme inscrite »

“ Registrar of Midwives ” means the registrar;

« registraire des sages-femmes »

“ standards of practice ” means the standards of

practice that are specified by the Registrar of Midwives

under section 15, as amended from time to time;

« normes d’exercice »

“ student ” means an individual who is enrolled as a

student in a midwifery program at an educational

institution in Canada that has been approved by a

regulatory authority in Canada; « étudiant »

“ unprofessional conduct ” of a midwife means any of

the following acts or omissions:

(a) displaying a lack of knowledge, skill or

judgement in the provision of services within

the practice of midwifery,

(b) misrepresenting their status as a midwife or

the practice limitations, restrictions or

conditions imposed on their licence or

registration,

(c) failing or refusing to cooperate with the

Registrar of Midwives or an inspector,

(d) contravening any of the following:

(i) a provision of the Act or this Regulation,

(ii) an order made under the Act,

(iii) a practice limitation, restriction or condition

imposed on their licence or registration,

(iv) a provision of any other enactment of Yukon

or Canada that applies to the profession,

(v) the code of ethics,

(vi) the standards of practice,

(e) acting in a way that is detrimental to the best

interests of the public,

(f) making a false representation in an application

under this Regulation,

(g) consenting, whether expressly or impliedly, to

unprofessional conduct committed by a person

who is employed, managed or supervised by,

or who is an agent of, the midwife,

(h) harming or risking harm to the integrity of the

profession of midwifery,

(i) being convicted of an offence related to any

matter described in paragraphs (a) to (f).

« conduite non professionnelle »

### Designation

2 Midwifery is designated as a health profession for the

purposes of the Act.

### Prohibitions

3 (1) An individual must not practise midwifery unless

they are registered and hold a licence under this

Regulation.

(2) An individual must not practise midwifery except in

accordance with the Act and this Regulation and with

any practice limitations, restrictions or conditions

imposed on their licence or registration.

(3) An individual must not practise midwifery if their

licence or registration is suspended or cancelled or has

expired.

(4) An individual whose licence is suspended or has

expired or whose registration is suspended or cancelled

must not display their licence or represent themself as

being permitted to practise midwifery in Yukon.

### Prohibited marketing

4 (1) In this section

“ marketing ” is a type of advertising and includes

(a) an advertisement,

(b) a promotional activity or material, a listing in a

directory, a public appearance, a publication or

a communication in any medium to a client,

prospective client or the public in the nature of

an advertisement, or any other means by which

a registrant's midwifery services are promoted,

and

(c) contact with a prospective client that is

initiated by or under the direction of a

registrant. « marketing »

(2) A registrant must not undertake or authorize any

person to undertake any marketing in respect of

midwifery services that

(a) is false, inaccurate or reasonably capable of

misleading the recipient or intended recipient;

(b) is unverifiable; or

(c) harms or risks harm to the integrity of the

profession of midwifery.

### Prescribed information in register

5 The following types of information are prescribed for

the purpose of subsection 9(2) of the Act to be recorded

by the Registrar of Midwives in the register in respect

of every registrant and former registrant:

(a) a notation of their class of licence;

(b) work contact information;

(c) a notation of the date on which their licence

expired or is to expire;

(d) a notation of any practice limitations,

restrictions or conditions imposed under the

Act or this Regulation on their licence or

registration;

(e) a notation of any advanced midwifery services

listed or referred to in section 14 that they are

certified as qualified to perform and, if

applicable, the date of expiry of the

certification;

(f) information regarding every complaint,

investigation or disciplinary proceeding of

which the registrant or former registrant is the

subject;

(g) a record of every undertaking they have made

with a regulatory authority in any jurisdiction

to carry out voluntary remedial measures;

(h) a notation of a change in the class of their

licence, and the effective date of the change;

(i) a notation of any expiry of their licence or

suspension or cancellation of their licence or

registration and the reason for the suspension

or cancellation;

(j) a notation of any reinstatement of a

registration under section 18 or reinstatement

of a licence under section 44.

### Notification of Registrar required

6 A registrant must notify the Registrar of Midwives in

writing without delay after any of the following occurs:

(a) there is a change in their name or contact

information, including their address and any

other contact information that is required by

the Registrar;

(b) they are refused a licence or registration by a

regulatory authority that regulates a profession

in any jurisdiction;

(c) a practice limitation, restriction or condition is

imposed on their licence or registration by a

regulatory authority that regulates a profession

in any jurisdiction;

(d) they become the subject of a complaint,

investigation, reprimand, hearing, finding or

appeal respecting their professional conduct or

their competence to practise any profession in

any jurisdiction;

(e) they become a party to a process for

mediation, conciliation, negotiation or any

other means of facilitating the resolution,

without a hearing, of a complaint respecting

their professional conduct or their competence

to practise any profession in any jurisdiction;

(f) they become the subject of a professional

liability insurance claim in any jurisdiction;

(g) they cease to hold professional liability

insurance of the required type and level;

(h) they are charged in any jurisdiction with an

offence that relates to a matter described in the

definition "unprofessional conduct" in section 1

or that relates to the practice of any other

profession or occupation, or with an offence

under any other enactment of Canada or a

province;

(i) they are found guilty in any jurisdiction of an

offence that relates to a matter described in

the definition "unprofessional conduct" in

section 1 or that relates to the practice of

any other profession or occupation, or of an

offence under any other enactment of

Canada or a province;

(j) their licence or registration in another

jurisdiction is suspended or cancelled by the

regulatory authority of the jurisdiction.

### Duty to report — registrant

7 (1) A registrant who believes on reasonable grounds

that either of the following circumstances exists in

relation to another registrant must, as a condition of

their licence, report it without delay to the Registrar of

Midwives in writing, and include in the report the

reasons for their belief:

(a) the actions of the other registrant demonstrate

u nprofessional conduct or indicate incapacity;

(b) the continued practice of the other registrant is

likely to pose a danger to clients in the other

registrant's care.

(2) The Registrar must treat a report received under

this section in the same manner as if the report was a

complaint.

### Duty to report — employer

8 (1) An employer of a registered midwife must report

to the Registrar of Midwives, in writing, any of the

following actions without delay after it occurs:

(a) the termination or suspension of the midwife's

employment;

(b) the imposition of any practice limitations,

restrictions or conditions on the duties of their

employment.

(2) Subjection (1) applies only if the employer acted on

a reasonable belief that either of the following

circumstances exists:

(a) the actions of the registered midwife

demonstrate unprofessional conduct or indicate

incapacity;

(b) the continued practice of the registered

midwife is likely to pose a danger to clients in

their care.

(3) An individual must report the following

circumstance to the Registrar in writing, without delay

after a registered midwife who was employed by them

resigns from their employment:

(a) that the individual was the employer of a

registered midwife;

(b) that the individual had a reasonable belief that

either of the circumstances set out in

paragraphs (2)(a) and (b) existed;

(c) that the registered midwife resigned from

employment before the employer could

reasonably have acted on the belief, as

required by subsections (1) and (2).

### Professional liability insurance

9 (1) A registrant who holds a full licence or an

emergency licence must maintain, as a condition of the

licence, professional liability insurance for claims

against them in respect of their conduct in the practice

of midwifery.

(2) The professional liability insurance must also be

maintained by the registrant in respect of every

employee, second attendant and student under their

supervision and any other person in connection with

their practice for whom they are liable.

(3) Professional liability insurance described in

subsections (1) and (2) must have limits of at least

(a) $10 million per claim; and

(b) $20 million annually in the aggregate.

(4) A registrant who is not in compliance with

subsections (1) to (3) must immediately cease

practising midwifery and inform the Registrar of

Midwives of that fact without delay.

### Training and certification costs to be paid by applicants

10 For greater certainty, any costs that are incurred in

connection with programs of study, courses,

examinations or certifications required of an applicant

for a licence or advanced certification under this

Regulation are to be paid by the applicant.

### Fees payable

11 (1) The following fees are payable to the Registrar of

Midwives by applicants in respect of licences and

registrations under this Regulation:

(a) application fee, full licence and inactive licence,

$50;

(b) issuance of full licence, $200;

(c) issuance of inactive licence, $50;

(d) renewal of full licence, $200;

(e) renewal of inactive licence, $50;

(f) reinstatement of suspended full licence, or

reinstatement of both the registration and full

licence, $200;

(g) reinstatement of suspended inactive licence, or

reinstatement of both the registration and

inactive licence, $50;

(h) issuance of certificate of standing and

professional conduct, $50.

(2) An applicant for the issuance or renewal of a

licence or for the reinstatement of a licence or

registration must include with their application any

outstanding fees that they owe to the Registrar under

this Regulation in respect of licences or registrations.

(3) The fees are not refundable.

## PART 2

## SCOPE OF MIDWIFERY PRACTICE

### Scope of practice

12 The practice of midwifery consists of the core

midwifery services listed in section 13 and the

advanced midwifery services listed or referred to in

section 14.

### Core midwifery services

13 (1) A registered midwife who holds a full licence or,

subject to subsection (4), an emergency licence is

entitled to perform the following services in the course

of their practice of midwifery:

(a) counsel, support, advise, examine, monitor and

care for clients during pregnancy, labour,

delivery and the post-partum period;

(b) carry out assessments and physical

examinations necessary to confirm and manage

pregnancies;

(c) advise on and secure the further assessments

necessary for the earliest possible identification

of medically complex pregnancies;

(d) identify any conditions in a client, foetus or

infant that necessitate consultation with or

referral to a medical practitioner or other

health care professional, in accordance with the

standards of practice;

(e) monitor the condition of the foetus;

(f) manage labour and conduct vaginal deliveries;

(g) examine and care for the infant in the post-

partum period;

(h) perform, order, collect samples for and

interpret the results of screening and

diagnostic tests, in accordance with the

standards of practice;

(i) perform episiotomies and amniotomies, and

repair episiotomies and simple lacerations;

(j) take emergency measures to save the life of

the client or infant, when necessary;

(k) provide counselling, advice and education in

respect of health promotion, childbearing and

care for infants;

(l) teach, train and coach other individuals in the

practice of midwifery;

(m) in respect of a client or infant, during

pregnancy, labour, delivery and the post-

partum period

(i) prescribe and administer a drug that is set

out in Schedule I or IA to the BC Drug

Schedules Regulation if

(A) prescribing the drug is not listed as an

advanced midwifery service in section 14

of this regulation, and

(B) the drug belongs to a category of drugs

that is set out in column 1 of a schedule to

the BC Midwives Regulation and is being

prescribed or administered only for the

purpose set out in column 2 of that

schedule,

(ii) prescribe and administer a drug that is set

out in Schedule II to the BC Drug Schedules

Regulation, and

(iii) on the order of a medical practitioner that

relates to the midwifery care of a particular

client or infant, administer any drug or

substance by the route and in the dosage

that the medical practitioner specifies;

(n) in the case of a registrant who holds a full

licence, supervise students in the performance

of services listed in this subsection.

(2) A student is entitled to perform the services listed

in paragraphs (1)(a) to (m) only under the supervision

and on the direction of the registered midwife who is

supervising them.

(3) A registrant who holds a full licence and agrees to

supervise a student must provide reasonable

supervision of the student in the performance of

services listed in paragraphs (1)(a) to (m) to ensure

that the student practices midwifery

(a) safely and effectively;

(b) in accordance with every enactment of Canada

or a province that applies to the practice of

midwifery in Yukon; and

(c) in accordance with the standards of practice

and the code of ethics.

(4) A registered midwife who holds an emergency

licence is not entitled to perform any service listed in

subsection (1) that they are not entitled to perform in

the jurisdiction where their equivalent licence was

issued.

(5) A registered midwife who holds an inactive licence

is not entitled to perform any of the services listed in

subsection (1).

### Advanced midwifery services

14 (1) A registered midwife who holds a full licence or,

subject to subsection (3), an emergency licence is

entitled to perform the following services in the course

of their practice of midwifery only if they have provided

the Registrar of Midwives with proof of their successful

completion of an education or training program

approved by the Registrar that certifies that they are

qualified to perform that service:

(a) prescribe contraceptives;

(b) use acupuncture during labour or the post-

partum period;

(c) conduct a vacuum-assisted delivery in hospital;

(d) serve as a first surgical assistant to a medical

practitioner for a caesarean section;

(e) fit barrier methods and insert intrauterine

devices for contraception;

(f) manage sexually transmitted infections in

accordance with the standards of practice;

(g) induce or augment labour in hospital when

there is early rupture of membranes at term,

non-progressive labour or post-dates

pregnancy when foetal assessment is

reassuring and there are no indications for

consultation with a medical practitioner or

transfer of care;

(h) monitor epidural anesthesia in hospital;

(i) evacuate the uterus;

(j) provide well-baby care for up to one year post-

partum;

(k) provide sexual and reproductive health care to

individuals who may become pregnant.

(2) A registered midwife who holds a full licence or,

subject to subsection (3), an emergency licence is

entitled to perform any other midwifery service in the

course of their practice of midwifery if

(a) the Registrar approves an education or training

program that leads to certification to perform

the service; and

(b) the registered midwife provides the Registrar

with proof that they have successfully

completed that education or training program

and are certified to perform the service.

(3) A registered midwife who holds an emergency

licence is not entitled to perform any service listed in

subsection (1) or referred to in subsection (2) that they

are not entitled to perform in the jurisdiction where

their equivalent licence was issued.

(4) A registered midwife who holds an inactive licence

is not entitled to perform any of the services listed in

subsection (1) or referred to in subsection (2).

### Standards of practice and code of ethics

15 (1) The Registrar of Midwives must specify the

standards of practice and the code of ethics.

(2) The Registrar must consult with the Advisory

Committee before making any amendments to the

standards of practice and the code of ethics.

(3) A registered midwife must practise midwifery in

accordance with the standards of practice and the code

of ethics specified or as amended by the Registrar.

## PART 3

## REGISTRATION AND LICENSING

## DIVISION 1

## REGISTRATION

### Registration on issuance of licence

16 The Registrar of Midwives must, on the issuance of a

licence of any class to an eligible individual under

Divisions 2 and 3, register the individual on the

register.

### Validity period of registration

17 A registration does not expire unless it is cancelled

under section 19 or 20.

### Reinstatement of registration suspended by discipline committee

18 (1) If a discipline committee suspends a registration

under paragraph 27(1)(d) of the Act, the registrant

may apply to the Registrar of Midwives for the

reinstatement of their registration by providing the

Registrar with the following information and

documents, together with the application fee set out in

subsection 11(1) and any applicable fees described in

subsection 11(2):

(a) all of the information and documents that are

or may be required by section 38 for the

renewal of a full licence;

(b) proof that the registrant has complied with any

requirements that the discipline committee has

imposed on the registrant's practice of

midwifery under paragraph 27(1)(c) of the Act.

(2) The Registrar must notify the registrant of the

reinstatement decision in writing, and in the case of a

refusal include the reasons for the decision.

(3) Despite paragraph (1)(a), the Registrar may specify

that the registrant meet continuing competency and

quality assurance requirements other than those

referred to in paragraph 38(1)(f) as a condition of the

reinstatement of their registration.

(4) On the reinstatement of their registration, the

registrant must pay the applicable reinstatement fee

set out in section 11.

(5) The Registrar must make a notation in the register

of every reinstatement decision.

### Cancellation of registration — grounds

19 (1) A registration is cancelled on the day on which

any period of two years ends during which the

registrant did not hold a valid licence of any class.

(2) The Registrar of Midwives must make a notation in

the register of the cancellation of the registration under

subsection (1).

### Cancellation of registration on request — registrant

20 (1) On the request of a registrant, in a form approved

by the Registrar of Midwives, the Registrar must cancel

the registrant's registration.

(2) The Registrar must make a notation in the register

of the cancellation of the registration under

subsection (1).

## DIVISION 2

### LICENSING — GENERAL

### Classes of licence

21 Three classes of licence are established:

(a) full licence;

(b) inactive licence;

(c) emergency licence.

### Condition of every licence

22 A registrant who holds a licence of any class must, as a

condition of the licence, comply with the Act, this

Regulation, the standards of practice and the code of

ethics.

### Disclosure of specified information

23 (1) An applicant for any class of licence must disclose

in their application all of the following information that

relates to themself and to their practice of midwifery:

(a) any finding by a regulatory authority of the

applicant's professional misconduct,

incompetence or incapacity;

(b) any ongoing investigation that is being carried

out by a regulatory authority;

(c) any reprimand or the imposition of any

conditions or educational requirements by a

regulatory authority as a result of a complaint;

(d) any undertaking they have made with a

regulatory authority to carry out voluntary

remedial measures;

(e) any dismissal of the applicant for cause by an

employer, or any resignation of the applicant

when facing a complaint or an investigation;

(f) any refusal by a regulatory authority of a

licence, registration or other authorization to

practise midwifery;

(g) any cancellation of a licence, registration or

other authorization to practise midwifery on

the request of the applicant;

(h) any ongoing coroner's investigation, inquiry or

inquest into an incident that occurred under

the applicant's care;

(i) any verdict and recommendations of a

coroner's investigation, inquiry or inquest into

an incident that occurred under the applicant's

care;

(j) any professional liability insurance claim;

(k) any pending criminal charges in any

jurisdiction in relation to an offence that relates

to a matter described in the definition

"unprofessional conduct" in section 1 or that

relates to the practice of any other profession

or occupation, or in relation to an offence

under any other enactment of Canada or a

province;

(l) any conviction in any jurisdiction of an offence

that relates to a matter described in the

definition "unprofessional conduct" in section 1

o r that relates to the practice of any other

profession or occupation, or of an offence

under any other enactment of Canada or a

province;

(m) any physical or mental ailment, any emotional

disturbance, any addiction to alcohol or drugs,

or any other condition from which the applicant

suffers that could impair their ability to

practise midwifery.

(2) The Registrar of Midwives may disclose to a

regulatory authority in Canada, on request, any of the

information disclosed under subsection (1).

### Validity period of licence

24 (1) A full licence and an inactive licence are valid for

one licensing year.

(2) An emergency licence is valid for two weeks

beginning on the day of issuance.

### Eligibility for licence

25 An individual who provides the Registrar of Midwives

with all of the information and documents referred to

in section 29 or 30, as applicable, in a satisfactory form

and manner is eligible for a licence of the class of

licence for which they applied.

### Titles licence holders may use

26 (1) A registered midwife who holds a full licence or an

emergency licence is entitled to use the title “Midwife”

or “Registered Midwife” or an abbreviation of those

expressions.

(2) A registered midwife who holds an inactive licence

is entitled to use the title “Inactive Midwife” or

“Inactive Registered Midwife” or an abbreviation of

those expressions.

### Inspection

27 A registered midwife must make their licence available

for inspection by any person on request.

## DIVISION 3

## ISSUANCE OF LICENCES

### Application required

28 An individual who wishes to practise midwifery must

apply to the Registrar of Midwives for a licence in

accordance with this Division.

### Contents of application — full licence and inactive licence

29 (1) An applicant for a full licence or an inactive licence

must provide the Registrar of Midwives with an

application that includes all of the following information

and documents, together with the application fee set

out in subsection 11(1) and any applicable fees

described in subsection 11(2):

(a) a copy of a valid government-issued photo

identification card;

(b) contact information, including work contact

information, if any, that includes a postal

address, an email address and a telephone

number;

(c) satisfactory results of a criminal record check

carried out within three months before the date

of the application;

(d) in the case of an applicant who has resided or

practised midwifery in a jurisdiction outside

Canada at any time during the 12-month

period that ends on the date of the application,

satisfactory results of an international criminal

record check from that jurisdiction that was

carried out within three months before the date

of the application;

(e) a satisfactory certificate of standing and

professional conduct from each jurisdiction

other than Yukon where the applicant has ever

been licensed, registered or otherwise

authorized to practise midwifery;

(f) a declaration that the applicant has made all of

the disclosures required by subsection 23(1);

(g) a statement by the applicant

(i) that authorizes the Registrar or their

delegate to make inquiries of any person,

government or body in respect of the

information and documents provided by the

applicant in their application, and

(ii) that authorizes any such person,

government or body to provide the Registrar

with any such information and documents

that the Registrar requests;

(h) a declaration that the applicant has reviewed

and understands the Act, this Regulation, the

standards of practice and the code of ethics

and that they understand that their compliance

with all of them is a condition of registration

and licensing.

(2) In addition to the information and documents

required by subsection (1), an application for a full

licence must also include all of the following

information and documents:

(a) a declaration that the applicant meets the

professional liability insurance requirements set

out in section 9;

(b) proof that the applicant holds an equivalent

licence and has held it for at least one year;

(c) proof that the applicant holds any current

certifications for continuing competency

specified by the Registrar in the standards of

practice;

(d) proof that the applicant has completed

coursework approved by the Registrar on the

prescription of drugs set out in Schedule 1A to

the BC Drug Schedules Regulation.

(3) In addition to the information and documents

required by subsection (1), an application for an

inactive licence must also include one of the following:

(a) proof that the applicant holds an equivalent

licence;

(b) proof that the applicant holds an inactive

equivalent licence;

(c) a statement that the applicant holds a full

licence in Yukon.

(4) An applicant for a licence must provide the

Registrar, on request, with any additional relevant

information and documents that the Registrar requires

to determine the applicant's eligibility for the licence.

(5) In determining an applicant's eligibility for the

licence, the Registrar must consider the following

information and documents as part of the application:

(a) information and documents provided to the

Registrar by any person, government or body

referred to in paragraph (1)(g);

(b) information and documents provided to the

Registrar by the applicant under subsection (4).

(6) The Registrar may waive the requirement set out

in paragraph (1)(d) if it is not reasonably practicable

for the applicant to comply with that paragraph, either

because the foreign jurisdiction for any reason does not

have or keep such records, or for any other reason

outside the applicant's control.

### Contents of application — emergency licence

30 An applicant for an emergency licence must provide the

Registrar of Midwives with an application that includes

all of the following information and documents,

together with the application fee set out in

subsection 11(1) and any applicable fees described in

subsection 11(2):

(a) a copy of a valid government-issued photo

identification card;

(b) contact information, including work contact

information, if any, that includes a postal

address, an email address and a telephone

number;

(c) proof that the applicant holds an equivalent

licence and has held it for at least one year;

(d) a declaration that the applicant meets the

professional liability insurance requirements set

out in section 9;

(e) a declaration that the applicant will practise in

Yukon strictly within the scope of their

equivalent licence.

### Issuance of licence

31 If the Registrar of Midwives is satisfied that the

applicant for a licence is eligible for the licence, based

on their application, the Registrar must issue the

applicant a licence of the class of licence for which they

applied.

### Payment of fees on issuance

32 On issuance of the licence, the applicant for the licence

must pay the Registrar of Midwives the applicable fees

set out in section 11.

### Refusal to issue licence

33 (1) If the Registrar of Midwives determines that,

based on their application, an applicant is not eligible

for a licence of the class of licence for which they

applied, the Registrar must refuse to issue the licence.

(2) The Registrar may also refuse to issue a licence to

an applicant if the Registrar considers that

(a) it is necessary to do so to protect the public as

a result of a complaint or disciplinary or

criminal proceedings in any jurisdiction with

respect to the applicant's conduct, character or

competence to practise midwifery;

(b) it is not reasonably practicable for the Registrar

to impose any practice limitation, restriction or

condition on the licence under section 35; or

(c) in the case of an applicant for an emergency

licence

(i) there is no emergency, or

(ii) there is an emergency but there are already

enough registered midwives who hold a full

licence or an emergency licence and who are

available to respond to the emergency.

(3) If the Registrar refuses to issue a licence, the

Registrar must provide the applicant with written

reasons for the refusal.

### Information to be recorded on licence

34 The Registrar of Midwives must include the following

information on a licence:

(a) the name of the registrant;

(b) the class of licence;

(c) a notation of any advanced midwifery services

listed or referred to in section 14 that the

registrant is entitled to perform and, if

applicable, the date of expiry of their

certification to perform those services;

(d) a notation of any practice limitations,

restrictions or conditions that have been

imposed by the Registrar or by a discipline

committee on their registration or licence;

(e) the effective date and expiry date of the

licence.

### Practice limitations, restrictions and conditions on licence

35 (1) The Registrar of Midwives may impose practice

limitations, restrictions and conditions on the full

licence of a registrant if the Registrar considers it

necessary to do so to protect the public as a result of a

complaint or disciplinary or criminal proceedings in any

jurisdiction with respect to the registrant's conduct,

character or competence to practise midwifery.

(2) If the registrant holds an equivalent licence that

contains any practice limitations, restrictions or

conditions, the Registrar may impose those same or

similar limitations, restrictions and conditions on their

full licence if the Registrar considers it necessary to do

so to protect the public.

(3) The Registrar may impose practice limitations,

restrictions and conditions on an emergency licence.

### Change from full licence to inactive licence

36 (1) A registrant who holds a full licence may apply to

have their licence changed to an inactive licence by

filing an application with the Registrar of Midwives that

includes all of the following information and

documents, together with the application fee set out in

subsection 11(1) and any applicable fees described in

subsection 11(2):

(a) updated disclosures under subsection 23(1),

except for the disclosure required by

paragraph 23(1)(m), that provide any new

information since the last issuance or renewal

of the full licence;

(b) a statement by the registrant

(i) that authorizes the Registrar or their

delegate to make inquiries of any person,

government or body in respect of the

information and documents provided by the

registrant in their application, and

(ii) that authorizes any such person,

government or body to provide the Registrar

with any such information and documents

that the Registrar requests;

(c) a declaration that the registrant has reviewed

and understands the Act, this Regulation, the

standards of practice and the code of ethics

and that they understand that their compliance

with all of them is a condition of registration

and licensing.

(2) The registrant must provide the Registrar, on

request, with any additional relevant information and

documents that the Registrar requires to determine

their eligibility for the inactive licence.

(3) In determining the registrant's eligibility for the

inactive licence, the Registrar must consider the

following information and documents as part of the

application:

(a) information and documents provided to the

Registrar by any person, government or body

referred to in paragraph (1)(b);

(b) information and documents provided to the

Registrar by the registrant under

subsection (2).

(4) If the Registrar is satisfied that the registrant is

eligible for the inactive licence, based on their

application, the Registrar must issue the licence and

add a notation in the register of the new class of

licence that the registrant holds.

(5) On issuance of the inactive licence, the registrant

must pay the Registrar the applicable fees set out in

section 11.

### Change from inactive licence to full licence

37 (1) A registrant who has previously held a full licence

and who now holds an inactive licence may apply for a

full licence by filing an application with the Registrar of

Midwives that includes all of the information and

documents that are or may be required by section 38

for the renewal of a full licence, together with the

application fee set out in subsection 11(1) and any

applicable fees described in subsection 11(2).

(2) The registrant must provide the Registrar, on

request, with any additional relevant information and

documents that the Registrar requires to determine

their eligibility for the full licence.

(3) In determining the registrant's eligibility for the

full licence, the Registrar must consider the following

information and documents as part of the application:

(a) information and documents provided to the

Registrar by any person, government or body

referred to in paragraph 38(1)(i);

(b) information and documents provided to the

Registrar by the registrant under

subsection (2).

(4) If the Registrar is satisfied that the registrant is

eligible for the full licence, based on their application,

the Registrar must issue the licence and add a notation

in the register of the new class of licence that the

registrant holds.

(5) On issuance of the full licence, the Registrar may

(a) specify that the registrant meet continuing

competency and quality assurance

requirements other than those referred to in

paragraph 38(1)(f) as a condition of their

licence; and

(b) if the Registrar considers it necessary to do so

to protect the public, impose practice

limitations, restrictions and conditions on the

licence.

(6) On issuance of the full licence, the registrant must

pay the Registrar the applicable fees set out in

section 11.

(7) If the Registrar determines that, based on their

application, the registrant is not eligible for the licence

applied for, the Registrar must refuse to issue the

licence and must notify the registrant in writing of the

refusal and of the reasons for the refusal.

## DIVISION 4

## LICENCE RENEWALS

### Contents of application for licence renewal — full licence and inactive licence

38 (1) An application for the renewal of a full licence or

an inactive licence must include all of the following

information and documents, together with the

application fee set out in subsection 11(1) and any

applicable fees described in subsection 11(2):

(a) a satisfactory certificate of standing and

professional conduct from each jurisdiction

other than Yukon where the applicant has held

any licence, registration or other authorization

to practise midwifery in the year before the

date of the application;

(b) proof that the applicant has

(i) complied with any undertaking given in

response to a request made under

paragraph 24(1)(a) or (b) of the Act,

(ii) performed any action specified by the

Registrar of Midwives and undertaken or

consented to by the applicant in response to

a request made under paragraph 24(1)(d) of

the Act,

(iii) complied with any order of a discipline

committee made under subsection 27(1) of

the Act, and

(iv) complied with any order of the Supreme

C ourt made under subsection 28(9) of th e

Act;

(c) satisfactory results of a criminal record check

that was carried out within three months

before the date of the renewal application, if

two or more years have elapsed since the last

one was carried out;

(d) satisfactory results of an international criminal

record check that was carried out within three

months before the date of the renewal

application if

(i) the applicant has resided or practised

midwifery in a jurisdiction outside Canada

during the current licensing year, and

(ii) two or more years have elapsed since the

last such record check from that jurisdiction

was carried out;

(e) in the case of a full licence, a declaration that

the applicant meets the professional liability

insurance requirements set out in section 9;

(f) in the case of a full licence, proof that the

applicant meets any certification, continuing

competency and quality assurance

requirements specified by the Registrar in the

standards of practice;

(g) in the case of a full licence, updated disclosures

under subsection 23(1) that provide any new

information since the last issuance or renewal

of the licence;

(h) in the case of an inactive licence, updated

disclosures under subsection 23(1), except for

the disclosure required by paragraph 23(1)m),

that provide any new information since the last

issuance or renewal of the inactive licence;

(i) a statement by the applicant

(i) that authorizes the Registrar or their

delegate to make inquiries of any person,

government or body in respect of the

information and documents provided by the

applicant in their application, and

(ii) that authorizes any such person,

government or body to provide the Registrar

with any such information and documents

that the Registrar requests;

(j) a declaration that the applicant has reviewed

and understands the Act, this Regulation, the

standards of practice and the code of ethics

and that they understand that their compliance

with all of them is a condition of registration

and licensing.

(2) The applicant must provide the Registrar, on

request, with any additional relevant information and

documents that the Registrar requires to determine the

applicant's eligibility for the renewal of their licence.

(3) In determining an applicant's eligibility for the

renewal of their licence, the Registrar must consider

the following information and documents as part of the

application:

(a) information and documents provided to the

Registrar by any person, government or body

referred to in paragraph (1)(i);

(b) information and documents provided to the

Registrar by the applicant under subsection (2).

(4) The Registrar may waive the requirement set out

in paragraph (1)(d) if it is not reasonably practicable

for the applicant to comply with that paragraph, either

because the foreign jurisdiction for any reason does not

have or keep such records, or for any other reason

outside the applicant's control.

### Licence renewal — time for filing application

39 An application for the renewal of a full licence or an

inactive licence must be filed with the Registrar of

Midwives by the March 1 before it expires.

### Issuance of licence renewal

40 (1) If the Registrar of Midwives is satisfied that a

registrant is eligible for the renewal of their licence,

based on their application, the Registrar must renew

the licence.

(2) On issuance of the licence, the registrant must pay

the Registrar the applicable fees set out in section 11.

### Refusal to renew licence

41 (1) If the Registrar of Midwives determines that,

based on their application, a registrant is not eligible

for the renewal of their licence, the Registrar must

refuse to renew the licence.

(2) If the Registrar refuses to renew a licence, the

Registrar must provide the applicant with written

reasons for the refusal.

### Emergency licence not renewable

42 An emergency licence may not be renewed.

## DIVISION 5

## LICENCE SUSPENSIONS, REINSTATEMENTS AND

## CANCELLATIONS

### Suspension of licence

43 (1) The licence of a registrant who holds a full licence

or an emergency licence is suspended when the

registrant ceases to hold the professional liability

insurance as required by section 9.

(2) The Registrar of Midwives must notify the

registrant in writing of the suspension of their licence

under subsection (1), and make a notation in the

register of the suspension and its effective date.

### Reinstatement of suspended licence

44 A registrant whose licence is suspended under

section 43 may apply to have their licence reinstated by

providing the Registrar of Midwives with proof that

they have the professional liability insurance required

by section 9 and by paying the application fee set out

in subsection 11(1) and any other applicable fees set

out in section 11.

### Cancellation of licence

45 (1) A licence is cancelled when the licence holder's

registration is cancelled for any reason under the Act or

this Regulation.

(2) When a licence is cancelled, the Registrar of

Midwives must notify the registrant in writing of the

cancellation and include the reasons for the cancellation

and its effective date.

## PART 4

## COMMMITTEES

## DIVISION 1

### GENERAL — COMMITTEES

### Conflict of interest

46 An individual may not be a member of both the review

committee and the discipline committee in respect of

the same matter.

## DIVISION 2

## MIDWIFERY ADVISORY COMMITTEE

### Appointment and composition of Advisory Committee

47 (1) The Minister must appoint a Midwifery Advisory

Committee, to be composed of at least four members,

one of whom must be appointed by the Minister to be

the chair of the Committee.

(2) In appointing the members of the Advisory

Committee, the Minister must ensure that

(a) at least two members are eligible for a full

licence in Yukon;

(b) one member is a medical practitioner; and

(c) one member is a person who is neither

described in paragraph (a) or (b) nor otherwise

a licensed or registered health professional in

Yukon.

(3) A member is appointed to the Advisory Committee

for a term of up to three years, and may be

reappointed for one or more further terms.

(4) Three members of the Advisory Committee

constitute a quorum.

(5) The Registrar of Midwives is not a member of the

Advisory Committee, but may attend and participate in

meetings of the Committee.

### Functions of Advisory Committee

48 (1) The function of the Advisory Committee is to

provide the Registrar of Midwives with advice on

(a) the standards of practice;

(b) the code of ethics;

(c) the requirements for licences and classes of

licences;

(d) the composition of the discipline committee;

(e) information to be provided to the public about

the functions of registrants and the areas of

knowledge, skill and judgement of registrants,

and about the standards of practice and the

code of ethics; and

(f) any other matters that relate to governing the

practice of midwifery, other than matters in

relation to complaints, discipline and

unprofessional conduct.

(2) The Advisory Committee performs the following

functions on the request of the Registrar:

(a) review and provide the Registrar with advice

on applications for licences and licence

renewals;

(b) convene meetings of the Advisory Committee;

(c) participate in national initiatives related to the

practice of midwifery and provide the Registrar

with feedback on those initiatives.

## DIVISION 3

## DISCIPLINE COMMITTEE

### Appointment and composition of discipline committee

49 (1) If the Minister appoints a discipline committee of

only one person, that person must be eligible to hold a

full licence in Yukon.

(2) If the Minister appoints a discipline committee of

more than one person, the Minister must ensure that

(a) at least one member is a registered midwife

who holds a full licence in Yukon or holds an

equivalent licence; and

(b) at least one member is a person who is neither

described in paragraph (a) nor a licensed or

registered health or medical professional in

Yukon.

### Maximum fine

50 For the purpose of paragraph 27(1)(f) of the Act, the

maximum fine is $25,000.

### Costs to be assessed

51 For the purpose of paragraph 27(1)(g) of the Act, the

maximum amount of costs is prescribed as a total of

$500,000 in respect of the following reasonably

incurred costs of the hearing:

(a) travel and accommodation expenses paid to

any person who attends at or participates in an

investigation, hearing or appeal in relation to

the matter being heard;

(b) transcripts of proceedings;

(c) rental of rooms, rental of recording equipment

and hiring of a reporter to transcribe the

proceedings;

(d) fees or disbursements that are payable to an

inspector or lawyer acting on behalf of the

Registrar of Midwives or on behalf of the

discipline committee;

(e) service of documents;

(f) any other costs or expenses that are

attributable to the investigation, hearing and

appeal of the matter.

### Records of proceedings

52 A discipline committee must keep a record of its

proceedings and of all the evidence that it receives in

relation to a hearing.

### Transcripts

53 A person who requests a transcript of the oral evidence

that is received by a discipline committee must pay for

the preparation of the transcript.

## DIVISION 4

## REVIEW COMMITTEE

### Appointment and composition of review committee

54 (1) A review committee appointed under

subsection 22(3) of the Act must be composed of at

least three members, one of whom must be appointed

by the Minister to be the chair of the committee.

(2) In appointing the members of a review committee,

the Minister must ensure that

(a) at least one member is a registered midwife

who holds a full licence in Yukon or holds an

equivalent licence; and

(b) at least one member is a person who is neither

described in paragraph (a) nor a licensed or

registered health or medical professional in

Yukon.

## PART 6

## COMING INTO FORCE

### Coming into force

55 This Regulation comes into force on April 15, 2021.
