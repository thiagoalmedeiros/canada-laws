## INFORMATION TO THE DRIVER

### What has just happened?

You have now received a 90 day suspension or disqualification AND/OR have

received a 24 hour suspension of your driving privileges. You may also have

received a temporary driving permit OR, if you had a temporary driving permit, it

may have been revoked.

### What is the 24 hour suspension?

A 24 hour suspension removes all driving privileges for a period of 24 hours

from the date and time it is issued. It can be issued either with a temporary

driving permit or without one.

If the officer believes you are impaired, the officer will issue you a 24 hour

suspension which means that you are not allowed to drive any vehicle for

24 hours from the time stated on this form. This suspension will reduce the 14

day temporary driving permit by one day. When the 24 hour suspension is over,

your temporary driving permit (if you were issued one) allows you to drive for

the remainder of the permit period or until your Yukon driver’s licence would

expire, to a maximum of another 13 days. After the 24 hours ends, you may ask

the RCMP for the return of your licence, unless you received a temporary driving

permit.

### What is a temporary driving permit?

A temporary driving permit is a temporary extension of driving privileges. The

permit is valid for up to 14 days from the day and time it was issued, or for less

than 14 days if your driver’s licence was going to expire sooner.

After the temporary driving permit expires, you are not permitted to drive any

motor vehicle for the following 90 days.

The temporary driving permit replaces your Yukon driver’s licence that has been

taken from you or surrendered by you. It must be carried by you whenever you

operate a motor vehicle. The temporary driving permit only allows you to drive

the same vehicles under the same restrictions as allowed on your regular

driver’s licence.

The temporary driving permit will be revoked if you are caught driving while

impaired or if you refuse to provide a breath or blood sample when requested to

do so by the RCMP.

If you are licensed to drive in another jurisdiction you may keep your driver’s

licence and you will not be issued a temporary driving permit. You may continue

to drive in the Yukon for up to 14 days from the date the disqualification was

issued or for less than 14 days if your driver’s licence was going to expire

sooner. You cannot apply for or hold a Yukon driver’s licence during this period.

If you qualify for a temporary driving permit you may use the time to make

alternate transportation arrangements before the 90 day non-driving period

starts.

### What is a 90 day suspension?

A 90 day suspension is an administrative sanction designed to temporarily

remove the driving privileges of Yukon drivers who are impaired or refuse to

provide a breath or blood sample when requested to do so by the RCMP.

Example: On October 14 at 4:00 p.m. Mary was caught driving impaired. She

has a Yukon licence. She is issued a 90 day suspension, a temporary driving

permit and a 24 hour suspension. This means she may not drive any vehicle

until at least 4:00 p.m. on October 15 and now has only 13 days left to drive

under this temporary driving permit. (If Mary’s usual driver’s licence would have

expired in less than 14 days, say in perhaps 7 days, then Mary’s 24 hour

suspension would use up one day of the temporary driving permit. The permit

would then be valid for only another 6 days.)

### What is a 90 day disqualification?

The 90 day disqualification is an administrative sanction designed to temporarily

remove the driving privileges of drivers who hold a licence issued in another

jurisdiction or do not hold a valid driver’s licence and are found to be driving

impaired, refuse to provide a breath or blood sample when requested to do so

by the RCMP, or drove while their licence is under suspension. The

disqualification also means these drivers cannot apply for, or hold, a Yukon

driver’s licence during this period of time.

Example: John had a valid Manitoba driver’s licence when he was stopped for

impaired driving on June 10 at 10:30 p.m. He is issued a 90 day disqualification,

and a 24 hour suspension. He is allowed to keep his Manitoba licence, but,

under the 24 hour suspension, he is not to operate any vehicle until at least

10:30 p.m. June 11. He may drive in the Yukon for up to 14 days or for a lesser

period of time if his Manitoba licence expires prior to that time. The 90 day

disqualification starts when the 14 day or lesser period ends. He also cannot

apply for, or hold, a Yukon driver’s licence during the disqualification period.

### What can you do if you disagree with the 90 day suspension or

### disqualification?

A review process allows you to have the 90 day suspension or disqualification

reviewed if you feel it was wrongfully imposed. A review can be held any time

after the suspension or disqualification has been imposed.

You may request a review of this suspension or disqualification by completing

an Application for Review form, paying the application fee and completing the

review requirements. Application for Review forms can be obtained through

Court Services: Phone 667-5441 or Toll Free 1-800-661-0408 extension 5441.

Once the form and payment or proof of payment have been received, and your

driver’s licence has been turned in, or you have satisfactorily declared it lost or

destroyed, you will be contacted and arrangements for the review will be made.

### How can you get your licence back after the suspension/

### disqualification period?

Call Motor Vehicles at 667-8224 or toll free 1-800-661-0408 extension 5315 to

discuss getting your licence back.

## INFORMATION TO THE DRIVER

### What just happened?

The vehicle you were driving has been seized and will be impounded.

### Why was the vehicle impounded?

The reason is shown on the previous page of this document.

### How long will the vehicle remain impounded?

The Motor Vehicles office will calculate how long the vehicle must be

impounded by checking if the registered owner of this vehicle had a vehicle

impounded in the last 5 years.

Generally, the impoundment period will be for:

• 30 days if this is the first impoundment offence by the owner of this

vehicle;

• 60 days if this is the second impoundment offence by the owner of this

vehicle;

• 120 days if this is the third impoundment offence by the owner of this

vehicle. In addition to the 120 days of impoundment, the registration of

this vehicle will be void and the owner of this vehicle is disqualified from

registering the vehicle again until 240 days have elapsed after the

impoundment expires.

### Note: Where the driver has consumed alcohol in such a quantity that the

### concentration of it in their blood is double or higher than that of legal

### limit allowed in the Criminal Code of Canada, then the impoundment

### period for the vehicle is double the usual period.

### If a driver refuses to comply with a lawful demand made for a sample or

### other requirement then the impoundment period for the vehicle is double

### the usual period.

The Motor Vehicles office will advise the registered owner of the length of

the impoundment by letter.

### Where will the vehicle be taken?

The vehicle will be taken to the location designated by the peace officer on

the Notice of Impoundment form.

### How will the vehicle be taken there?

In most cases the vehicle will be taken by tow truck to this location.

However, depending on the circumstances, the peace officer may allow the

vehicle to be delivered to an impoundment facility by a driver with a valid

licence who is not impaired.

If the vehicle is not delivered to the impoundment location on time, the

RCMP can immediately seize the vehicle.

### Who can claim the vehicle and pay the towing and storage charges?

The vehicle will not be released until all fees owed for towing and storage

have been paid by the registered owner to the impoundment facility

operator.

### What if the vehicle is not mine?

If the vehicle that has been impounded is not owned by the driver who was

driving at the time the impoundment was ordered, the registered owner may,

in some circumstances, apply to have the vehicle released from

impoundment.

### What will happen to items left in the vehicle when it is impounded?

The driver of the vehicle is encouraged to remove all of his/her personal

property from the vehicle before it is impounded. Items left in the vehicle

when it is impounded may be noted on the Notice of Impoundment form.

The registered owner of the vehicle or the owner’s representative is

encouraged to make arrangements with the impoundment facility operator to

pick up these items as soon as possible. There is no charge to pick up

personal items. Items attached to and used in connection with the operation

of the vehicle may not be removed because they are impounded. (For

example, the spare tire and stereo may not be removed.)

### How will the registered owner know when to pick up the vehicle?

The registered owner will receive a letter by mail from the Motor Vehicles

office. This letter will contain information about where the vehicle is

impounded, how long it must remain there, and how to pick it up once the

impoundment period is over.

### What happens to the vehicle and its contents if it is not picked up after the

### impoundment period is over?

If the vehicle is not claimed within 30 days after the last day that it was

legally impounded, it can be sold or in some cases the ownership of the

vehicle can be transferred to the impoundment facility operator.

Any unclaimed personal property will be turned over to the Yukon

government for disposal.

### For more information about vehicle impoundment call Motor Vehicles at

(867)667-8217 or toll free 1-800-661-0408 extension 5315.

### May I ask for this vehicle to be released immediately?

Yes, you may do any of the following. You may contact the Court Services

office to ask if you are eligible to have a review officer decide if the facts of

this impoundment match what the law allows for an immediate release. You

would need an “Application for Review” form from Court Services. Please

read it carefully. It shows the only reasons in the law that a review officer can

order the immediate release of this vehicle.

If you feel an error has been made in impounding the vehicle, you may also

make a request to the RCMP to immediately release this vehicle. They can

release the vehicle if you can clearly show or provide proof that an error has

been made in impounding it. For example, information the RCMP used was

incorrect. If the RCMP do not agree to release this vehicle, then and only

then, may you apply to a judge to explain the reasons why you think the

RCMP should release this vehicle. Contact Court Services about applying to

the court.
